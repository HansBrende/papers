\subsection{Nonlinear sources and a recoverable affine hull}
\label{sec:local-sources}

Local generation can be supplied by local changes to a nonlinear encoder.
The additional requirement is recovery on the encoder's entire affine hull.
Recovery on its image alone does not suffice.

\begin{theorem}[Payment for exceptional sources]\label{thm:local-sources}
\notformalized
Retain the graph, parent sets, and radius of
\cref{thm:local-generation}, and suppose $K$ is finite, with $q=|K|$.
Let $I$ be a finite set, let each $A_i$ be finite and nonempty, and let
\[
 F:\prod_{i\in I}A_i\longrightarrow\bigoplus_v W_v,
 \qquad S=\operatorname{im}F,\qquad
 \operatorname{aff}_K S=a+\mathcal C.
\]
Assume that each specified parent set recovers its coordinate on all of
$a+\mathcal C$. Fix a set $B\subseteq I$ of exceptional sources.
For each $j\notin B$, assume there is a center $c_j$ such that changing
only source $j$ changes no output coordinate outside $B_R(c_j)$.
Put
\[
 k=\dim_K\mathcal C,\quad
 r=\sum_v\dim_K\pi_v(\mathcal C),\quad
 \mathcal L=\mathcal L_R(\mathcal C),\quad
 \ell=\dim_K\mathcal L,\quad M=\prod_{i\in B}|A_i|.
\]
Each fixed exceptional-source fibre has image in one affine coset of
$\mathcal L$. Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-source-size}
 2\log_q|S|\le r+\log_q M.
\end{equation}
For any probability law of a source tuple $Z$ on the stated Cartesian
domain, with $X=F(Z)$ and entropies in bits,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:local-source-entropy}
 2H(X)\le r\log_2q+H(Z_B).
\end{equation}
Neither independence nor full support of this law is required.
If $B=\varnothing$, then $\mathcal C=\mathcal L$ and $2k\le r$.
If $S=a+\mathcal C$, then also
\[
 q^{\dim_K(\mathcal C/\mathcal L)}\le M;
\]
if, in addition, $X$ is uniform on this affine space, then
$\dim_K(\mathcal C/\mathcal L)\log_2q\le H(Z_B)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{informalproof}
Translation by $a$ transfers recovery from $a+\mathcal C$ to
$\mathcal C$. Thus \cref{thm:local-generation} gives $k+\ell\le r$.
Two source assignments differing only in a nonexceptional coordinate
have output difference in $\mathcal C$, supported in its specified
radius-$R$ ball, and hence in $\mathcal L$. Any two assignments with
the same exceptional tuple can be joined by changing the remaining
coordinates one at a time. All intermediate assignments are in the
Cartesian domain. Summing the differences proves that their outputs
lie in one affine $\mathcal L$-coset.

There are at most $M$ such fibres, each with at most $q^\ell$ outputs,
whereas $S\subseteq a+\mathcal C$ gives $|S|\le q^k$. Adding the
logarithms of these two bounds and using $k+\ell\le r$ proves
\eqref{eq:local-source-size}. For any source law the same support
bounds give
\[
 H(X)\le k\log_2q,\qquad
 H(X\mid Z_B)\le\ell\log_2q.
\]
Combine the second with $H(X)\le H(Z_B)+H(X\mid Z_B)$, add the first,
and apply $k+\ell\le r$ to obtain \eqref{eq:local-source-entropy}.

If $B$ is empty, choose $a\in S$. The coset assertion puts
$S-a$ in $\mathcal L$. Since $S-a$ spans $\mathcal C$, this proves
$\mathcal C=\mathcal L$. Finally, the quotient of $F(Z)-a$ modulo
$\mathcal L$ is a function of $Z_B$. If $S=a+\mathcal C$, that
function maps the exceptional-source domain onto
$\mathcal C/\mathcal L$, proving the quotient cardinality bound.
A uniform $X$ induces the uniform law on this quotient, whose entropy
is $(k-\ell)\log_2q$. A function of $Z_B$ has entropy at most
$H(Z_B)$, proving the last assertion.
\end{informalproof}

The quantity $r$ measures affine coordinate dimensions. The theorem
does not replace it by $\sum_v H(X_v)/\log_2q$ or by
$\sum_v\log_q|\pi_v S|$. For uniform $X$ on the entire affine hull,
these quantities do agree with $r$. Empty source sets, singleton
alphabets, constant encoders, and zero-dimensional coordinates are
included. At radius zero, $\mathcal L=0$, so nonexceptional sources
cannot affect the output.

The all-local conclusion recovers the weighted half bound for locally
generated linear codes: choose finitely many ball-supported generators,
use their coefficients as the sources, and let $F$ be their sum.
The more general statement permits nonlinear source dependence and
charges the actual exceptional tuple. To apply it to a native fibre,
one must supply the Cartesian representation, the local influence
sets, and recovery on the whole affine hull. A small circuit or a
shallow recovery cone supplies none of these automatically.

\begin{example}[Recovery can fail on the affine hull]
\label{ex:affine-hull}
On the bipartite graph $K_{3,3}$, the binary encoder
\[
 (a,b,c)\longmapsto
 (a,b,c,\ c+ab,\ a+c+ab,\ b+c+ab)
\]
has eight outputs and exact neighbor recovery. Writing the last three
coordinates as $y_1,y_2,y_3$, the inverse formulas are
$a=y_1+y_2$, $b=y_1+y_3$, and $c=y_1+ab$.
Each source affects only the radius-one star centered at its own
left vertex. Nevertheless, the affine hull has dimension four, with
basis
\[
 (1,0,0,0,1,0),\quad (0,1,0,0,0,1),\quad
 (0,0,1,1,1,1),\quad (0,0,0,1,1,1).
\]
The first three vectors are outputs at the three unit sources; the
fourth is $F(1,1,0)+F(1,0,0)+F(0,1,0)$. They span every output and
are independent, as their first three coordinates show. Their span
contains $(0,0,1,0,0,0)$, so the third coordinate is not recoverable
there from its neighbors. All six coordinate dimensions are one;
the inequality $2\dim\mathcal C\le6$ would fail if recovery on the
affine hull were omitted. The original eight-word code still has
exactly half rate.
\end{example}
