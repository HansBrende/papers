Linear-time matrix transposition on multitape Turing machines · statement (T)

Transposition Impossibility Frontier

The rectangle is every deterministic multitape Turing machine that transposes a d × d matrix of ⌈log2 d⌉-bit entries (read-only input, k work tapes, write-only output). Circles are machine properties and overlap as in a Venn diagram. Each piece is coloured by what is known about transposing in time O(L), L = d2⌈log2 d⌉. The three red bands cut across every circle: a one-tape machine, for instance, may or may not be oblivious. Hover or tap any piece for its status and sources.

The pieces

How the colours are decided

Theorem D of the fourth paper splits impossibility for oblivious machines into two statements. Depth reduction (TCP): a genie revealing εL bits cuts the machine's time–space dependency graph to bounded depth. The crux: index codes for the side information of the remaining bounded-depth cones need many bits. Directed Recovery Structures turns the crux into one machine-free storage-code question, Question 9.1.

A piece is orange when one of the two is proved there, yellow when neither is. The red lens has both: depth reduction (Theorems F, B, M) and a proved half-rate bound for its cone relations (papers 7, 8 and 10 together). Adaptive machines enter only through Corollary F″, which freezes the most likely trace.

Paper 9 adds a third route that needs no coding question. For an oblivious machine whose head excursions overlap boundedly, complete source certificates on the actual return geometry show directly that the machine is wrong on most inputs, provided only a few excursions are large. That is the small red island inside the depth-reduction region.

Paper 11 adds three things. Given Question 9.1, hop charging excludes every oblivious transposer whose code merges deep data lineages only boundedly often, on every schedule however deeply nested (the orange island in the deep-nesting piece; for linear codes the linear crux suffices). For adaptive machines, bounded entry on every tape, the input tape included, is excluded at any adaptivity (Corollary RV), and a machine whose work tapes have bounded entry must carry about L/2 bits of the data in the coarse motion of its input head (Corollary IS). Unconditionally, paper 9's exclusions extend to machines of sufficiently low adaptivity (Theorem LA), and every linear-time transposer, adaptive or not, runs an external-memory transposition with O(d) I/Os that is oblivious on 2L−O(d) inputs (Theorem EM), so an I/O lower bound without the indivisibility assumption would refute (T).

A negative answer to (T) for all machines would give the first superlinear lower bound for integer multiplication on multitape machines (Harvey and van der Hoeven). The best known upper bound is O(L·(log d)1−κ), from an unrefereed preprint.
