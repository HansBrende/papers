\section{Normal forms}\label{sec:normal}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem N: two-sided codes on symmetric relations]\label{thm:N}
\formal{TwoSidedCodeTransfer.lean}{RowSpread.difference_fibre_twoSided, RowSpread.wholeRow_card_sq_le_of_twoSided, RowSpread.commonDeficit_iff_twoSided}
\formal{NormalizedCodeTransfer.lean}{RowSpread.twoSided_card_le_of_fixedCross, RowSpread.NormalizedTwoSidedWholeRowCardinalityBound.twoSided}
\formal{NormalizedRateTransfer.lean}{RowSpread.commonDeficit_iff_normalizedTwoSided, RowSpread.commonDeficitCanonicalBits_iff_normalizedTwoSided}
Let $C$ be a whole-row code for $\rho$ over a finite abelian group $\Sigma$ of size $q\ge2$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]
\item Some $D_z=C\cap(z+C^{\mathsf T})$ is two-sided for $\rho$ and has at least $|C|^2/q^{d^2}$ elements.
\item Let $R\in\N$. If every two-sided code on a symmetric reflexive relation of window at most $w+R+1$ has at most
$q^a$ elements, every two-sided code for a map of window at most $w$ has at most $q^{a+2wd^2/(R+1)}$ elements.
\item The existence of a common deficit is equivalent for all codes, for two-sided codes, and for two-sided codes
on symmetric reflexive relations. This holds both for canonical bit alphabets and for bounds uniform over all finite
alphabets. A two-sided deficit $\delta$ gives a one-sided deficit $\delta/2$; symmetrizing the relation has no further
asymptotic loss.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
\begin{outline}
Every ordered pair $(x,y)\in C^2$ has exactly one difference $z=x-y^{\mathsf T}$. Summing $|D_z|$ over the $q^{d^2}$
possible differences gives $|C|^2$. Membership in $C$ supplies column recovery, and membership in $z+C^{\mathsf T}$
supplies row recovery, since coordinate translations preserve agreement. This proves (a).

For (b), at most $wd/(R+1)$ rows occur in more than $R$ helper sets. Fix their entries and the entries of the
corresponding columns. Within each fibre, remove those rows from every helper set, then add the reverse relation
and the diagonal. The resulting relation is symmetric and reflexive with width at most $w+R+1$. There are at most
$q^{2wd^2/(R+1)}$ raw records, yielding (b). The exact finite version pays the overlap of the fixed rows and columns
only once, as used in \cref{thm:F}.

For (c), choose $R$ large enough for any prescribed error at a fixed original window, and apply (a). Reverse
implications are restrictions to subclasses. Canonical alphabets carry their usual bitwise additive structure;
for an arbitrary finite alphabet one may transfer a cyclic group structure along a bijection. Neither the code nor
its decoder is assumed invariant under that group operation or under transposition.
\end{outline}
