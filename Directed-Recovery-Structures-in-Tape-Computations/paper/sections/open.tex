\section{Construction requirements and the remaining coding problem}\label{sec:open}

A candidate oblivious linear-time transposer must fit both sides of the interface: its trace must evade the available cheap depth-reduction theorems, or its actual finite-alphabet recovery relation must support enough codewords to absorb every applicable genie cost. Expansion, connected demand pairs, and multiple cyclic inputs are not by themselves evidence of sufficient coding capacity. Conversely, a complex-linear theorem cannot exclude a machine unless its actual fibres meet that theorem's algebraic hypotheses.

The structural theorem suggests a finite-alphabet question that mentions neither a transition function nor a choice of local encoding gates. It has since been answered negatively~\cite{StorageCodes2026}.

\begin{question}[All-code paired-distance bound]\label{prob:open-paired}
Fix a degree bound~$\Delta$. Let $N_j$ be recovery relations on finite coordinate sets $V_j$ with $|V_j|\to\infty$. Suppose their incidence graphs have maximum degree at most~$\Delta$ and satisfy the paired-distance condition. Must every sequence of nonempty finite-alphabet storage codes $\mathcal C_j\subseteq\mathcal A_j^{V_j}$ satisfy
\[
 \limsup_{j\to\infty}\frac{\log_{|\mathcal A_j|}|\mathcal C_j|}{|V_j|}\le\frac12?
\]
Here $|\mathcal A_j|\ge2$; the alphabets may vary. The fixed binary case also has a negative answer, using nonlinear codes~\cite[Corollary~A$'$]{StorageCodes2026}; the case of $\mathbb F_2$-linear codes remains open.
\end{question}

\textbf{Update (November 2026).} The thirteenth paper constructs relations of fixed incidence degree $\Delta<127^{8\,791\,448}$ with every paired distance tending to infinity and $\mathbb F_{127^2}$-linear storage codes of rate at least $8100/16130>0.5021$~\cite[Theorem~A]{StorageCodes2026}. Its binary encoding has rate above $0.5013$, and its guaranteed rates over larger fields approach $4-2\sqrt3$~\cite[Corollary~A$'$ and Theorem~A$^*$]{StorageCodes2026}. Symmetrization also gives counterexamples with symmetric recovery relations~\cite[Corollary~U]{StorageCodes2026}, by \cref{rem:dc-comparison}. No tape realization is known. The native conclusion of \cref{prop:open-transfer}, a deficit strong enough for the comparison below, $\mathbb F_2$-linear codes, and the least degree at which the half bound fails remain open.

The directed analytic theorem answers the linear, bounded-complex-coefficient version at fixed vector width. The hyperfinite theorem answers the all-code question under an additional separator hypothesis. The native examples show that this separator hypothesis is not a consequence of a linear-time origin. Their separate coding bounds show, at the same time, that its failure is not evidence for a high-rate code. The two-input cyclic theorem gives another all-code regime, but \cref{prop:cc-native-obstruction} rules out universally cheap reduction to it. The row-word theorem supplies a fixed deficit under a different, explicitly verified interface.

\begin{proposition}[What an affirmative answer would supply]\label{prop:open-transfer}
\notformalized
If \cref{prob:open-paired} has an affirmative answer for every fixed~$\Delta$, then for every fixed tape count, linear-time constant, and cone depth, all finite-alphabet storage codes on the corresponding actual transposition-labelled cone relations have rate at most $1/2+o(1)$, uniformly over those schedules and genies.
\end{proposition}
\begin{informalproof}
Let $B$ be the fixed upper bound on recovery-set sizes. For any fixed $0<\delta<1$, condition on the coordinates whose left incidence degree exceeds an integer $M\ge B/\delta$. There are at most $\delta L$ such coordinates. The retained relation has maximum degree at most $\max(B,M)$, and the native structural theorem gives $b_E(r)=O_r(L/\log d)=o(L)$ for every fixed~$r$. Its number of coordinates is at least $(1-\delta)L$, so the same condition holds relative to its size.

An affirmative answer gives half-rate plus $o(1)$ on the retained relation. By \cref{lem:pre-conditioning}, a full code has logarithmic size at most
\[
 |H|+\tfrac12(L-|H|)+o(L)
 \le\tfrac12L+\tfrac12\delta L+o(L).
\]
First take $d\to\infty$ with $\delta$ fixed, then let $\delta\downarrow0$. Uniformity follows by contradiction: a violating sequence of schedules, genies, and codes would itself be a sequence to which \cref{prob:open-paired} applies after the same fixed-threshold trimming.
\end{informalproof}

\textbf{Update (November 2026).} The premise of \cref{prop:open-transfer} is false~\cite{StorageCodes2026}, so the implication is vacuous as stated. Its conclusion for the full native cone relations remains open. The proof needs the half bound only on the retained relations after paid degree trimming; assuming that restricted bound gives the same conclusion for the full relations. The eighth paper uses this native conclusion~\cite{BoundedNesting2026}; the eleventh requires analogous half bounds for relations inside bounded-radius balls and for skeleton-class relations~\cite[Section~2]{MergeDepth2026}.

This implication requires no assertion that the input and output ports are disconnected in the finite graphs. It also does not require that the recovery relation be symmetric. The counterexample to \cref{prob:open-paired} defeats the proposed abstract half bound; it supplies no transposer or known native realization~\cite{StorageCodes2026}.

\subsection{Depth, rate, and the order of choices}
The transposition application still needs a cheap genie. The fourth paper supplies it for home-based head schedules and certain other restricted motion classes, but the general depth-reduction problem remains open~\cite{BrendeCurved}. If an all-code half bound held on the actual cone relations, any such class admitting genies of an arbitrarily small fixed positive cost would be excluded from oblivious linear-time transposition.

The exact value $1/2$ is not essential to that comparison. A deficit $\rho>0$ works if one can choose a genie of cost $\eps L$ with $\eps<\rho$ and then apply the coding bound at its resulting fixed depth. A bound $\rho(D)>0$ for each~$D$ is not by itself enough: making the genie cheaper can increase~$D$, and the deficit may then fall below its cost. This is why the depth-independent leading constant in the connected-demand example is useful, even though it is smaller than one half.

\textbf{Update (November 2026).} A quantitative deficit is still a possible abstract route. On paired-distance-separated relations of incidence degree at most $\Delta$, the acyclic-set argument already gives rate at most $1-1/(\Delta+1)+o(1)$~\cite[Proposition~4.14]{BrendeCurved}; mere positivity at each fixed degree is therefore not the open problem. For the eighth paper's bounded-nesting reduction, a bound $1-\rho(\Delta)+o(1)$ suffices if $\rho(\Delta)\log\log\Delta\to\infty$~\cite[Remark~6.5]{BoundedNesting2026}. A positive deficit independent of degree would also suffice. The thirteenth paper refutes neither form.

For the specific native examples, the conclusion concerns all codes on their full cone relations. Their schedules already lie within classes excluded by the first paper's pass bounds. Extending the relation-level theorem to more general schedules would be new work; the examples are not a reduction of arbitrary machines to a finite set of scans.

\subsection{Finite fields and nonlinear recovery}
The complex theorem controls neither modular rank collapse nor nonlinear recovery. Bounded integer lifts expose modular rank loss as torsion; the thirteenth paper realizes this phenomenon in characteristic $127$ (\cref{sec:torsion}). The appendix's matrix certificates give an algebraic way to prove finite-field bounds without controlling coefficients or vector width, but only when the required matrix labels exist. The group-transfer theorem further covers endomorphic-expression recoveries over finite $2$-groups, and a sustained excess above half on its family requires a linear fraction of exceptional rows. Neither the label premise nor that expression language is known for arbitrary native codes. An unrestricted all-code half inequality is false; a native bound or a sufficiently strong quantitative deficit remains a substantive obligation.

The exact code class must therefore remain explicit in each application. A machine's genie is an arbitrary function of its binary input even when its head schedule is fixed. No replacement of that function by a complex linear map is available from the geometry alone.

\subsection{Adaptive schedules}
The geometric incidence theorem applies to each fixed legal adaptive trace. Its proof transports abstract labels along frozen head walks and does not randomize the inputs that select the trace. To use it for an adaptive machine, however, one must control the family of inputs producing that trace and the information needed to identify the relevant recovery relation. Different inputs can have different cones, output times, and genies. The tracewise theorem supplies none of that description for free.

Thus the paper isolates a specific structural invariant and several coding mechanisms that respect it. A complete lower-bound argument still needs a native half bound or another sufficiently strong finite-alphabet deficit, a compatible cheap depth reduction, and, for unrestricted~(T), a paid all-input treatment of adaptivity.
