3. EXACT INVERSE-FINAL-FACTOR IDENTITY.
Let b be a real trace-free two-by-two matrix and u=sI+t b, v=pI+q b, g be determinant-one matrices. Define
  D_g=det(bg-gb).
Then, with all products taken in SL2(R),
  tr(v u^-1 g v u g^-1)=2+(D_g-4det(b))*q^2-D_g*t^2.
This is verified by a general polynomial identity that retains determinant factors before specializing to SL2. It uses neither diagonalization nor a nonzero discriminant hypothesis.
If D_g!=0, the same identity reads
  tr(v u^-1 g v u g^-1)=2-D_g*(t^2+lambda_g*q^2),
  lambda_g=4det(b)/D_g-1.
Thus if X is the set of squared scalar coordinates of a common centralizer family, varying u,v over that same family gives an exact affine image of X+lambda_g X. This removes the moving-base-set issue in the earlier Y_g+D_g X formula. It does NOT establish that enough distinct/nonconcentrated lambda_g are realized by actual bounded words.

4. NILPOTENT SPECIALIZATION.
When trace(b)=det(b)=0, the identity becomes
  tr(v u^-1 g v u g^-1)=2+D_g*(q^2-t^2).
For D_g!=0 the normalized coefficient lambda_g is identically-1. Therefore this particular coefficient family cannot supply nonconcentrated scalar projections in the nilpotent case. This is an obstruction to this mechanism, not an impossibility theorem for multiplication, transposition or group growth. Near-nilpotent b may also cause a quantitatively narrow coefficient family and requires an explicit budget or separate branch.
