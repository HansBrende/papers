\label{sec:formalization}

All 32 numbered results and 6 numbered definitions
are Lean-verified;
their declarations and axiom commands accompany the paper. The index in

\subsection{Dependency order}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{The model and complete-source engine.}
Actual last-visit hops, full first-maximum families, the half-open span
partition and family accounting are formalized by reusing the eighth
paper and finite incidence counting. Strict clipped shadows and their
variation bounds, including successful words and disjoint packing, are
also formalized, as are protected nonleaf phases and the exact packed bank,
including closure across selected unpaid actual edges. Paid residual recovery
is formalized for arbitrary locally determined records, with revealed-root
reads and unpaid parents retained. The any-argument-path bound covers metric-valued paths;
it is not restricted to the original input-head timeline.

\item \emph{Finite deficit.}
Circular gap area, one common cyclic ordering of all raw coordinates,
sharp stripe discrepancy, and entropy recovery from a deterministic helper
are formalized in \cref{thm:cyclic-freezing}. Its sharper finite bound
retains maximum slot oscillations and improves the additive stripe error.
The helper is charged by entropy, without inferring a support-cardinality
bound. Minimum fractional core covers, interval rounding and the fractional
raw-source gap bound are also formalized. The shared-root count sharpens
its denominator to $2(2K+1)\kappa$; exact component incidences and
union-edge variation are retained before this relaxation.
Anchored erosion is formalized with loss $e-e^2/2$, where
$e=\min\{E/L,1\}$, and the cover credit retains that saving.
The full paid-cover assembly in \cref{thm:paid-source-cover} is verified:
the actual parent relation, bank, phase and transcript are consistent
throughout. Its terminal-core-orbit corollary,
\cref{cor:orbit-count-deficit}, is also verified, with the stronger mean
reciprocal of the actual fractional cover weights retained before applying
the worst-case count. All covering roots lie within the computation.

\item \emph{Uniform consequences.}
The actual hop ordering, common numerical joint profile, inherited mass
bounds and vanishing tails in \cref{def:joint-profile} are formalized.
Only numerical coordinates converge; no limiting geometry is asserted.
\Cref{thm:necessary-hop-tail} is also verified, using actual prefixes,
rounded widths and the full outside-family budget. The order is fixed
finite parameters, dimension limit, cutoff limit, then vanishing width
error. The distinguished and protected cutoffs are independent, and the
critical-moment conclusion is literal divergence of nonnegative partial
sums. The uniform one-exception deficit in \cref{cor:one-exception}
is verified as well, with constants independent of the positive entry
width. A reusable sequential-exclusion lemma turns the absence of a
near-full sequence into uniform constants, preserving any additional
class restriction. \Cref{cor:common-envelope} is verified in the stronger
form requiring only finitely many leading-rank bounds with positive slack.
Its power-envelope case reuses Mathlib's $p$-series theorem and the
moment-to-tail estimate. The finite-skeleton exclusion is verified for
every $q\ge0$, using an explicit zero profile and reclassification of the
same computation. Its contrapositive counts families with more than
$\eps L$ hops. \Cref{thm:necessary-cover} is verified, including its
inverse-square probability bound. It retains the actual mean reciprocal
cover through both upper limits; the earlier hop-tail inequality follows
by the terminal-orbit bound. The stronger form permits any fixed legal
modulus and independent cutoffs, and its probability consequence needs
only an envelope on the limiting profile. Both uniform cover criteria in
\cref{cor:uniform-cover} are verified, including the real-power case
for every $p>1$. A more general criterion permits any summable envelope
whose cover-budget-weighted tail has lower limit zero. Finite Cauchy,
Mathlib's integral comparison and the existing uniform-gap theorem supply
the proof; the same chosen order witnesses both class restrictions.
Do not replace the ordered limits by a single growing finite certificate.

\item \emph{Two-exception refinements.}
The complete continuation suppliers in \cref{prop:continuation-covers}
are verified. Their stronger forms retain actual orbit sizes and
allow interval covers with the quadratic erosion penalty.
\Cref{cor:necessary-exposure} is also verified, retaining the mean
quadratic penalty through the dimension and prefix lower limits.
It permits independent cutoffs and any fixed legal modulus; the original
linear loss follows directly. The full shared-support payment in
\cref{prop:finite-sharing} is verified. It retains the actual raw union
at the recovery stage, and the finite mean-support bound uses the smaller
older-bank movement constant while preserving the full residual-cut cost.
The global seed set ranges over all actual output-bit times, and its raw
payment is valid for every shift. The uniform global-sharing criterion,
necessary continuation density and superlinear seed growth are verified,
with dimension taken first along the same joint-profile subsequence.
The metric criterion and its covering-exponent corollary are also verified:
short raw intervals cost at most two grid blocks each, and one power-scale
argument gives both the envelope and no-envelope criteria. All geometric
bounds use the same original order and common dimension-independent cutoffs.

\item \emph{Independent construction tests.}
The serial native geometry is verified as an exact finite construction,
including all its return families, payments and arbitrary-seed cover
lower bound. The finite movement theorem is also verified, strengthened
to charge the largest slot movement at each step and retain the integer
partition count. The interval-query corollary is verified with the same
strengthening and only internal edge disjointness. The coded-caching
calibration is verified for the explicit helper and raw query interface,
including uniform fibres, joint independence, actual gap area and limits.
Its asymptotic movement corollary is verified as well: independent
internal thresholds give the interval interface the sharper centre-query
constant, even for varying repetition and word width. Causal group typing
is formalized, with the actual local observation domains and shared
capacity constraint on each attained fibre. The local collector is also
verified, with exact prefix capacity, output-delay volume, and the support
bound for every supersource/sink cut. Real-capacity max-flow/min-cut and
the actual augmentation give a flow of value $\log_2|\mathcal A_a(z)|$
on each group fibre, with the stated row caps and zero supply from unattached rows.
The full value is carried by a finite weighted path family. Removing
the two artificial initial arcs retains the actual row attachment and every
remaining traversal. Uniform allocation among the row's group bits preserves
each retained edge's load and gives each bit mass at most one. Actual
backward memory walks complete each bit's route to its own output.
Collector copies are charged to their original edges, and both traversal
directions count. On each group fibre the extra load is at most twice the
prefix size; its volume is at most twice the sum of output delays. Group
combination and helper-fibre averaging preserve mass $R_{\mathrm{sup}}$,
unit bit caps and shared physical capacities. The conditional projected
support is identified by positive probability on the exact input support.
The entropy and metric consequences complete \cref{thm:causal-group-routing},
including the total collector-volume bound and nonnegative total correlation.
The original interval-budget statement follows from the sharper prefix-budget
theorem. The native adapter identifies actual edges, attachments and
transposed bit positions, allowing the verified short-bit count to apply.
Explicit radius and volume estimates give \cref{cor:causal-group-obstruction},
with uniform constants and arbitrary varying positive entry widths.
These three branches do not serve as premises of the main uniform
exclusion theorem.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Reuse and statement fidelity}

The first, second, fourth, fifth and eighth papers contain relevant tape,
dependency, entropy and owner-order libraries. The native short-pair
count in \cref{sec:causal-groups} already imports the first paper's
row and ball counting declarations, and strengthens its output-block count.
The imported results are read from their original sources through
the existing build adapter in the second paper; no copy is introduced.
Family accounting imports the eighth paper's canonical family library
and strengthens the bound by retaining the owner's two distances to
the span endpoints. A shared weighted incidence identity supplies both
span-length and variation accounting; the original Q2 estimates follow
by bounding the overlap by two.
The clipped-shadow module reuses those actual families and the shared
monotone-patch library. Threshold crossing counts, Mathlib's finite-path
triangle inequality and order-convex preimage lemmas give the variation
bounds without a separate induction for each shadow word or packing.
The proof retains exact crossing counts and handles metric-valued paths.
The phase lemma reuses Mathlib's finite paths, cycle rotation and cardinality
bounds. Shared interval geometry, periodic phase counting and finite-orbit
modules keep the three arguments separate. The stronger cut deletes only
original nonleaves and charges their actual lower supports. The complete
orbit equality explicitly accounts for inactive actions.
The packed-bank proof reuses the patch bound and Mathlib's uniqueness of
paths in a forest. A common set of rank residues labels every spine
component injectively. Unordered endpoint pairs index the packed maps;
list composition handles the leaf sandwich and exceptional families.
The exact orbit proof permits the representing word of a packed slot to
vary with the argument. All originally selected unpaid actual edges are
covered, since the phase pays every lower of each deleted family.
Paid residual recovery uses the shared causal induction and grid-payment
lemmas. Cyclic freezing reuses finite-path variation and the first paper's
transpose-position formula, constructs actual disjoint eroded gap arcs,
and counts safe common orders. Sharp periodic-stripe discrepancy transfers
those counts to actual demands. The second paper's entropy inequalities
complete the deficit bound without new entropy machinery.
The interval-cover lemma imports the shared library's greedy cover and
packing certificate. It permits an empty target and arbitrary order-convex
sets in a linear order. Its fractional bound uses Mathlib's finite-sum
and order tools; no linear-programming development is needed.
Minimum fractional covers use Mathlib's extreme value theorem on the
finite weight cube. Canonical time components reuse path oscillation and
have disjoint internal edge sets. The actual complete-orbit count spends
distinguished owners and counts the common root once. Fractional weights
are transferred to these shared components before interval rounding;
the actual circular-gap estimate uses finite edge counting and
Cauchy--Schwarz. No cover-dependent merging or new entropy lemma is needed.
Anchored erosion uses arbitrary real lengths on a finite circular subdivision.
The sorted gaps partition its elementary edges. Anchoring preserves the old
gap relation on pairs of uncovered edges, and the shared partition-energy
lemma charges exactly the pairs touching the covered union. Real-coordinate
telescoping and a unit-weight specialization identify the area definitions.
The formal model is this finite subdivision, not an intrinsic AddCircle
or Haar-measure construction.
The paid-cover assembly reuses these results and Mathlib's finite
expectation identities. The subdivision-refinement adapter preserves raw
coordinates and their actual gap area; boundedness, rather than attainment,
justifies the supremum over chosen witnesses. Local record recovery and
whole-record capacity supply the same transcript for every output.
A common paid-averaging theorem accepts any valid raw-gap credits;
the terminal-orbit corollary reuses it with exact fractional covers.
A shared unit-weight cover lemma and regression of the actual roots
supply the finite fractional upper bound without a new covering premise.
The native adapter reuses finite-product equivalences and the first paper's
output-block and graph-ball definitions. Mathlib supplies logarithm,
square-root and quotient limits. The shared logarithmic-radius lemma gives
an explicit dimension threshold. All imports use sibling packages through
relative paths; the earlier frozen theorem statements are unchanged.

The following distinctions are part of the specification: a physical
head is not a return family; an endpoint witness is not a record with
free capacity; a source overcover is not a set of independent bits;
and a numerical subsequential profile is not a limiting geometry.
The movement theorem quantifies over order and matching but retains
its specified query interface. The causal theorem assumes local
functional separation on every helper fibre, not merely separated
final outputs. The serial example has a prescribed geometry and one
root and phase, not an implemented transposer or an averaged
counterexample.
