Local coding barriers and the limits of tape geometry for matrix transposition

Historical build and strengthening measurements remain in verification/.

COMMON NOTATION AND INTERPRETATION

A d-by-d matrix has b bits per entry and P=d^2*b input bits. For a nonempty
correct-input family F, D=P-log_2|F| is its entropy deficit. H(Z) denotes helper
entropy in bits for uniform input from F unless an arbitrary input law is specified. A high-entropy family has D=o(P).
The finite coding theorems allow general d,b. The native asymptotic theorems
use the original paper's canonical b=entryBits(d)=floor(log_2(d-1))+1, with
its d=1 convention, and P=inputLength(d). G is bits charged per work-tape
cell; k is the number of work tapes. Native means the original paper's
finite multitape machine, input/output conventions and paid transition count.

A formalized restricted-model exclusion is not a lower bound for arbitrary
transposition. No theorem below decides O(P) or O(P log* P) unrestricted
transposition, or integer-multiplication complexity. Kernel verification
checks the stated mathematical claims; it does not establish novelty.

1. ADAPTIVE ORIGINAL-ROW SEPARATORS AND TREEWIDTH [sources181/186/188]

A public deterministic strategy broadcasts bits. Each bit may be an arbitrary
nonlinear function of any finite set of ORIGINAL rows. Its support and
stopping decision depend only on the helper and public transcript. Receiver j
has only original row j, the helper, and transcript, and must reconstruct
column j. The strategy need terminate and be correct only on F.

For each completed transcript let G_x join every pair of rows queried together
(each support contributes a clique). Choose any separator S_x as a function
of helper and transcript. Let C range over components of G_x minus S_x, and
put L_x=d*b*|S_x|+b*sum_C |C|^2. Then

  E[length(transcript)] >= P - E[L_x] - 2D - 2H(Z).

R1911 proves this expected inequality for EVERY probability law w on F,
with expectations and helper entropy taken under w and D replaced by
D_w=P-H_w(X). The treewidth inequality below has the same arbitrary-law
extension. Weights may be zero; exact correctness still holds on all of the
declared F. Uniform-source statements and worst-case consequences remain.

If every transcript has length <=B, every L_x<=L, and the helper has at most
2^g attained values, then B>=P-L-2(D+g). Both coefficients2, for deficit and
helper separately, are sharp in the stated model (explicit d=2 witnesses at
every positive b). These witnesses have deficit or helper cost Theta(P);
sharpness within the o(P)-deficit/o(P)-helper regime is not established. Full virtual-fiber factorization and the charged prefix
budget are derived, not assumptions; correlated F and adaptive graphs are
allowed. The expected formulation uses a finite helper type.

For d,b>0 and E[length]<= (1-epsilon)P, with eta=(D+H(Z))/P,

  E[treewidth(G_x)] >= (d/4)*max(epsilon-2eta,0)^2
                          + max(epsilon-2eta,0)/2 - 1.

The supporting fragmentation theorem gives a separator with components of
size at most L and (L+1)|S| <= (treewidth(G)+1)d.

Strengthening: coefficient1/4 replaces the written1/9 treewidth coefficient
and removes its 1/d rounding loss. R1909 additionally retains the positive
max(epsilon-2eta,0)/2 term from integer-threshold optimization. Referee review also removed the unnecessary
two-row-per-query restriction; arbitrary finite supports are now admitted.
This is not permission to query arbitrary coded workspace as if it were an
original row. Original-row locality and public control remain essential.

Use: substantial communication savings require correspondingly complicated
interaction graphs. This eliminates separator-friendly original-row coding
strategies, while leaving globally coupled coded computation open.

Lean: GraphSeparatorBound, SeparatorSharpness, Treewidth, AdaptiveTreewidth.

2. NONLINEAR ROW RECODING FOLLOWED BY FIXED PAIRWISE XOR [source181B]

Apply an arbitrary bijection independently to each row. Broadcast B fixed
single-coordinate or two-coordinate XOR packets of the recoded data,
with pairwise supports local to a fixed row graph G. Each receiver has its
own ORIGINAL row and the broadcasts, and must recover its column for EVERY
input. Repetitions, same-row pairs, self-XORs and disconnected graphs are
permitted. Let Q_ell be the number of raw bit positions whose row and column
vertices are joined by a walk shorter than ell. For every integer ell>=0,

  B >= P - (P+ell*Q_ell)/(ell+1).

For maximum degree at most Delta and d>=2,

  B >= P - 6*log(max(2,Delta))*P/log(d).

For d>0 and any injective graph homomorphism into a cycle (including paths,
cycles, arbitrary relabelings and their subgraphs),

  B >= P - b*d*(sqrt(8*(d+3))-4).

The path/cycle inequality illustrates the XOR distance method. It is not the
strongest bound available here: the separator theorem also applies and yields
loss 2*b*d^(3/2)+O(b*d), rather than leading constant 2*sqrt(2). On a 100-cycle,
ten evenly spaced separator vertices leave ten components of size nine,
giving separator loss 1810*b. The degree bound is useful even on bounded-degree
expander families, for which small-component separators are not available.

Strengthening/precision: explicit finite constants and all stated graph
edge cases; no linearity restriction on the row bijections. The core result
matches the written finite bound. All-input correctness and fixed XOR
packets remain essential; this does not cover arbitrary adaptive nonlinear
broadcasts or noninvertible row recodings.

Use: independently recoding rows does not produce a constant-fraction saving
when the subsequent fixed XOR network has bounded degree. A useful coding
construction must escape this architecture or its graph constraints.

Lean: XorComponentCount, XorDegreeBound, XorPathCycleBound.

3. AFFINE HEAD MOTION AND QUANTITATIVE EXCEPTIONAL TIME [sources200/210]

For the original normalized Lipschitz head paths satisfying the required
M* inequality, suppose each head is locally affine on an open region covered
by countably many affine branches. Branch slopes may be positive, zero or
negative; the open branches need not form a disjoint interval partition.
The sum, across heads, of the measures of the omitted time sets is at least
1/2. In physical time (native steps divided by P), the corresponding bound
is 1/(2G). If all heads' exceptions lie in a common set, that set has measure
at least1/(2kG). Thus countably affine motion almost everywhere is impossible
for these required limiting paths. The proof uses the original full-level
consecutive-crossing convention and one admissible label with arbitrarily
small classes and crossing integral; it does not replace them by clipped
crossings or independent labels.

Consequently no fixed-k,fixed-G native O(P) transposition family can succeed
on correct sets of deficit o(P), with o(P) bits of nonuniform finite control,
if the relevant actual-head limits have such affine motion. Popular trace
classes and their deficits are derived. The nonuniform theorem includes
fixed uniform machines as a special case.

R1911 strengthens the abstract approximation support lemma: convergence of
u_i to the required limiting paths need only be pointwise on [0,H]. Its
separate uniform approximation error |u_i-q_i|<=e_i->0 remains required.
Existing native endpoints still derive uniform convergence and imply the
weaker pointwise premise; their statements remain unchanged.
R1916 further removes the uniform error envelope in new abstract and native
subsequential endpoints: the approximation error need only vanish at each fixed
head/time point. The fixed finite affine-cover bound on the chosen subsequence
remains. Existing uniform-error endpoints are exact specializations.

An approximation version excludes actual physical head paths approximable
with vanishing normalized error by a fixed finite number of affine interval
pieces (after the permitted subsequence selections). The intervals may overlap
or collapse; approximants need not be Lipschitz. The geometry assumption is quantified over every eligible diverging size
sequence and every input sequence eventually in the correct sets, with the
further-subsequence choices specified by the formal endpoint. No hypothetical
geometry for inputs outside the correct sets is needed.

Strengthening relative to our earlier internal formulations: eventual bounded interval covers replace more rigid partitions;
no Lipschitz assumption on the approximants; the correct-set-only geometry
premise is weaker. The local abbreviation SatisfiesStar uses just0<theta<1/2
of the original M* inequality, sufficient for this obstruction. Native padding
in item4 retains the full original M* conclusion, not just this restriction.

Use: fast constructions must generate enough genuinely non-affine physical
head motion. This rules out concrete motion classes but, in view of item4,
a geometric necessary condition alone must not be mistaken for an unrestricted
computational lower bound.

Lean: CountableAffinePathLabels, OpenAffineTimeBound, AffineTimeScaling,
NativeAffineMotion, BoundedAffineCovers, AffineApproximationObstruction,
PhysicalAffineApproximationObstruction. Independent abstract measure and
measurable finite-branch results are retained separately in CountableAffineLabels
and FiniteAffineObstruction.

4. UNCONDITIONAL FULLY PAID PADDING [sources201/202]

There exist absolute integers A,H,K0,E such that, for EVERY original native
machine M, there exists one fixed native machine M' with cellBits(M')=3
(five work symbols) with the following properties. For every positive
canonical dimension d, every input w of length P, and every output out and
time t for which M computes out on w within t, M' actually halts with the
same output at a time T<=A*P+t. Its actual entire execution up to T satisfies
the literal original zero-slack CutIneq. Generation of the padding walk,
initialization, all tape motion, cleanup, resetting and payload handoff are
paid. No precomputed walk, expansion assumption or unpaid oracle is supplied.
The resource constants also precede M, with exact formulas

  states(M')=H+states(M),
  tapes(M')=K0+(tapes(M)+E)*max(1,Nat.size(M.g)).

Here states(M)=M.q+1 and M.g+1 is the source work-alphabet size. R1910 exposes
these uniform resource bounds using the existing compiler contracts.

The wrapper includes E*max(1,Nat.size(M.g)) idle tapes, which stay blank with
heads at zero. Its fixed tape base is K0=53+q for the fixed rational family's
page count q. The finite-cut proof uses the coarse 3*k*M_b(S) boundary charge
and the chosen overhead ensuring 4*q<=3*k*eta. It does not prove the analogous
cut with a charge only for visited memory. The S=empty-set instances, excess lower bound, and existence of a
nonmonotone, nonantitone head are unaffected by discarding these idle heads.
The full M* assertion for nonempty S is not claimed to survive this deletion.

For every sequence d_i tending to infinity and every sequence of computations
of M bounded by a*P_i, the SAME M' has corresponding actual halting times
T_i<=(A+a)P_i and satisfies NativeRunLimits.Conclusion, including existence
of convergent subsequences and the full original M* conclusion for every
eligible subsequential limit.

Strengthening: all positive canonical dimensions, not merely sufficiently
large ones; an unconditional rational continuous expander and its paid native
compiler; universal A chosen before M (identified in referee review). The
fixed rational family precedes the scale and walk-length choices. This is
an existence theorem, not a practical extracted table of constants.

An immediately halting empty-output payload gives an unconditional example
satisfying these conditions without transposing. The construction supplies a
data-free prefix, not the data-coupled encoder/decoder proposed in the first paper.

Use: the finite CutIneq condition can be appended at additive linear cost
to arbitrary computation; full limiting M* conclusions also hold for
linear-time payload families. Therefore a contradiction
using only those conditions cannot by itself establish a general superlinear
transposition lower bound. It identifies what a stronger impossibility
argument must distinguish about the useful computation.

Lean: TransposeFollowup.PaidPadding.exists_uniform_paid_padding, in the
UnconditionalPaidPadding module, through
TransposeFollowup.exists_rational_continuous_expander in that same module,
and the actual paid compiler to NativeRunLimits.
The separate weak-limit return-measure identification/nonhyperfinite relation
mentioned in source201 is not a formal endpoint claimed here. The proposed
source201 o(P) opcode-generator speed is also not proved: the dense formal
generator has O(P) cost, sufficient for the intended total O(P) padding. The
source200 CFW/measured-hyperfinite method is replaced by direct affine proofs.

5. EXACT TWO-STAGE NONLINEAR HELPER BARRIER [source216.3/216.4]

One global helper selects the entire architecture and its row orders:
local input maps/permutation, a first triangular bijection, local bijections
and a permutation, a second causal stage, and local output maps/permutation.
The first triangular stage is bijective in each row's own argument with
arbitrary nonlinear dependence on earlier rows. Input and final local maps
and the second stage need not be invertible. No extra banks, unrestricted
middle global operation, or additional uncharged global control are allowed.

For any correct family F and any0<=r<=d,

  |F|*2^(b*r*(d-r)) <= |helper(F)|*2^P.

In particular |F|*2^(b*floor(d^2/4)) <= |helper(F)|*2^P. If |F|>=2^(P-D),
for any real D, and |helper(F)|<=2^g, then

  D+g >= b*floor(d^2/4).

The cardinality theorem allows empty or arbitrarily correlated F and an
infinite ambient helper type; only the attained image is counted. The positive
density premise excludes the empty case. Inverse block injectivity and suffix
support are derived from the specified primitive architecture.

Strengthening/precision: exact finite counts, every split r, real deficit
without rounding, and attained helper image. Noninvertible end wrappers and
the second causal stage were already allowed by the source, so they are not
claimed as a new strengthening. Source216.2/216.5's other extensions are
outside the six target statements.

Use: for this two-stage architecture, a near-full correctness family requires
about P/4 global helper bits. Merely adding nonlinear local maps and changing
row orders cannot eliminate that toll.

R1910 generalizes R1909's actual-helper-entropy toll to EVERY probability law
X supported on F, with entropy deficit D_X=P-H(X):

  D_X+H(helper(X)) >= b*r*(d-r), and >= b*floor(d^2/4).

Uniform X gives D_X=P-log2|F| and recovers the previous entropy statements.
The attained-helper subtype permits an infinite ambient helper type. A shared
fiber-cap entropy lemma applies to the existing per-branch cardinal bound;
there is no new architecture assumption. H(helper)<=log2|helper(F)|<=g
recovers the old charge. Original uniform, range and density statements remain
available. This broadens input laws, not the computational model.

Lean: TwoStageRows, TwoStageHelper, TwoStageHelperEntropy.

6. CONCRETE SCAN PALETTES AND CONTRACTING RECURSION [sources206/208/210.5]

Along any canonical size sequence d_i tending to infinity, no eventually
bounded-length family of words in any of the following declared palettes
can transpose correct sets with deficit o(P_i):

(a) Integer row addition/difference modulo2^(db), row XOR prefix/difference,
    row/entry reversals, declared row-dependent cyclic rotations, and full-bank
    increment/decrement.
(b) The declared F3 palette P/p,Q/q,R,C,E/e,D,S/s, with all b trit lanes kept
    exactly between operations. The final emission may even project trit2
    to bit0; no intermediate trit projection is made.
(c) The declared mixed binary masked-stride/shift word palette, including
    arbitrary public size-dependent masks, strides and offsets.

Public operation choices may depend arbitrarily on size but not on the input.
All final concrete palette theorems derive their native implementation,
correctness, time, resources and geometry; none asks the caller to assume
these missing computational obligations. The integer/F3 whole-word executor
loads once, uses actual prefix-produced stage states, then emits once. For
word length<=K it has two work tapes, five symbols and time<=(7+24K)P. Its
head-approximation error is <=32(db+1)K+4 and becomes o(P). State-bit cost
is also derived to be o(P).

The contracting-recursion theorem assumes fixed branching B, fixed stage
count J per node, nonnegative volumes V, sum of children's volumes<=rho*V
with0<=rho<1, root volume<=C*P, and own-work ticks per node<=a*V. Every actual
tick has an owner and stage. Each own-stage occupies a contiguous global-time
interval. Regularity has this precise meaning: for every fixed node, stage and
head, every subsequence of positive-duration stage occurrences with V tending
to infinity admits a further subsequence on which the frozen relative path

  [head(A+min(floor(V*s),L))-head(A)]/V,  0<=s<=a,

converges uniformly to a function with a finite affine interval cover. Here A
is the actual stage start and L its duration. The finite number of pieces may
depend on the stage and chosen subsequence. Clocks may depend on the input,
and this condition is required over all eligible sequences of actual correct
executions, not one specially chosen favorable sequence. Running time is
derived from these paid budgets. There is NO requirement that all descendant
calls form nested contiguous call intervals. Thus the formal exclusion covers
a broader stage-clock class than the source's contiguous-call recursion.

The clock budgets alone do not exclude arbitrary linear-time schedules:
when constants may be chosen, one node, one stage, and volume proportional to
P suffice. The affine regularity of large stages is the substantive restriction.
The intended non-affine Mobius stages in the first paper are outside it unless
the relevant limits satisfy that additional regularity. The exceptional-work
bound is a quantitative requirement, not an exclusion of every recursive design.

For high-entropy correct native executions with fixed k,G and o(P) control
bits, let arbitrary exceptional stages occupy at most eta*P+o(P) actual ticks,
uniformly over correct inputs; regularity is required for the other stages.
Then

  1/(2G) <= k*eta, and eta >= 1/(2kG).

Zero exceptional work gives the regular-recursion impossibility. If instead
each node's exceptional ticks are at most eta*V(node), the explicit aggregation
and native corollary give

  eta*C/(1-rho) >= 1/(2kG).

Strengthening relative to our earlier internal formulations: arbitrary size-dependent bounded words, high-entropy correctness
sets, broader stage-clock scheduling, and final ternary projection. The per-node
corollary is now explicit and reuses the existing tree-charge theorem. These
are exclusions of the stated palettes/clocks. Unbounded word counts, unmodeled
operations or uncharged access must not be silently folded into a primitive.
Arbitrary-width primitive correctness is not an arbitrary-width asymptotic
transposition exclusion.

Use: reject proposed constructions immediately if they fall into these concrete
classes. Any surviving linear-time construction must escape their proven
geometric/computational restrictions, with its cost actually accounted for.

Lean: MatrixPaletteObstructions, RawMixedBinaryWordObstruction,
NativeRegularRecursion, NativeExceptionalRecursion, ExceptionalStageClock.
