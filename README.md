| [High-Rate Separated Storage Codes and Tape Transposition](High-Rate-Separated-Storage-Codes-and-Tape-Transposition/) | Hans Brende and GPT-6 Astra (OpenAI) | November 2026 | Paper ([PDF](https://hansbrende.github.io/papers/High-Rate-Separated-Storage-Codes-and-Tape-Transposition/paper/main.pdf), LaTeX sources). Binary storage codes with polynomially vanishing rate deficit and diverging paired distances at each sufficiently large fixed degree cap, and common partitions for every source subset at all large lengths. These rule out the unrestricted slow-gap route suggested by the paper's tape-transfer theorem. It also gives native tape criteria, quantitative transfer bounds and limits on local prediction. The new construction is Lean-verified from an explicit seed hypothesis, supplied by a written proof from Paper 13; the tape-transfer, prediction and amplifier barriers are also formalized. |
| [Oblivious Linear-Time Tape Transposers Need Macroscopic Crossings](Oblivious-Linear-Time-Tape-Transposers-Need-Macroscopic-Crossings/) | Hans Brende, Claude Opus 5.5 (Anthropic) and Gemini 4 Argon (Google DeepMind) | November 2026 | Paper ([PDF](https://hansbrende.github.io/papers/Oblivious-Linear-Time-Tape-Transposers-Need-Macroscopic-Crossings/paper/main.pdf), LaTeX sources) and its Lean 4 formalization. Every oblivious linear-time transposer routes a linear amount of information through crossings of revisits whose excursions are a fixed fraction of the input: from every large output set, cutting these macroscopic crossings off costs linearly many steps. The expansion sits on one leg of a coarse skeleton drawn from a finite family independent of the size, two work heads must make long revisits, and the same holds on heavy traces of adaptive machines and on the heavy traces of the fifteenth paper's simulator. Also a cut bound from the transposition demand itself, grouped pass counting with coarse passes, class exclusions for boundedly many macroscopic phases, oblivious and adaptive, and a catalogue of excluded schedules (written proofs for Proposition 10.2 and Corollary 7.3; proof sketches for Remark 8.2, the θ = 2 perfect-unshuffle realization after Corollary 10.4, and the individual schedules of the catalogue). |
| [Order, Symmetry and the Swap: A Synthesis of Matrix Transposition on Tapes](Order-Symmetry-and-the-Swap-A-Synthesis-of-Matrix-Transposition-on-Tapes/) | Hans Brende and Claude Fable 5.2 (Anthropic) | November 2026 | Paper ([PDF](https://hansbrende.github.io/papers/Order-Symmetry-and-the-Swap-A-Synthesis-of-Matrix-Transposition-on-Tapes/paper/main.pdf), LaTeX sources) and its Lean 4 formalization. A synthesis of the sixteen earlier papers and their memos for a new reader, a diagnosis of where they stop, and two rigidity theorems for the swap in a linear one-hop model. Order: the code of the matrices that K = Σ A_a ⊗ B_a transposes correctly has dimension at most the sum of the dimensions of the images of any flag under the B_a, so two terms, commuting families and, in characteristic p with d = p^m, all affine index maps stay at the dimension d(d+1)/2 of the symmetric matrices. Symmetry: decoders shared along rotations of one index on M intervals give dimension at most (d² + Md)/2, on a cycle and on a line, with equality for equal intervals; for classes with no almost-period the bound fails, by an explicit code of dimension 25 > 24 at d = 6. Lean: the coordinate form of the first theorem and its attainment, the pigeonhole lemma, the second theorem on a cycle and on a line with its attainment, the twist-class variant, the bounds for decoders that change at single entries, with and without advice, and the explicit code; written proofs for the corollaries that need an algebraically closed field or p-groups, for copy rules, for the modulation and digit corollaries, and for an unbounded gain at every fixed number of classes with no almost-period, over every field: d/4 − 1 for two classes at every d = 4k ≥ 8 (a rule from the paper's memo, extended to characteristic 2), and by blow-ups for more, and for two bounds for sets of matrices and decoders that are not linear; exact computations, at sizes up to six and for the two-class rule and its blow-ups at larger sizes, as numerical remarks. Both theorems are statements about the one-hop model, not about machines. |
