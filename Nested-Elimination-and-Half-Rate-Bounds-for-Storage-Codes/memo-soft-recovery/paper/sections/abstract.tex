\begin{abstract}
We extend the decomposition theorem of \emph{Nested Elimination and
Half-Rate Bounds for Storage Codes} to recovery with nonzero conditional
uncertainty. Every certified decomposition gives a half-rate inequality
with an explicit residual term, retaining the conditioning supplied by
earlier pieces. We then account for additional helper records by their
actual occurrences and conditioning contexts. A reused helper pays for
its terminal mutual information and positive information losses between
uses; literal nesting of contexts is unnecessary. Combining these bounds
with exact revelation costs and coordinate credits gives one inequality
that permits correlated nonlinear records, discarded information, and
incomplete recovery. Simpler capacity bounds, including a coefficient
$\min\{2,k_e\}$ for a record covered by $k_e$ retained lists, follow as
corollaries. All new numbered results are proved in Lean. Finite entropy
examples establish sharp coefficients and show why the refined loss must
be evaluated after revelation. The memo supplies no new uniform tape
transposition lower bound; extracting affordable certificates from actual
computations remains open.
\end{abstract}
