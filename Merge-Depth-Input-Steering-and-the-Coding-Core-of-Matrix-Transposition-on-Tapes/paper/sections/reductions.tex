\section{Unconditional reductions}\label{sec:reductions}

This section proves two reductions that use no coding hypothesis. Both reduce adaptive machines to oblivious ones, at a
price in input entropy. \Cref{thm:LA} freezes one likely trace: the exclusions of the ninth
paper~\cite{SourceCovers2026}, which are proved there for one fixed computation geometry, then apply to every machine of
sufficiently low adaptivity whose likely trace has the family structure they require. \Cref{thm:EM} works at block
scale, where no hypothesis on adaptivity is needed: every linear-time transposer yields an external-memory transposition
with $O(d)$ I/Os, linear in the number of blocks, that is oblivious on a set of $2^{L-O(d)}$ inputs. Consequently an
I/O lower bound of order $\omega(d)$ for this form of transposition, proved without the indivisibility assumption of the
classical external-memory bounds, would refute~(T) (\cref{cor:EM}).

\paragraph{Machines and traces.} We use the machine model and the notation of \cref{sec:prelim}, with the following
details~\cite[Section~2.1]{BoundedNesting2026}. A machine~$M$ has state set~$Q$ and $k$ work tapes over the
alphabet~$\Gamma$, which contains the blank; every step writes the cell under each work head, and the input cells outside
$[0,L)$ hold an end marker. For $X$ uniform on $\{0,1\}^L$, the \emph{probability} of a trace~$\tau_0$ is
$\Pr[\tau(X)=\tau_0]$. The value $v_t(X)$ of step~$t$ consists of the state after the step, the $k$ symbols written and
the output bit appended at the step, or a symbol for ``none''. It takes at most $2^{\beta(M)}$ values, where
$\beta(M)=\lceil\log_2\card Q\rceil+k\lceil\log_2\card\Gamma\rceil+2$ is the \emph{value width} of~$M$~\cite[Section~4.1]{FourthPaper2026}.
We say that $M$ \emph{transposes at dimension~$d$ within $c(L+1)$ steps} if on every input $X\in\{0,1\}^L$, $L=d^2b(d)$,
it halts within $c(L+1)$ steps with output~$X^\T$.

\subsection{Low adaptivity}\label{sec:reductions-LA}

We recall the return families of the ninth paper. Let $x_1,\dots,x_k$ be the work-head walks of a trace with halting
time~$T$, on the times $0,\dots,T-1$. A long hop $(p,u)$ of head~$h$ (\cref{sec:prelim}) has $u-p\ge2$, $x_h(p)=x_h(u)$
and $x_h(t)\ne x_h(u)$ for $p<t<u$. Its \emph{side} is upper or lower according as the head stays above or below the
cell $x_h(u)$ between $p$ and~$u$, and its \emph{owner} is the first time in $(p,u)$ at which the head is farthest from
that cell~\cite[Section~2.2]{BoundedNesting2026}. A \emph{complete family} is the set of all long hops of one head, on one
side, with one owner; its \emph{hop count} is its number of hops, and its \emph{full span} is the interval $(p_G,U_G]$ of
times, where $(p_G,U_G)$ is its outermost hop. The complete families of the trace admit an \emph{$N$-class span
partition} if they can be partitioned into $N$ classes whose full spans are pairwise disjoint within each class, and a
set of complete families is a \emph{$\mathrm Q_2$ core} if it admits such a partition into two
classes~\cite[Definition~2.1]{SourceCovers2026}. An $N$-class span partition exists exactly when no time lies in more
than $N$ full spans: one direction is clear, and for the other, colour the families greedily in the order of the left
ends of their spans; when a family is coloured, the families already coloured whose spans meet its span all contain the
first time of its span, so they use at most $N-1$ colours (\lean{MergeDepth.hasSpanColoring_iff_activeFamilies_card_le}).

\begin{theorem}[Theorem LA: low adaptivity]\label{thm:LA}
\formal{MergeDepth/LowAdaptivity.lean}{MergeDepth.low_adaptivity_a,MergeDepth.low_adaptivity_b,MergeDepth.low_adaptivity_a_exists,MergeDepth.low_adaptivity_b_exists}
Let $M$ be a machine, let $\beta\ge\beta(M)$ and $c>0$, and let $N\ge1$ and $q\ge0$ be integers.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item Let $\eps>0$ and $d_0$ be constants that~\cite[Corollary~4.5]{SourceCovers2026} provides for the parameters
$(2c,\beta,N,q)$, and let $\eta<\eps$. Then at no $d\ge\max(d_0,1)$ does $M$ have both of the following properties at
$L=d^2b(d)$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $M$ transposes at dimension~$d$ within $c(L+1)$ steps;
\item some trace $\tau_0$ has probability at least $2^{-\eta L}$, its complete families admit an $N$-class span partition,
and at most $q$ of them have more than $\eps L$ hops.
\end{enumerate}
\item Let $\eps>0$ and $d_0$ be constants that~\cite[Corollary~4.3]{SourceCovers2026} provides for the parameters
$(2c,\beta,N)$, and let $\eta<\eps$. Then the conclusion of~(a) holds when the last clause of~(ii) is replaced by:
the complete families of $\tau_0$ split into a $\mathrm Q_2$ core, at most one further family, and families of at most
$\eps L$ hops each.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{outline}
We prove~(a). Suppose that $M$ has properties (i) and~(ii) at some $d\ge\max(d_0,1)$, and let $W$ be the set of inputs
$X\in\{0,1\}^L$ with $\tau(X)=\tau_0$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:LA-fibre}
 \card W=2^L\Pr[\tau(X)=\tau_0]\ge2^{(1-\eta)L}.
\end{equation}
The ninth paper allows its fixed geometry to be one trace shared by a set of inputs~\cite[Section~2.1]{SourceCovers2026};
we show that $\tau_0$ and~$W$ form an admissible geometry in the sense of~\cite[Definition~2.2]{SourceCovers2026}.

\emph{The geometry.} The trace $\tau_0$ fixes the halting time~$T$, the input-head walk~$x_0$ and the work-head walks
$x_1,\dots,x_k$ on $\{0,\dots,T-1\}$, all of which move by at most one cell per step, and the append times. By~(i), the
machine appends exactly $L$ bits on every $X\in W$, at most one per step, so the append times are
$t_0<t_1<\dots<t_{L-1}<T$, and output position~$r$ is appended at step~$t_r$. Moreover $T\le c(L+1)\le2cL$, because
$L\ge1$; this is why the runtime constant is~$2c$, as the ninth paper's geometries have $T\le cL$. Let $x(t)$ be $x_0(t)$
clamped to $[0,L-1]$.

\emph{Local determination.} Let $X\in W$ and $t<T$. Step~$t$ applies the transition function of~$M$ to the state at
time~$t$, which is the initial state if $t=0$ and the state component of $v_{t-1}(X)$ otherwise; to the input symbol,
which is $X_{x_0(t)}$ if $0\le x_0(t)<L$ and the end marker otherwise; and to the symbols under the work heads. All runs
on~$W$ move their heads as $\tau_0$ prescribes, and every step writes the cell under each work head. Hence the cell under
work head~$h$ at time~$t$ holds the symbol written at step $\prev_h(t)=\max\{u<t:x_h(u)=x_h(t)\}$, a component of
$v_{\prev_h(t)}(X)$, and the blank if that set is empty. If $\prev_h(t)\ne t-1$, then $(\prev_h(t),t)$ is a long hop of
head~$h$. Consequently, for $X,Y\in W$: if $X_{x(t)}=Y_{x(t)}$ and $v_u(X)=v_u(Y)$ for $u=t-1$ (when $t\ge1$) and for the
lower ends~$u$ of all long hops ending at~$t$, then $v_t(X)=v_t(Y)$. This is the local recursion
of~\cite[Lemma~4.2]{FourthPaper2026}, for all inputs that follow~$\tau_0$. Its parents are the state edge and the work
previous-visit edges of~\cite[Section~2.1]{SourceCovers2026}, and the input bit at the clamped position $x(t)$ is the
direct read there; when $x_0(t)$ lies outside the input, that read is superfluous, which only enlarges the source sets,
as noted there.

\emph{Correctness and capacity.} For $X\in W$ and $0\le u<L$, the output bit at position $\tpos(u)$ is appended at step
$t_{\tpos(u)}$ and is a component of $v_{t_{\tpos(u)}}(X)$; by~(i) it equals~$X_u$. This is the transpose demand
of~\cite[Section~2.1]{SourceCovers2026}. The records $v_t$ take at most $2^\beta$ values.

Thus the walks and append times of~$\tau_0$, the correctness set~$W$, the values~$v_t$ as records and the map that
extracts the output bit from a record form an admissible geometry with entry width $b(d)$, runtime constant~$2c$, capacity
$\beta$ and the $N$-class span partition of~(ii). Its core and exceptional families are labels only; for~(a) we take both
empty and let $a_0$ be the largest hop count of any family, or $0$ if there is none. Its correctness deficit is
$\Delta=L-\log_2\card W\le\eta L$, by~\eqref{eq:LA-fibre}.

Let $\mathcal E$ be the set of complete families of~$\tau_0$ with more than $\eps L$ hops. By~(ii), $\card{\mathcal E}\le q$.
Every other family has at most $a=\lfloor\eps L\rfloor$ hops, and $a\le\eps L$. Since $d\ge d_0$,
\cite[Corollary~4.5]{SourceCovers2026}, which permits a skeleton~$\mathcal E$ that differs from the geometry's labels,
gives $\Delta\ge\eps L$. Hence $\eps L\le\eta L$, contradicting $\eta<\eps$.

For~(b), take the given $\mathrm Q_2$ core as the core and the further family, unless it lies in the core, as the
exceptional set, and put $a_0=\lfloor\eps L\rfloor$, which bounds the hop count of every remaining family. The same
modelling gives an admissible geometry with at most one exception and $a_0\le\eps L$, and
\cite[Corollary~4.3]{SourceCovers2026} gives $\Delta\ge\eps L$, which contradicts $\Delta\le\eta L$ as before.

Formally, the machines are those of the frozen model, and~(i) is \lean{MergeDepth.TransposesAt}, the time bound of the
frozen statement of~(T) at the one dimension~$d$. The trace of a run records, for every step before the halting time,
the moves and whether a bit is appended (\lean{MergeDepth.trace}); its probability is \lean{MergeDepth.traceProb}, and
the step values and the value width are \lean{MergeDepth.stepValue} and \lean{MergeDepth.valueWidth}. The constants are
any $(\eps,d_0)$ that satisfy the conclusion of the ninth paper's corollaries (\lean{MergeDepth.IsSkeletonConstant},
\lean{MergeDepth.IsOneExceptionConstant}), which provide them as
\lean{SourceCovers.finite_skeleton_uniform_deficit} and \lean{SourceCovers.one_exception_uniform_deficit};
\lean{MergeDepth.low_adaptivity_a_exists} and \lean{MergeDepth.low_adaptivity_b_exists} choose them depending only
on $(c,\beta,N,q)$ and $(c,\beta,N)$, for all machines of value width at most~$\beta$ at once. The modelling step is \lean{MergeDepth.fibreGeometry}, with local
determination \lean{MergeDepth.stepValue_eq_of_parents} and the deficit bound \lean{MergeDepth.fibreGeometry_deficit_le}.
The bound $d\ge1$ is needed: at $d=0$ the input is empty, and a machine that halts at once has properties (i) and~(ii).
The proof gives more. It needs the inputs to follow~$\tau_0$ only up to a time by which $\tau_0$ has appended $L$ bits,
and correctness only on those inputs: with the same constants, for every machine whose step values have capacity
$\log_2(3\card Q\card\Gamma^k)\le\beta$, every entry width $b\ge1$ in place of $b(d)$, and every time $T'\le c(L+1)$
before which $\tau_0$ appends at least $L$ bits, at most $2^{(1-\eps)L}$ inputs
follow~$\tau_0$ up to time~$T'$ and are eventually transposed, if the families of~$\tau_0$ formed before~$T'$ have the
structure of~(ii) (\lean{MergeDepth.card_correctSet_le_a}, \lean{MergeDepth.card_correctSet_le_b}). The motion after~$T'$
and the halting time are unconstrained.
\end{outline}

Hypothesis~(ii) holds, for instance, if at most $2^{\eta L}$ distinct traces occur and each of them has the stated family
structure; more generally, it holds if the trace has Shannon entropy $\mathrm H(\tau(X))\le\eta L$ for a uniformly random
input and every trace of probability at least $2^{-\eta L}$ has that structure, since
$\mathrm H(\tau(X))\ge-\log_2\max_{\tau}\Pr[\tau(X)=\tau]$. In probability form, for a machine that transposes at
dimension $d\ge\max(d_0,1)$ within $c(L+1)$ steps, every trace with the family structure of~(ii) has probability at most
$2^{-\eps L}$ (\lean{MergeDepth.traceProb_le_a}, \lean{MergeDepth.traceProb_le_b}), which gives (a) and~(b) for all
$\eta<\eps$ at once. In particular, a transposer all of whose traces have the structure of~(a) has at least $2^{\eps L}$
distinct traces (\lean{MergeDepth.card_traces_ge_a}).

The theorem is the freezing argument of the proof of~\cite[Corollary~6.4]{BoundedNesting2026} applied to the unconditional
exclusions of the ninth paper: the information of the trace is paid once, through the probability of~$\tau_0$, and it is
smaller than the deficit $\eps L$ that the frozen geometry forces. It thereby moves the classes excluded by the ninth paper
from oblivious machines to machines of low adaptivity, without a coding hypothesis. The ninth paper's formal object for a
fixed geometry takes the geometric data as hypotheses and does not derive them from a Turing
machine~\cite[Section~2.1]{SourceCovers2026}; here that modelling step is formal too, since
\lean{MergeDepth.frozenGeometry} derives every field of the object from a trace of a machine in the frozen model.

\begin{remark}[Scope of Theorem~LA]\label{rem:LA-scope}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
\item The constant~$\eps$ is not explicit. The ninth paper obtains its uniform constants by contradiction, extracting
convergent hop profiles by compactness~\cite[Section~4.1]{SourceCovers2026}. \Cref{thm:LA} therefore excludes adaptivity
below an unspecified threshold, whereas the conditional Corollary~F$''$ of the eighth
paper~\cite[Corollary~6.4]{BoundedNesting2026} allows every $\eta<\frac12$.
\item Like the entropy hypothesis of Corollary~F$''$, hypothesis~(ii) concerns the full trace, including the motion of
the input head, so padding that motion with data-dependent detours that leave the computation unchanged can destroy
it~\cite[Section~6.2]{BoundedNesting2026}. Theorem~SE (\cref{thm:SE}) avoids this for Corollary~F$''$ by passing to the
coarse skeleton; the admissible geometries of the ninth paper, however, fix the fine walks of all heads, the input head
included.
\item The proof uses~\cite[Corollary~4.5]{SourceCovers2026} only through its bound on the correctness set, which holds
uniformly over a class of admissible geometries. The same freezing therefore transfers the other class-uniform bounds
of~\cite[Section~4]{SourceCovers2026}, its Corollaries~4.4 and~4.7, with their hypotheses imposed on the frozen geometry
of~$\tau_0$ (\lean{MergeDepth.card_correctSet_le_of_class}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\subsection{Block scale: an oblivious external-memory image}\label{sec:reductions-EM}

A simulation that keeps two consecutive blocks of each tape in internal memory sees the motion of a machine only when a
head leaves its two blocks. A linear-time machine does so $O(d)$ times, each time carrying a constant number of bits; so
fixing this coarse motion costs only $O(d)$ bits of input entropy, and on the inputs that share it the simulation is
oblivious. We use the following form of the external-memory model of Aggarwal and Vitter~\cite{AggarwalVitter1988}.

\begin{definition}[External-memory algorithms]\label{def:EM}
\formal{MergeDepth/EMModel.lean}{MergeDepth.EM.Alg,MergeDepth.EM.Runs,MergeDepth.EM.inputDisk,MergeDepth.EM.HaltsWith,MergeDepth.EM.CorrectAt,MergeDepth.EM.ObliviousOn}
Fix a finite alphabet $\Sigma$ containing $0$, $1$ and a blank~$\sqcup$, and integers $B,m\ge1$ and $r\ge0$. A
\emph{block} is a word in $\Sigma^B$. A \emph{disk} is a map $D\colon\Z\to\Sigma^B$ whose values are blank blocks at all
but finitely many \emph{addresses}. The \emph{internal memory} consists of $m$ \emph{slots}, each holding one block, and a
\emph{register} of $r$ bits; its contents form the set $\mathcal M=(\Sigma^B)^m\times\{0,1\}^r$, and its size is
$mB\log_2\card\Sigma+r$ bits. An \emph{I/O} is a triple $(\sigma,i,a)$ with a direction
$\sigma\in\{\mathrm{read},\mathrm{write}\}$, a slot $i\in\{1,\dots,m\}$ and an address $a\in\Z$. An
\emph{external-memory algorithm} with these parameters is a map
\[
 \mathcal A\colon\mathcal M\to\{\mathrm{halt}\}\cup\bigl(\{\mathrm{read},\mathrm{write}\}\times\{1,\dots,m\}\times\Z
 \times\mathcal M\bigr).
\]
A \emph{run} from a disk~$D$ starts with blank slots and a zero register and repeats the following. If
$\mathcal A(\mu)=\mathrm{halt}$ for the current contents~$\mu$, the run stops. Otherwise
$\mathcal A(\mu)=(\sigma,i,a,\mu')$; a read replaces $\mu$ by $\mu'$ with slot~$i$ replaced by $D(a)$, and a write sets
$D(a)$ to slot~$i$ of $\mu'$ and replaces $\mu$ by~$\mu'$. The arbitrary map $\mu\mapsto\mu'$ models free internal
computation. The \emph{I/O sequence} of a run is the sequence of the I/Os $(\sigma,i,a)$ it performs, and their number is
its number of I/Os.

For transposition at dimension~$d$ the block size is $B=d\,b(d)$, one matrix row, so that $L=dB$. On input
$X\in\{0,1\}^L$ the initial disk holds row~$i$ of~$X$, that is the bits at input positions $iB,\dots,iB+B-1$, at
address~$i$ for $0\le i<d$, and is blank elsewhere. The algorithm is \emph{correct} on~$X$ if its run halts with row~$j$
of~$X^\T$ at address $d+j$ for every $0\le j<d$. It is \emph{oblivious on a set}~$\mathcal C$ of inputs if its runs on
all inputs in~$\mathcal C$ halt with the same I/O sequence.
\end{definition}

In the formal model a disk is any map $\Z\to\Sigma^B$; nothing changes, since runs from the input disks keep all but
finitely many addresses blank.

\begin{theorem}[Theorem EM: an oblivious external-memory image]\label{thm:EM}
\formal{MergeDepth/ExternalMemory.lean}{MergeDepth.EM.theoremEM,MergeDepth.EM.theoremEM_sharp,MergeDepth.EM.em_core,MergeDepth.EM.em_oblivious}
Let $M$ be a machine with $k$ work tapes, state set~$Q$ and work alphabet~$\Gamma$, let $c>0$, and put
\[
 c_1=4(k+1)c+2,\qquad c_2=\bigl(2(k+1)c+1\bigr)\log_2(2k+4).
\]
There is $\gamma>0$, depending only on $k$, $c$ and $\card Q$, with the following property. Let $d\ge1$ be a dimension at
which $M$ transposes within $c(L+1)$ steps, and put $B=d\,b(d)$. Then there is an external-memory algorithm over the
alphabet~$\Sigma_M$ that consists of $0$, $1$, the end marker and the symbols of~$\Gamma$, with the blank of~$\Gamma$ as
its blank, with block size~$B$, $2k+3$ slots and a register of at most $\gamma\log_2(2L)$ bits, such that:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item it is correct on every input $X\in\{0,1\}^L$ and performs at most $c_1d$ I/Os on each;
\item it is oblivious on a set $\mathcal C$ of at least $2^{L-c_2d}$ inputs.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{outline}
Fix~$d$, and let $T_0=\lfloor c(L+1)\rfloor$, so that $M$ halts within $T_0$ steps on every input. Call the input tape
tape~$0$ and the work tapes tapes $1,\dots,k$. Block~$j\in\Z$ of a tape consists of its cells $jB,\dots,jB+B-1$.

\emph{Layout.} Block~$i$ of the input tape, for $0\le i<d$, is row~$i$ of the input, stored at address~$i$. The other
blocks of the input tape consist of end markers and are never stored. Block~$j$ of work tape~$h$ is stored at address
$2d+k\,z(j)+h-1$, where $z(j)=2j$ for $j\ge0$ and $z(j)=-2j-1$ for $j<0$; these addresses are distinct and at least
$2d$, and they are blank initially, as the work tapes are. The $n$-th group of $B$ consecutive output bits, $0\le n<d$, is
written to address $d+n$.

\emph{Memory.} Slots $2\tau+1$ and $2\tau+2$ hold the \emph{window} of tape~$\tau\in\{0,\dots,k\}$: its blocks $j_\tau$
and $j_\tau+1$, the lower one in slot $2\tau+1$. Slot $2k+3$ is an output buffer. The register holds the state of~$M$, the
head positions $x_\tau$, the window indices $j_\tau$, the number~$n$ of flushes so far, the number of bits in the buffer,
and a \emph{phase}: before the first I/O, running, or waiting for the second I/O of a shift of work tape~$h$ in one of
the two directions, $2k+2$ phases in all. Since a run starts with the zero register, the register values are stored by
an injective encoding that maps the initial value, in the phase before the first I/O, to the zero word.

\emph{Invariants.} Each window contains the current contents of its blocks; every work-tape block outside its window has
its current contents at its address; the head of tape~$\tau$ lies in its window $[j_\tau B,(j_\tau+2)B)$, or, right
after a step of~$M$, one cell outside it; and the output bits appended so far are those at the addresses
$d,\dots,d+n-1$ followed by those in the buffer. Initially $j_\tau=-1$ for every~$\tau$. The work windows are then blank
and the lower input slot consists of end markers, both without an I/O, and the algorithm reads address~$0$ into
slot~$2$; this first I/O is the same for all inputs.

\emph{Simulation.} With the windows in memory, the algorithm simulates the steps of~$M$ without I/Os: the symbols under
the heads are in the windows, every work symbol written goes into its window, and every output bit appended goes into the
buffer. After each step it processes the \emph{events} of that step, first the shifts in the order of the tapes, then a
flush, and then it simulates the next step, or halts if $M$ has halted.
\begin{itemize}
\item If the head of tape~$\tau$ has reached $(j_\tau+2)B$ (a \emph{right shift}), the algorithm writes the lower slot to
the address of block~$j_\tau$ if $\tau\ge1$, copies the upper slot into the lower one, fills the upper slot with block
$j_\tau+2$, and increases $j_\tau$ by one. The block is read from its address if $\tau\ge1$ or if $\tau=0$ and
$0\le j_\tau+2<d$; otherwise it consists of end markers and is produced without an I/O. A \emph{left shift}, when the head
has reached $j_\tau B-1$, is symmetric: write back block $j_\tau+1$ if $\tau\ge1$, move the lower slot up, fill the lower
slot with block $j_\tau-1$, and decrease~$j_\tau$.
\item If the buffer holds $B$ bits (a \emph{flush}), it is written to address $d+n$, and $n$ increases by one.
\end{itemize}
A write sends slot~$i$ of the new contents~$\mu'$ (\cref{def:EM}), so an application of the map cannot both write a slot
and change it. A flush therefore empties the buffer by resetting the count of buffered bits in the register, and a shift
of a work tape takes two applications: the first writes back the leaving block and enters the waiting phase, and the
second moves the remaining window block to its new slot and reads the entering block. A shift performs at most two I/Os,
a shift of the input tape at most one, and a flush one.

\emph{Correctness.} The invariants are preserved: a shift writes back the block that leaves the window and fills the
block that enters it with its current contents, after which the head, which moves by at most one cell per step, lies
inside the window again; and a flush moves the buffer to the next output address. Hence the simulation follows the run
of~$M$ exactly. By transposition, $M$ appends exactly $L=dB$ bits, so there are exactly $d$ flushes, all before~$M$ halts,
and address $d+n$ receives output bits $nB,\dots,nB+B-1$, which form row~$n$ of~$X^\T$. The algorithm is correct on every
input.

\emph{Counting I/Os.} After a shift of tape~$\tau$ the head lies at offset $B$ (right shift) or $B-1$ (left shift) in the
new window, and at the start it lies at offset~$B$. In each case it is at distance at least~$B$ from both cells just
outside the window, so the next shift of that tape comes after at least $B$ moves of its head. A head moves at most $T_0$
times, so tape~$\tau$ shifts at most
\[
 \frac{c(L+1)}B=c\Bigl(d+\frac1B\Bigr)\le2cd
\]
times. The number of I/Os is therefore at most $1+d+2(k+1)\cdot2cd\le c_1d$, since $d\ge1$.

\emph{Obliviousness.} Record each event by a letter: $(\tau,+)$ or $(\tau,-)$ for a right or left shift of tape~$\tau$, and
$\phi$ for a flush. The \emph{event word} of a run is the sequence of these letters, a word over an alphabet of $2k+3$
letters, of length at most $(k+1)\cdot2cd+d=(2(k+1)c+1)d=:n_0$. The I/Os performed for an event are determined by its
letter and by the current window indices and flush count, which are determined by the earlier letters. Hence the I/O
sequence of a run is a function of its event word. There are at most $\sum_{n=0}^{\lfloor n_0\rfloor}(2k+3)^n\le(2k+4)^{n_0}$
words of length at most~$n_0$, so some event word is shared by a set $\mathcal C$ of at least
$2^L(2k+4)^{-n_0}=2^{L-c_2d}$ inputs, and the algorithm is oblivious on~$\mathcal C$.

\emph{Register.} The register holds the state of~$M$, the phase, and $2k+4$ integers: the $x_\tau$, the $j_\tau$, the
flush count and the buffer count. Each has absolute value at most $T_0+2$: $\abs{x_\tau}\le T_0$,
$\abs{j_\tau}\le\abs{x_\tau}+2$ because the head is within one cell of its window, and both counts are at most the number
of bits appended so far, hence at most~$T_0$. So each integer takes at most $2T_0+5\le(4c+5)L$ values, and since
$\log_2(2L)\ge1$, the register needs
$\bigl\lceil\log_2\bigl(\card Q\,(2T_0+5)^{2k+4}(2k+2)\bigr)\bigr\rceil\le\gamma\log_2(2L)$ bits with
$\gamma=(2k+4)\bigl(\log_2(4c+5)+1\bigr)+\log_2\card Q+\log_2(2k+2)+1$.

Formally, the algorithm is \lean{MergeDepth.EM.emAlg}: a step function that performs at most one I/O per step, collapsed
so that one application of the map follows the internal steps up to the next I/O. Its invariant relates each state to
the configuration of~$M$ at the same time. \lean{MergeDepth.EM.em_core} shows that on every input on which $M$ computes
an output of length $dB$ within $T_0$ steps, the run halts with that output at the addresses $d,\dots,2d-1$ and performs
the first read followed by the I/Os of an event word with at most $\lfloor T_0/B\rfloor$ shifts per tape and exactly $d$
flushes, and \lean{MergeDepth.EM.em_oblivious} chooses $\mathcal C$ by pigeonhole over the event words, padded to a
common length.
The proof gives more. It counts exactly: at most $1+(2k+1)\lfloor T_0/B\rfloor+d$ I/Os, since a shift of the input tape
costs at most one, and $2^L\le\card{\mathcal C}\,(2k+4)^{(k+1)\lfloor T_0/B\rfloor+d}$
(\lean{MergeDepth.EM.theoremEM_nat}). As $\lfloor T_0/B\rfloor\le c(d+1)$, this gives at most $((2k+1)c+1)(d+1)$ I/Os
and $\card{\mathcal C}\ge2^{L-(((k+1)c+1)d+(k+1)c)\log_2(2k+4)}$ (\lean{MergeDepth.EM.theoremEM_sharp}), so that for
large~$d$ the constants $c_1$ and~$c_2$ improve to about $(2k+1)c+1$ and $((k+1)c+1)\log_2(2k+4)$. Transposition is used
only through the output length: for every machine that computes outputs of length $dB$ from inputs of length $dB$ within
$T_0$ steps, and every set~$W$ of such inputs, the algorithm is oblivious on a subset $\mathcal C\subseteq W$ with
$\card W\le\card{\mathcal C}\,(2k+4)^{(k+1)\lfloor T_0/B\rfloor+d}$ (\lean{MergeDepth.EM.em_oblivious}).
\end{outline}

\Cref{thm:EM} thus places every linear-time transposer, adaptive or not, inside one concrete external-memory question:
a transposition with $O(d)$ I/Os and $O(B)$ bits of internal memory that is oblivious on a set of $2^{L-O(d)}$ inputs.
The constants $c_1,c_2$ do not depend on the alphabet, which affects only the memory size in bits.

\begin{corollary}[An external-memory route to the negation of (T)]\label{cor:EM}
\formal{MergeDepth/ExternalMemory.lean}{MergeDepth.EM.corollaryEM,MergeDepth.EM.EMLowerBound,MergeDepth.EM.corollaryEM_some}
Suppose that for every finite alphabet $\Sigma$ containing $0$, $1$ and a blank, and all constants $m\ge1$, $\gamma\ge0$
and $A\ge0$, there is $d_0$ such that for every $d\ge d_0$ the following holds at $L=d^2b(d)$ and $B=d\,b(d)$: every
external-memory algorithm over~$\Sigma$ with block size~$B$, $m$ slots and a register of at most $\gamma\log_2(2L)$ bits
that is correct and oblivious on some set of at least $2^{L-Ad}$ inputs performs more than $Ad$ I/Os on the inputs of that
set. Then (T) fails: no machine, with any number of work tapes, transposes in linear time.
\end{corollary}

\begin{outline}
Suppose that (T) holds, witnessed by a machine~$M$ with $k$ work tapes and a constant~$c$. Let $\gamma$, $c_1$ and $c_2$
be as in \cref{thm:EM}, and put $\Sigma=\Sigma_M$, $m=2k+3$ and $A=\max(c_1,c_2)$. Let $d_0$ be given by the hypothesis
for these constants, and let $d\ge\max(d_0,1)$. By \cref{thm:EM} there is an algorithm over~$\Sigma$ with block size
$d\,b(d)$, $m$ slots and a register of at most $\gamma\log_2(2L)$ bits that is correct on every input, oblivious on a set
of at least $2^{L-c_2d}\ge2^{L-Ad}$ inputs, and performs at most $c_1d\le Ad$ I/Os on each input. This contradicts the
hypothesis. Formally, the conclusion is the negation of the frozen statement of~(T), \lean{Transpose.LinearTimeTransposable},
and the hypothesis \lean{MergeDepth.EM.EMLowerBound} is needed at one dimension $d\ge1$ only, for each alphabet and choice
of constants (\lean{MergeDepth.EM.corollaryEM_some}).
\end{outline}

At block scale, then, one indivisibility-free I/O lower bound would refute~(T), with no hypothesis on adaptivity, nesting
or coding.

\begin{remark}[What the hypothesis of \cref{cor:EM} asks]\label{rem:EM-status}
The lower bounds of Aggarwal and Vitter for permuting and for matrix transposition~\cite{AggarwalVitter1988} assume that
records are indivisible: they are moved and copied but never combined. Under that assumption, transposition with a
constant number of blocks of internal memory needs more I/Os than linearly many, by a logarithmic factor; so the
algorithm that \cref{thm:EM} builds from a linear-time machine must combine records, that is, it must code. The
hypothesis of \cref{cor:EM} asks for a bound of order $\omega(d)$ without indivisibility, for algorithms that need only be
correct and oblivious on a set of $2^{L-O(d)}$ inputs, which need not be a product set. Farhadi, Hajiaghayi, Larsen and
Shi prove external-memory lower bounds for sorting without indivisibility, by network coding~\cite{FHLS2019}. They reduce
non-oblivious algorithms to a fixed I/O graph by a pigeonhole over I/O graphs, the step that produces~$\mathcal C$ in
\cref{thm:EM}. Their bounds are conditional on the conjecture that network coding gives no advantage over multicommodity
flow in undirected graphs, and that conjecture is false in general~\cite{BravermanHe2026}. We know of no unconditional
bound of the required kind. Such a bound need not deal with adaptivity: fixing the I/O sequence costs only $O(d)=o(L)$
bits of input entropy, so on~$\mathcal C$ a machine's adaptivity shapes only its motion within blocks. The reduction goes
one way only: since internal computation is free and blocks are accessed at random, an external-memory transposition with
$O(d)$ I/Os would not by itself give a linear-time machine.
\end{remark}
