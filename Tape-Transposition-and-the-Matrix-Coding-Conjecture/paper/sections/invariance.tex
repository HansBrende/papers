\section{Where one half is proved}\label{sec:invariance}

One half is known for $w\le2$ (\cref{prop:known}) and for several further classes in the series. The theorem here
adds a condition on the code, with no restriction on the helper relation beyond its forward window.

Let $\iota$ be an involution of the entries. A \emph{pairing shift} adds one group element to both entries of a
two-entry orbit of $\iota$, or adds it to a fixed entry. Different orbits may be shifted independently. For the
transpose involution these are the independent symmetric-array shifts; invariance under their sum alone is not
the hypothesis.

\begin{theorem}[Theorem I: invariance under a pairing]\label{thm:I}
\formal{LeanMachine/WholeRow/Pairing.lean}{LeanMachine.WholeRow.pairing_card_sq_le_forward, LeanMachine.WholeRow.pairing_card_pow_le_half, LeanMachine.WholeRow.pairing_card_pow_le_half_of_inactive_fixed}
\formal{LeanMachine/WholeRow/ParityTrimming.lean}{LeanMachine.WholeRow.parity_card_le_forward, LeanMachine.WholeRow.parity_card_pow_le_baseline}
Let $\Sigma$ be a finite abelian group of order $q\ge2$, $\rho$ a helper map of window $w$ on $[d]$, and $C$ a
whole-row code. Suppose $C$ is invariant under every pairing shift except possibly those on $b_0$ two-entry orbits.
For every integer $n\ge1$ with $d\ge n(w+2w^3n)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pairing-invariance}
  \card C^{2n}\ \le\ q^{\,n(d^2+2b_0)+2d^2}.
\end{equation}
Thus its rate is at most $1/2+b_0/d^2+1/n$.

More generally, the shifts on a set $F$ of fixed entries may also be withheld. Put
$u=\card{F\setminus\{(i,j):i\in\rho(j)\}}$. Then \eqref{eq:pairing-invariance} holds with $2b_0+u$ in place of
$2b_0$: only the inactive fixed entries that are nonincident are charged.

For the parity variant let $B_1,\ldots,B_m$ be disjoint sets of entries, each of size at least two. Suppose $C$ is
invariant under every shift supported on their union whose coordinate sum on each $B_a$ is zero. At the same
threshold,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:parity-invariance}
 \card C^n\le q^{\,n(d^2-m)+d^2},
\end{equation}
so its rate is at most $1-m/d^2+1/n$. These are zero-sum shifts on each block, not constant shifts.
\end{theorem}

\begin{outline}
The finite group count has two parts. Recovery forces compatibility between the subgroup of allowed shifts and
each coordinate's helper view. Suitable records then distinguish the subgroup cosets that meet the code. One
records block sums, the designated exceptional coordinates, and additional block data where an enclosure of the
helper views fails. The coset count bounds the code by the subgroup size times the number of records. This works
for arbitrary sets and nonlinear recovery functions.

For disjoint parity blocks the starting exponent is $N-m$, where $N$ is the number of coordinates. Raw values
outside the blocks can be pinned; their record cost cancels the missing kernel dimensions. After deleting rows
helping more than $t$ columns, the extra exponent is at most
\[
 \card{\text{blocks touching the recorded rows}}+
       \frac{\lambda d(w+w^2t)}2
 \ \le\ \frac{wN}{t+1}+\frac{\lambda d(w+w^2t)}2,
\]
where $\lambda$ is the lane count and $N=\lambda d^2$. Disjointness charges at most one symbol per block touching
a recorded row. Set $t=2wn$. Multiplication by $n$ makes the first term at most $N/2$, and
$d\ge n(w+2w^3n)$ makes the second at most $N/2$. This proves \eqref{eq:parity-invariance}, uniformly in the
alphabet, number of lanes, block sizes and relation.

For pairing, use a weighted difference on each two-entry orbit, so that its kernel consists of equal shifts on
the two entries; omit the $b_0$ exceptional orbits by pinning their coordinates. Every active fixed-coordinate
shift forces that coordinate to be incident: otherwise it changes a recovered value while keeping its helper rows
fixed. Reserve these original incidences before heavy-row deletion. This cancels their contribution to the orbit
count, while omitted pairs contribute two entries in the squared inequality. If shifts on $F$ are inactive, their
raw record and lost kernel dimension cancel, and only $F$ outside the original incidences remains unreserved.
The same heavy-row calculation, in the squared count, gives \eqref{eq:pairing-invariance} and its $u$ variant.
The Lean statements allow several lanes and coordinatewise additive automorphisms in the weighted sums; the
unweighted one-lane forms above are special cases.
\end{outline}

For fixed $w$, choosing $n$ of order $\sqrt{d/w^3}$ gives error $O_w(d^{-1/2})$. A positive asymptotic gain over
one half therefore requires the loss of individual pairing shifts on a positive fraction of the two-entry orbits,
or a corresponding payment for inactive nonincident fixed entries. Nothing proved here forces this invariance on
the fibres of a machine.

To read the table as a conditional exclusion of an oblivious machine class, the additional premise must hold on the
large fibres produced by the cut and row-spread construction, at its canonical alphabet and its actual window.
A one-sided deficit $\delta$ then suffices when $\delta>169\eps/64$; an extra $p(d)$-bit record adds $p(d)/L$ to the
right side. If the bound is supplied only after the two-sided intersection, its deficit must exceed
$169\eps/32+2p(d)/L$. An arbitrary small structured subcode does not suffice: its size must survive these payments.
For linear rows of the table, a scalar realization over a field must be supplied; vector linearity of the bits does
not automatically give scalar linearity over the entry alphabet. The unrestricted implication of \cref{thm:T}
needs no such additional premise, but does not extract any of these restricted classes.
