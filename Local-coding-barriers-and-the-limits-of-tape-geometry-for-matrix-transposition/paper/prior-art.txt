CONJECTURE 36: CLOSED ROUTE AND ATTRIBUTION
Checked 18 October 2026. Drafting note; not a claimed new contribution.

The conjecture is Mittal--Raz, ITCS 2021, Section 8.1, Conjecture 36
[MittalRaz2021]. It concerns the uniform random binary n-by-n matrix game:
player j sees a prescribed set of rows and must output column j. It posits
a constant c>0 giving joint success at most 2^(-c*n^2) when each player sees
c*n rows (with the appropriate integer convention).

STATUS: FALSE. Do not spend further work refuting this universal assertion.
Its failure does not decide multitape transposition complexity: information
available to an abstract player does not provide a paid tape computation.

EXPLICIT PUBLIC DISPROOF

[Mittal2025], Section 10.8.2, Proposition 10.8.3 and Lemma 10.8.5, printed
pp.158-160 (PDF pp.169-171), gives the transpose-game counterexample and
credits Mark Braverman and Katherine Forbes. The thesis bibliography lists
their 2022 personal communication, "A strategy for the matrix-transpose
game." Cite the thesis as the accessible proof and retain that attribution;
do not present the personal communication as a separately published paper.

For infinitely many sizes, players observe at most delta*n rows and succeed
with probability at least 2^(-n^(1+epsilon)); epsilon can be any sufficiently
small positive constant with delta=n^(-alpha(epsilon)), or epsilon=o(1) and
delta=o(1). These parameters are stronger than our elementary product example.

EARLIER CONSTRUCTIONS THAT IMPLY COUNTEREXAMPLES

The following three supplied papers do not explicitly refute Conjecture 36.
They provide constructions from which its failure follows. The conversion
below is an explanation of the implication, not a claim of new priority.

For a square placement delivery array (PDA), let player j observe the rows
marked by stars in column j. A non-star symbol class is an induced matching:
for any one of its entries, all other entries in the class lie in rows that
the corresponding player observes. Predict that entry by XORing the others.
All predictions are correct exactly when every class has XOR zero. If there
are r nonempty classes, their supports are disjoint, so these are r independent
binary equations. Joint success is exactly 2^(-r), or 2^(-b*r) for independent
b-bit entries. Thus o(n) observed rows per player and r=o(n^2) disprove the
fixed-positive-c bound. Extra rows may be supplied and ignored.

1. [YanEtAl2017], arXiv:1510.05064v3, Definition 1(C3), Theorems 1 and 4.
   Theorem 4 gives K=q(m+1), F=q^m, Z=q^(m-1), S=(q-1)q^m.
   Choose m=q-1, q>=3. Then K=q^2 divides F. Repeat the column pattern F/K
   times with separate symbol labels in each copy, obtaining n=F columns
   and rows. Only the observation pattern is copied, not matrix data.
   Each player observes n/q rows; r=(q-1)n^2/q^2. Both fractions tend to zero.

2. [ShanmugamEtAl2017], arXiv:1701.07115v1, Definition 4 and Theorems 1-2.
   The induced-matching XOR scheme and imported Alon--Moitra--Sudakov dense
   graph construction give another route. Use a minimum-degree pruning
   argument before applying the scheme, rather than relying on v1 Lemma 1.
   If at most delta*K^2 unordered pairs are missing, delete vertices with
   complement degree >sqrt(delta)*K. At most 2*sqrt(delta)*K vertices are
   deleted. The remaining graph has K'=(1-o(1))K vertices and minimum degree
   K'-o(K'); induced matchings restrict and their count cannot increase.
   Theorem 2, with fixed sufficiently large C, supplies delta=o(1) and
   t=K^(1+epsilon+o(1)), epsilon<1, hence t=o(K'^2).

   Source caveat: the proof of v1 Lemma 1 uses the global mean of squared
   distance for a fixed vertex, although the conditional mean can differ.
   For C=5, x=(1,...,1) in dimension h, the global mean is 4h but the
   conditional mean is 6h. Adjacency requires distance <5h; Hoeffding gives
   degree/K <=exp(-h/128), incompatible with the claimed lower bound
   1-2*exp(-h/1250) for large even h. This concerns the supplied version and
   does not invalidate the preceding density-plus-pruning route.

3. [ChittoorEtAl2021], arXiv:2001.00399v2, Lemmas 10-11, Theorem 3, and
   Corollary 2 (Scheme C), Section V-E, pp.24-25.
   Scheme C is square: F=K, cached fraction a<=lambda, matching size
   gamma=binom(2h,h), h=lambda*q. Its r=K^2(1-a)/gamma classes partition
   all missing entries. Choose q=2^(2j), h=2^j, lambda=2^(-j), j>=1.
   Then a=o(1), gamma tends to infinity, and r=o(K^2). The finite field
   labels the construction; the payload may still consist of binary bits.

SUGGESTED MANUSCRIPT WORDING

The failure of the transpose prediction conjecture is explicitly recorded
in Mittal's dissertation, which credits Braverman and Forbes. Counterexamples
also follow from earlier coded-caching and PDA constructions of Yan et al.,
Shanmugam et al., and Chittoor et al., by restricting their XOR syndromes to
zero, with square conversion or minimum-degree pruning where necessary.
We recall this limitation of the lower-bound approach and claim no new
refutation. The elementary example in our research archive is explanatory
background, not a principal contribution.

This list audits the supplied sources; it is not an exhaustive priority claim.
The older research archive retains the detailed derivations and source checks.
