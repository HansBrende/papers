\begin{abstract}
We prove that linear-time transposition of dyadic bit squares implies
linear-time transposition of canonical square matrices with logarithmic-width
entries on deterministic multitape Turing machines. One fixed wrapper
recovers all dimensions and parameters from raw input, and the assumed bit
machine may have an adaptive schedule and nonlinear workspace encodings.
We also prove barriers for restricted compilers in the reverse direction.
A multiscale bound limits the transpose coordinates covered by a bounded
family of bounded-length paths through public interval permutations,
whole-entry operations, rotations, reversals and parity rearrangements.
A second theorem allows finitely many synchronous nonlinear scans over a
fixed number of banks, with bounded persistent state. One common helper
records exceptional values and boundary states; acyclic decoding yields
a finite inequality excluding all sufficiently large dyadic transposes in
this grammar. The unrestricted converse and unrestricted linear-time
transposition remain open. A constructive extension computes an equivariant
cyclic phase and the least period of every column in linear time. Computing
the entire matrix normalized by this particular phase is equivalent to
canonical linear-time word transposition. The reduction and compiler barriers
have Lean proofs in a shared framework; the phase and normalizer results
have complete written proofs and are not formalized.
\end{abstract}
