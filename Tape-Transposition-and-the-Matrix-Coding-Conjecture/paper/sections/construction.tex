\section{The construction side}\label{sec:construction}

What would a counterexample to the conjecture, and what would a linear-time transposer, have to look like?

\subsection{What a code of rate near one must avoid}\label{sec:constr-code}

Suppose that whole-row codes of rate $1-\eps$ exist at a window that depends on $\eps$ only, for all large~$d$.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item By \cref{thm:N} there are such codes, of rate $1-2\eps$, that are two-sided on symmetric reflexive relations.
\item By \cref{prop:known} and the classes of \cref{sec:invariance} the window is at least three and the rows and
offsets of the relation are spread.
\item By \cref{thm:I}, for every involution of the entries the codes fail to be invariant under many of its pairing
shifts: if the shifts of all fixed entries are kept, rate $1-\eps$ forces the number $b$ of orbits of two entries
without invariance to be at least $(\tfrac12-\eps-o(1))d^2$, and an involution has at most $d^2/2$ such orbits, so
invariance survives on a fraction of at most about $2\eps$ of them. Fixed entries that lose their shifts are charged
separately, by the term $u$ of the theorem.
\item The examples above the pairing rate that are known at small sizes, such as the Fano code of \cref{thm:E}, gain
from the incident entries and from short cycles of helpers. At a fixed window the incident entries are a fraction
$O(w/d)$ of all entries, so a gain of positive density as $d$ grows cannot come from them alone.
\item \Cref{thm:O} gives no information on mixing relations. \Cref{thm:E} shows that this is a limit of the method
and not evidence for such codes: entropy inequalities can hold where acyclic bounds do not.
\end{itemize}
The thirteenth paper constructs storage codes above half rate on abstract relations of bounded degree, and the
fifteenth amplifies them to rate tending to one~\cite{StorageCodes2026,HighRate2026}. In a whole-row relation an entry has $wd$ helpers, which fill $w$ rows,
and we do not know whether those codes can be laid out in this way (\cref{prob:highrate}).
Along an unbounded sequence of sizes, whole-row codes of rate tending to one do exist when the window may grow like
$d^{1-\alpha}$ (\cref{rem:growing}).
They are not the codes asked for above, whose window depends on $\eps$ only. They show that a proof of the conjecture
cannot be indifferent to the size of the window (\cref{prob:growing}).

The codes of \cref{rem:growing} have one form, and at a fixed window that form has asymptotically no greater rate
than the symmetric arrays. Call a set $M$ of non-incident entries an \emph{induced matching} for~$\rho$ if any two of its entries
$(i,j)\ne(i',j')$ satisfy $i\in\rho(j')$ and $i'\in\rho(j)$. Its entries then lie in distinct rows and in distinct
columns. Let $\mathcal M$ be a partition of the set of non-incident entries into induced matchings and let $A$ be a
finite abelian group. Let $C(\mathcal M)$ be the set of arrays over $A$ whose entries sum to zero over every
$M\in\mathcal M$. It is a whole-row code for~$\rho$: an entry $(i,j)$ of $M$ is minus the sum of the other entries
of~$M$, and these lie in rows of~$\rho(j)$. For $\rho(j)=\{j\}$ and the matchings $\{(i,j),(j,i)\}$ this is, over
$\F_2$, the code of symmetric arrays. The codes of Braverman and Forbes are of this form over $\F_2$, with the induced
matchings of a Ruzsa--Szemer\'edi graph, and so are the delivery schemes of a placement delivery array with as many
packets as users~\cite{YanEtAl2017}.

\begin{proposition}[Parities over induced matchings]\label{prop:matchings}
\formal{InducedMatchings.lean}{InducedMatchingCodes.isWholeRowCode, InducedMatchingCodes.cardinality, InducedMatchingCodes.large_cells, InducedMatchingCodes.cardinality_bound, InducedMatchingCodes.cardinality_bound_balanced, InducedMatchingCodes.cardinality_pow_le_half}
Let $\rho$ have window $w$ on $[d]$ and let $\mathcal M$ be a partition of its non-incident entries into induced
matchings. Then at most $(w^2+\tfrac32w)\,d^{3/2}$ entries lie in matchings with three or more entries, and
\[
 \card{C(\mathcal M)}\le\card A^{\,d^2/2+wd/2+(w^2/2+3w/4)\,d^{3/2}} .
\]
Balancing the counting charges gives the stronger growing-window bound
\[
 \card{C(\mathcal M)}\le\card A^{\,d^2/2+wd/2+(5/4)(wd)^{3/2}} .
\]
In particular these codes have rate at most $\tfrac12+o(1)$ whenever $w=o(d^{1/3})$, including every fixed window.
An explicit finite form is
\[
 n w+25w^3n^2\le d
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
 \card{C(\mathcal M)}^{2n}\le\card A^{(n+2)d^2}\qquad(n\in\N).
\]
All these bounds hold with arbitrary prescribed sums on the matchings in place of zero.
\end{proposition}
\begin{outline}
The shared proof also covers rectangular arrays and retains an arbitrary cutoff $x>0$. Let $\ell(i)$ be the number of columns helped by row $i$, and call a row heavy if
$\ell(i)>x$. Since $\sum_i\ell(i)\le wd$, the heavy rows contain at most $wd^2/x$ entries.

Let $e_1=(a_1,b_1)$ lie in a matching with at least three entries. If the matching has another entry
$e_2=(a_2,b_2)$ in a light row, choose a third entry $e_3=(a_3,b_3)$. Mutuality gives the incidence path
$b_1,a_2,b_3,a_1$. Its endpoint determines $e_1$, and there are at most $d\cdot w\cdot x\cdot w$ such paths.
No choice of matching has to be recovered from the path. In a matching with at least two light entries, every
entry has such a path. In any remaining matching of size at least three, there is at most one light entry and
at least two heavy entries, so the total size is at most three halves of the number of heavy entries.
Disjointness lets these charges be summed. Thus, if $B$ counts the entries in matchings of size at least three,
\[
 B\le w^2dx+\frac{3wd^2}{2x}.
\]
Taking $x=\sqrt d$ gives the first bound on $B$. Taking $x=\sqrt{d/w}$ gives
$B\le\tfrac52(wd)^{3/2}$; zero sizes or zero window are handled separately.

There are at least $d^2-wd-B$ entries in matchings of size at most two. Consequently
$2\card{\mathcal M}\ge d^2-wd-B$. Disjoint nonempty sums are independent equations over every finite abelian
group, even with prescribed nonzero right-hand sides, so
$\card{C(\mathcal M)}=\card A^{d^2-\card{\mathcal M}}$.
The finite threshold follows by squaring the square-root term; it is uniform in the alphabet and in $\rho$.
\end{outline}

The window dependence is essential: the balanced bound becomes vacuous when $w$ is of order $d^{1/3}$.
For matchings that all have the same size $r\ge3$, Alon, Moitra and Sudakov give the additional dependence on $r$:
in the present terms $wd\ge\Omega(r^{2/3}d^{4/3})$~\cite[Theorem~3.9]{AMS2013}.
Their proof is sketched there and also counts paths of length three among the incident pairs.
\Cref{prop:matchings} permits mixed matching sizes, but requires a partition: it makes no assertion about
arbitrary overlapping checks, equations without this mutual form, or nonlinear codes. Those remain part of the conjecture.

\subsection{What the conjecture forgets about machines}\label{sec:constr-forgets}

For oblivious machines the ladder starts with a same-table tableau family with a common motion schedule,
equivalent to an oblivious linear-time transposer (\lean{SameTable.uniformTableaux_iff}). It then passes to the
values on a cut with the few records that each output can reach; an index code for all inputs on the cone system;
and the whole-row codes on its fibres. The reduction of \cref{sec:rows} never uses that all steps apply the same
rule. The tableau equivalence covers only oblivious families; the adaptive exclusion uses the block-code
reduction of \cref{thm:T}.

\begin{proposition}[Runs that a cheap cut splits]\label{prop:hyperfinite}
\notformalized
Let an oblivious machine with $k\ge1$ work tapes run in time $c(L+1)$ with steps of $s$ bits. Suppose that for some
$\theta$ with $s(k+2)c\,\theta<\tfrac12$ and all large $d$ there is a set of at most $\theta T$ steps whose removal
leaves components of the dependency graph of \cref{sec:rows-cones}, edges taken without direction, with
$o(\log\log d)$ output steps each.
Then the machine does not transpose.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Fibres are nearly Markov]\label{rem:markov}
Proof sketch only: this remark is not used elsewhere and has not been independently reviewed. In the setting of
\cref{thm:T}, let $z$ record the exact messages on the edges out of the cut, the source bits demanded by outputs
rooted at cut slots, and the exceptional input columns. Let $G_z$ be the set of all inputs with these prescribed
values. Fixing the outgoing cut messages lets each cut slot be evaluated from the earlier prescribed messages
and the raw rows in its remaining backward cone. Thus $G_z$ is defined by bounded-row consistency conditions,
together with the recorded raw-bit constraints. The latter split into conditions on individual rows, including
those fixing exceptional columns. The uniform distribution on a nonempty $G_z$ therefore factorizes over these
row constraints and has the Markov property of their hypergraph. The factors may depend on~$z$.

Let $F$ be the solving set of co-entropy at most~$a$. Every $C_z=F\cap G_z$ is now a whole-row code: the cut-root
targets and the exceptional columns are fixed, and the remaining columns have the recovery maps of \cref{thm:T}.
Use exact messages, whose capacities hold on $F$, rather than the unrestricted residue fibres of the formal
observation map. The admissible $G_z$ are disjoint and cover $F$, though they need not cover the full input cube.
If there are at most $2^B$ admissible complete transcripts, some $z$ satisfies
\[
 \card{C_z}\ge 2^{L-a-B-2},\qquad
 D\bigl(\mathrm{Unif}(C_z)\,\big\|\,\mathrm{Unif}(G_z)\bigr)\le a+2\quad\text{bits}.
\]
Indeed the transcripts with $\card{C_z}<\card F/(4\cdot2^B)$ contain fewer than $\card F/4$ inputs of $F$;
those with $\card{C_z}<2^{-a}\card{G_z}/4$ contain at most $\card F/4$, since
$\sum_z\card{G_z}\le2^L$. Choose a fibre outside both classes and use
$D(\mathrm{Unif}(C_z)\|\mathrm{Unif}(G_z))=\log_2(\card{G_z}/\card{C_z})$.

On this same fibre, if a set $S$ of rows separates two sets of rows in the constraint hypergraph, their mutual
information given the rows $S$ is at most $a+2$: marginalize the relative entropy and use conditional independence
under $\mathrm{Unif}(G_z)$. Anything that is a function of each of the two sets then has conditional entropy at
most $a+2$ given~$S$. An entry is a function of its own row and of the helper rows of its column, so this applies
when those bundles are separated by~$S$. The bound is available for every separator on the selected fibre, but
we do not know how to combine separators without paying it repeatedly.
\end{remark}

\subsection{What a machine would need}\label{sec:constr-machine}

By the proof of \cref{thm:T}, read in the other direction, a linear-time transposer yields for every $\eps>0$ whole-row codes of
rate at least $1-\eps$ at a window that depends on $\eps$ and on the resources of the machine only, for all large~$d$;
for an oblivious machine \cref{thm:W} produces them directly from a cut of its run. So a construction of a machine
contains a refutation of \cref{conj:MCC}, with everything listed in \cref{sec:constr-code}. No converse is known:
such codes are sets, and a machine must also produce them by passes over tapes in linear time, with head
motion that meets the conditions of the earlier papers~\cite[Corollary~3.24]{FourthPaper2026}. This is why a
refutation of the conjecture would close this route to an impossibility proof without deciding~(T).
