\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

We use the definitions of the eighth paper of the series~\cite[Section~2]{BoundedNesting2026} and recall only what the
statements below need.

\paragraph{Machines and (T).} A machine is a deterministic Turing machine with a finite state set, a read-only two-way
input tape, $k\ge1$ two-way work tapes over a finite alphabet, and a write-only one-way output tape that receives at most
one bit per step; all heads start at cell~$0$ and move by $-1$, $0$ or~$+1$~\cite[Section~2.1]{BoundedNesting2026}. For
$d\ge1$ let $b(d)=\max(1,\lceil\log_2d\rceil)$ and $L=d^2b(d)$. The input is a $d\times d$ matrix with $b(d)$-bit entries
in row-major order, and the machine must write its transpose; on input~$w$, the output bit at position~$\ell$ is
$w_{\tpos(\ell)}$. Statement~(T) says that some machine does this within $c(L+1)$ steps, for a constant~$c$ and
every~$d$; its formal version is fixed in a frozen file of the first paper's library~\cite{FirstPaper2026}. A machine is
\emph{oblivious} at a length if its head positions, append times and halting time do not depend on the input of that
length. The \emph{trace} of a run is the sequence of all head moves together with the append times and the halting time;
for a uniformly random input~$X$, $\tau(X)$ is a random trace. The \emph{value} $v_t$ of step~$t$ consists of the state
after the step, the symbols written and the output bit or its absence; $\beta(M)$ bits suffice to encode it.

\paragraph{Walks, hops and entries.} A \emph{walk} of length~$T$ is a map $x\colon[0,T)\to\Z$ with
$\abs{x(t+1)-x(t)}\le1$. For a head~$h$ and a time~$u$, $\prev_h(u)$ is the last earlier time at which head~$h$ was at
the cell $x_h(u)$; the pair $(\prev_h(u),u)$ is a \emph{hop}, \emph{long} if $u-\prev_h(u)\ge2$. An \emph{entry} of a
cell~$c$ by a walk~$x$ is a time~$t$ with $x(t)=c$ and either $t=0$ or $x(t-1)\ne c$~\cite[Definition~2.1]{BoundedNesting2026}.
\emph{Owner-load} is defined in~\cite[Definition~2.2]{BoundedNesting2026}; if every cell is entered at most $V$ times,
the long hops have owner-load at most $2V-1$~\cite[Corollary~3.2]{BoundedNesting2026}. The \emph{walk DAG} of walks
$x_1,\dots,x_k$ has vertex set $[0,T)$, \emph{state edges} $t\to t-1$ and \emph{hop edges} $u\to p$ for the long hops
$(p,u)$; a \emph{cut} is a set of edges, of \emph{depth at most~$D$} if every path avoiding it has at most $D$ hop
edges~\cite[Section~2.3]{BoundedNesting2026}.

\paragraph{Theorem F.} For walks $x_1,\dots,x_k$ of length~$T$, coupled arbitrarily, and a set of their long hops of
owner-load at most~$\ell$, the corresponding sub-DAG of the walk DAG has a cut of at most $\eps T$ edges and depth less
than a number $\DF(k,\ell,\eps)=2^{O(k^3\ell^3/\eps)}$ that does not depend on~$T$, on the coupling or on the scales of
the walks~\cite[Theorem~3.6]{BoundedNesting2026}. Theorem~F is Lean-verified there; it is the only depth-reduction input
of this paper.

\paragraph{Dependency DAGs, genies and the crux.} For an oblivious machine~$M$ at length~$L$, or for one trace~$\tau$ of
any machine, the \emph{dependency DAG} $H_M$ or $H_\tau$ has vertex set $[0,T)$, state edges $t\to t-1$ and cell edges
$t\to\prev_j(t)$ along the work-head walks; the input head adds no edges~\cite[Section~2.4]{BoundedNesting2026}. A
\emph{genie} is a set $\Gamma\subseteq[0,T)$ of revealed steps, of cost $\beta\card\Gamma$, and it has \emph{full depth
at most~$D$} if every $\Gamma$-free path has at most $D$ edges. A cut of the walk DAG of small size and bounded depth
yields a genie of small cost and bounded full depth~\cite[Lemma~2.6]{BoundedNesting2026}. By the cone lemma of the fourth
paper~\cite[Lemma~4.4 and Corollary~4.5]{FourthPaper2026}, output bit~$\ell$ is then a function of the revealed word and
of the input bits at a set $I_\Gamma(\ell)$ of at most $(k+1)^{D+1}$ positions; every fibre of the revealed word is a
storage code for the \emph{cone relation} $N_\Gamma(q)=I_\Gamma(\tpos(q))$~\cite[Remark~4.9(b)]{FourthPaper2026}.

\paragraph{Question 9.1 and the linear crux.} \emph{Directed Recovery Structures in Tape Computations} shows that the cone
relations of every linear-time schedule, and of every single frozen trace, satisfy the paired-distance condition after a
paid deletion of few coordinates~\cite[Theorem~3.3, Corollary~3.8 and Remark~3.9]{DirectedRecovery2026}, and asks whether
every code on such relations of bounded incidence degree has rate at most $\frac12+o(1)$, for every
alphabet~\cite[Question~9.1]{DirectedRecovery2026}. We write \emph{[Q9.1]} for an affirmative answer to that question for
every degree bound; in Lean it is \lean{MergeDepth.Q91}, and its binary form \lean{MergeDepth.Q91Binary}, which follows
from it (\lean{MergeDepth.Q91.binary}), is all that the formal proofs below use. By~\cite[Proposition~9.2]{DirectedRecovery2026}, [Q9.1] bounds by $2^{(1/2+o(1))L}$ every code on the
cone relations of a linear-time schedule with a genie of bounded full depth; this is how [Q9.1] enters below, as in the
eighth paper's Corollaries~F$'$ and~F$''$~\cite[Corollaries~6.3 and~6.4]{BoundedNesting2026}. For machines whose step
values are vectors over a finite field~$\F$ and whose updates are $\F$-linear, the eighth paper states the \emph{linear
crux} $\LinCrux_\F$ as relative rigidity of the transposition matrix with respect to the ball pattern of the
schedule~\cite[Definition~7.4 and Theorem~7.5]{BoundedNesting2026}; we write \emph{[LinCrux]} for it.

\paragraph{Admissible geometries.} The source-cover paper~\cite[Definition~2.2]{SourceCovers2026} models one fixed
computation geometry, a set of inputs on which it is correct, records of bounded capacity determined by the actual
parents, a span partition of its return families, a core and a bounded number of exceptional families, as an
\emph{admissible geometry}; \cref{sec:reductions} uses its exclusions of geometries with few large return
families~\cite[Corollaries~4.3 and~4.5]{SourceCovers2026}.

\paragraph{Dependencies.} Results that assume [Q9.1] or [LinCrux] say so in their statements. Theorem~F is used through
its Lean-verified statement; the cone lemma, the paired-distance theorem and Proposition~9.2 of the fifth paper are used
through their written proofs there.
