\section{Cyclic recovery with two internal inputs}\label{sec:cyclic-cores}

A different all-code bound allows arbitrarily many one-way dependencies
between strongly connected components. Its restriction is on the number
of inputs participating in each component's cycles. We first prove the
bound, then show that reducing general cheap native cones to this class
can require a linear coordinate payment.

Orient a recovery arc from its supplier to its receiver: $p\to q$ means
$p\in N(q)$. A \emph{valid polymatroid} is a normalized, monotone,
submodular function $h:2^V\to\R$ with
$h(N(q)\cup\{q\})=h(N(q))$ for every $q$.
The entropy of any law on a storage code is one example.

\begin{theorem}[Two-input cyclic components]\label{thm:cc-two-input}
\notformalized
Let $D$ be a finite loopless recovery digraph. Suppose that every vertex
has at most two parents inside its own strongly connected component.
Let $\mathcal T(D)$ be the set of components that are bidirected triangles.
Every valid polymatroid satisfies
\[
 2h(V)\le\sum_{v\in V}h(\{v\})+
                 \sum_{S\in\mathcal T(D)}\min_{v\in S}h(\{v\}).
\]
Consequently every nonempty storage code over an alphabet of size $a\ge2$
satisfies, with $N=|V|$ and $t=|\mathcal T(D)|$,
\[
 \log_a|\mathcal C|\le\frac N2+\frac t2
                         \le\frac N2+\frac{b_E(3)}6.
\]
There is no restriction on external parents, outdegree, or recovery functions.
\end{theorem}
\begin{informalproof}
First each strongly connected component $S$ is either a union of two
induced acyclic sets, or a bidirected odd cycle. To see this, take an
inclusion-minimal subset $B\subseteq S$ that cannot be colored with two
acyclic colors, if one exists. Every vertex of $B$ has at least two
parents and two children in $B$: otherwise extend a coloring of its
deletion using a color absent from its parents, or from its children.
The indegree cap makes every indegree exactly two. Equality of total
indegree and outdegree then makes every outdegree exactly two as well.
No arc can enter $B$ from $S\setminus B$, since its target already has
two internal parents. Strong connectivity forces $B=S$.
The directed Brooks theorem, in the form of
\cite[Theorem~1.2 and Section~1.1]{AboulkerAubian}, now makes $S$ a
bidirected odd cycle. This application uses maximum in- and outdegree
two only after the minimal-subset argument; a global outdegree cap was
not assumed.

Order the components $S_i$ topologically, let $P_i$ be their preceding
union, and put $h_i(A)=h(P_i\cup A)-h(P_i)$ on subsets of $S_i$.
Contraction preserves the polymatroid inequalities. Every external
parent lies in $P_i$, so diminishing returns preserves validity for
the internal recovery relation.
If $S_i=A\sqcup B$ has two acyclic color classes, knowledge of $A$
recovers $B$ in topological order, and conversely. Thus
$h_i(S_i)=h_i(A)=h_i(B)$ and
$2h_i(S_i)\le\sum_{v\in S_i}h_i(\{v\})$.
For a bidirected odd cycle of length at least five, the same inequality
is the weighted good-cycle bound of~\cite[Proposition ``Further good targets'']{BrendeCurved}.
One may also pull back the pentagon bound along a closed walk consisting
of one pentagon traversal and any number of two-edge backtracks.
For a triangle, order the three singleton values as $x\le y\le z$.
Any two coordinates determine the third, so
$2h_i(S_i)\le2(x+y)\le x+y+z+x$.

Sum these inequalities. The left-hand increments telescope to $2h(V)$,
and $h_i(\{v\})\le h(\{v\})$ also bounds each exceptional minimum.
This proves the polymatroid assertion. Apply it to a uniform codeword
and use $h(\{v\})\le\log_2 a$ for the first cardinality bound.
In a bidirected triangle on $u,v,w$, the path
$L_u,R_v,L_w,R_u$ has three edges. All three coordinates are counted
by $b_E(3)$, and different triangle components are disjoint. Hence
$3t\le b_E(3)$.
\end{informalproof}

Reciprocal pairs attain half rate. Bidirected triangles attain the
triangle correction over any finite field, using a parity equation.
A directed three-cycle is not an exceptional component.
The graph-coloring input and the odd-cycle certificates are established
results; the conclusion here is their application to conditional recovery.

\begin{corollary}[Paid cyclic cleanup]\label{cor:cc-cleanup}
\notformalized
For any recovery relation $D$, let $U\subseteq V$ be such that $D[V\setminus U]$
is loopless and satisfies the internal-indegree hypothesis of
\cref{thm:cc-two-input}. If $t_U$ is its number of bidirected-triangle
components, then every nonempty storage code satisfies
\[
 \log_a|\mathcal C|\le\frac{N+|U|+t_U}{2}.
\]
For actual full-depth-$D_0$ native cones with $T\le c(L+1)$ and $k$ work
heads, put $B=\sum_{h=0}^{D_0}(k+1)^h$ and
$A_3=12c(B+1)(k+2)(k+6)+3$. Uniformly over eligible $U$,
\[
 t_U\le A_3\frac L{\log_2 d},\qquad
 \log_a|\mathcal C|\le\frac{L+|U|}{2}
                      +\frac{A_3L}{2\log_2 d}.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{informalproof}
Fix the actual values on $U$. Each remaining fibre is a code on the
induced relation, with the omitted parents replaced by constants.
\Cref{thm:cc-two-input} bounds each fibre by
$a^{(N-|U|+t_U)/2}$, and at most $a^{|U|}$ fibres occur.
For the native bound choose one three-edge paired path from each
surviving triangle component. They are vertex-disjoint in the original
incidence graph. Apply \cref{lem:nb-disjoint} with $s=3$.
This does not require bounded left degree or a bound on all short
paired paths of the untrimmed relation.
\end{informalproof}

Write $\tau_2(D)$ for the minimum eligible $|U|$. For native cones a
rate of at least $1/2+\varepsilon$ requires
$\tau_2(D)\ge2\varepsilon L-A_3L/\log_2d$.
Thus sublinear cyclic cleanup would suffice for the half bound.
The next result shows why that premise needs a separate argument.

\begin{proposition}[A linear native cleanup cost]\label{prop:cc-native-obstruction}
\notformalized
There is one fixed oblivious $O(L)$-time native machine, with home-based
work heads, that appends exactly
\[
 Y(r,t,s)=\sum_{c=0}^2 X(r+c,t-c,s)\pmod2
\]
in physical row-major order for every canonical $d\ge3$; indices are
reduced modulo $d$. For every whole-step genie $\Gamma$, its actual
transpose-demand relation $D_\Gamma$ satisfies
\[
 4\tau_2(D_\Gamma)\ge L-3db-6|\Gamma|.
\]
If its fixed whole-step value width is $\beta$, then for every fixed
$0<\varepsilon\le\min(1,\beta/12)$ there are genies of cost at most
$\varepsilon L$ and depth bounded independently of $d$, yet every genie
at that cost satisfies
\[
 \tau_2(D_\Gamma)\ge L/8-3db/4.
\]
The machine is not claimed to transpose.
\end{proposition}
\begin{informalproof}
Use three separate payload banks $A_c$, each with $L$ cells, an origin
flag, and an end flag. Reuse the paid length counter, home-based scratch
interpreter, and binary-to-unary guide construction in the proof of
\cref{prop:ne-native-shear}. Prepare reusable guides for $W=db$, $cb$,
and $W-cb$, for $c=0,1,2$. Each guided action moves the raw head or
copies one raw bit to the current bank cell per tick, then returns the
guide to its origin while the payload heads wait. Zero-length guides
perform no action. All transitions preserve the flags and the other
scanned payload cells.

To fill bank $c$, process raw rows in the order
$c,c+1,\ldots,d-1,0,\ldots,c-1$. For each row, with $a=cb$ and
$q=W-a$, perform five guided actions: move raw right $q$; copy $a$
bits; move raw left $W$; copy $q$ bits; move raw right $a$.
This writes the last $a$ bits followed by the first $q$ bits and ends
at the next raw row and bank row. Initial offsets cost at most $2W$.
At the raw right blank, wrap to the left blank and advance to zero,
paying $L+O(1)$; after filling the bank, reset raw and rewind the
bank completely. Then
$A_c(r,t,s)=X(r+c,t-c,s)$.
Finally advance all three banks together and append their three-bit
XOR in one fixed transition per position.

The initial length count and raw rewind cost $O(L)$. Shape recognition
and guide preparation cost $\operatorname{polylog}L+O(W)$; the $3d$
row routines, wraps, bank rewinds and appends cost $O(L)$.
Public row control costs $O(d\operatorname{polylog}L)=O(L)$ on the
canonical widths. There is no arithmetic charged only implicitly per
payload bit. Noncanonical lengths use the uniform fallback of the
same controller. Bank heads make full outward scans and returns,
with waits; guide and scratch heads make origin excursions. Hence
the physical work walks are home-based and the schedule is oblivious.

For each bank cell, list every visit from its raw-bit load through
its final XOR append, including parked rewrites and the rewind.
Read backwards, this list is a literal path of previous-visit edges
from the append to the raw read. One path is chosen per bank cell.
A step scans one cell per bank, so it belongs to at most three chosen
paths. Revealing $\Gamma$ destroys at most $3|\Gamma|$ of them.
Every surviving path is an incidence in the full actual cone relation;
no auxiliary dependency is pruned.

Under transpose-demand labels the three suppliers of $v=(i,j,s)$ are
$F_c(v)=(j+c,i-c,s)$. Each $F_c$ is an involution, and the three values
are distinct for $d\ge3$. Their fixed-coordinate sets are disjoint and
total $3db$ vertices. Form the undirected graph with nonfixed edges
$\{v,F_c(v)\}$. It has maximum degree three and degree exactly three
outside those fixed-coordinate sets. Each edge has two designated
directed paths. At most $3|\Gamma|$ edges lose either path, so the
set $Z$ of fixed coordinates and damaged-edge endpoints has
$|Z|\le3db+6|\Gamma|$.

If an eligible deletion $U$ leaves a vertex outside both $Z$ and the
closed neighborhood of $U$ in this degree-three graph, it leaves
that vertex and all three reciprocal neighbors. These four vertices
belong to one strongly connected component of the actual induced
relation, contradicting internal indegree at most two.
Thus $V\setminus Z$ lies in that closed neighborhood, of size at most
$4|U|$, proving the finite bound. Extra cone arcs cannot invalidate
this argument. Finally apply \cref{lem:ne-depth-import} to obtain
cheap fixed-depth genies. For any genie with
$\beta|\Gamma|\le\varepsilon L$ and $\varepsilon\le\beta/12$,
the finite bound gives the stated $L/8-3db/4$ estimate.
\end{informalproof}

The two results have complementary roles: few cyclic inputs give a
nonlinear coding bound, but a cheap native trace need not admit cheap
reduction to that class. A general lower-bound argument must handle
more internal inputs or exploit further information, such as transpose
correctness. This example supplies neither an above-half code nor a
counterexample to the general paired-distance coding question.
