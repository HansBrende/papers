\section{Adaptive packets and the cost of local recovery}\label{sec:adaptive-coded-bank}

The fixed-packet bounds of \cref{sec:local-coding} do not cover supports chosen
from earlier answers. We now allow that adaptation for nonlinear coded banks,
under a forest condition on each completed coordinate graph. The inverse
condition also changes: a short state must recover each consecutive interval
of original rows from the stored rows in the same interval. The resulting
bound excludes a fixed fractional saving in the number of public answers.
A complementary construction shows why inexpensive encoding alone cannot
supply this interval-recovery condition.

\subsection{The bank and the public query interface}

Fix $d,b\ge1$, write $W=db$ and $P=dW$, and index both raw and stored bits
by $(r,c,t)\in[d]\times[d]\times[b]$. A bank is a bijection $\Phi$ of the
\emph{full} $P$-bit cube; write $Y=\Phi(X)$. For a fixed integer $\sigma\ge0$,
put
\[
 N_\sigma(c)=[c-\sigma,c+\sigma]\cap[d].
\]
Each stored bit $(r,c,t)$ may depend on all raw bits in the columns
$N_\sigma(c)$, but on no other raw columns. For every nonempty consecutive
row interval $I$, there is a fixed finite-valued state $S_I(X)$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-bank-recovery}
 Y_I=Y'_I\ \text{and}\ S_I(X)=S_I(X')
 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad X_I=X'_I
\end{equation}
on the full cube. Assume $|\range S_I|\le2^s$ for a common real $s\ge0$.
These states are records used in the proof; no algorithm for evaluating them
is supplied for free.
\formal{GapBank}{TransposeFollowup.GapBank}
\formal{GapBankStateRange}{TransposeFollowup.GapBank.StateRangeBudget,TransposeFollowup.GapBank.restrictStates_budget_iff}

A public strategy chooses its next Boolean query, its declared support of
at most two stored coordinates, or a halt instruction from the preceding
public answers. Each query answer costs one bit, including constant answers,
repeated queries and unused declared arguments. The strategy must terminate
on a declared correct-input family $F$; it need not terminate elsewhere.
Receiver $j$ recovers the whole raw column $j$ from its original raw row
$X_j$ and the completed transcript. A deterministic public helper $Z=Z(X)$
may select the bank, all its states, the strategy and the decoders, with
common parameters $d,b,\sigma,s$.
\formal{AdaptiveBankStrategy}{TransposeFollowup.HelperGapBankStrategy,TransposeFollowup.HelperGapBankStrategy.TerminatesOn,TransposeFollowup.HelperGapBankStrategy.CorrectOn}

For each actual helper value and completed correct transcript, form the simple
support graph on \emph{all stored coordinates}, adding an edge for each distinct
two-coordinate support. Require that graph to be a forest. Its projection to
stored-row labels must lie in a simple row graph of maximum degree at most
$\Delta$. Within-row coordinate edges need no projected row edge. Both graphs
may depend on the completed transcript; their union over different transcripts
need not be a forest. Coordinate components may contain arbitrarily many bits
and rows.

\begin{theorem}[Adaptive forest bound]\label{thm:adaptive-coded-forest}
Under this interface, let $X$ have any probability law supported on $F$,
let $\ell(X)$ be the number of original public answers, and put
$D_{\mathrm{tot}}=P-H(X)+H(Z)$. Set $\delta=\max(2,\Delta)$. For any integers
$R\ge1$ and $1\le M\le d$, define
\[
 K=\delta^R,\quad \nu=\delta^{2R},\quad L=\ceil{d/M},\quad
 Q=\sum_{j=1}^{\min(K,M)}\binom Mj,\quad
 C=(K+1)Q,\quad N=W\nu(L+2\sigma).
\]
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:adaptive-coded-forest}
 \left(1+\frac1R\right)\E\ell
 \ \ge\ P-N-Cs-QD_{\mathrm{tot}}.
\end{equation}
Without a helper, take $H(Z)=0$. Correctness on $F$ includes its
zero-probability inputs.
\end{theorem}
\formal{AdaptiveForestRangeBound}{TransposeFollowup.HelperGapBankStrategy.forest_expected_length_of_state_range}
\formal{AdaptiveForestLaw}{TransposeFollowup.AdaptiveForestLaw.budget}

This is a query bound, not a native-time lower bound. Its hypotheses permit
nonlinear banks, correlated inputs and large coordinate components. They impose
forward column locality, interval recovery and leafwise acyclicity. The older
inverse-support theorem permits fixed cyclic packet graphs and arbitrary
forward dependencies; neither set of hypotheses subsumes the other.

\begin{proofoutline}
Truncate the public strategies at the maximum correct transcript length,
preserving the actual correct executions. At each forest leaf, root every
tree. Partition its edges by child depth modulo $R$ and choose a class with
fewest edges. Append the stored value of each child in that class as one
additional public unary answer. The choices depend only on the already
completed transcript. Distinct edges have distinct children, so the refined
length satisfies $\ell_A\le(1+1/R)\ell$.
\formal{AdaptiveForestRefinement}{TransposeFollowup.AdaptiveForest.refined_length_mul_le,TransposeFollowup.AdaptiveForest.refined_rectangle}

Fix an attained refined transcript and consider its fiber in the full stored
cube. On every cut edge, substituting the observed child value leaves a unary
constraint on the parent. The fiber therefore factors over the retained
pieces, each of height less than $R$. Each piece occupies at most $K$ stored
rows, and any two of its rows are within distance $2R-2$ in the original
leaf's row graph. The appropriate row balls have size at most $\nu$.
No independence of the actual conditional input law is asserted.

Divide the rows into consecutive buckets of size at most $L$, using at most
$M$ bucket labels. Group pieces by their exact nonempty set of occupied
buckets. There are at most $Q$ groups. A coordinate is near if its forward
column window meets an occupied bucket of its group. Because the bucket set
is also the exact bucket set of its own piece, the row-ball estimate bounds
the total number of near coordinates by $N$. Each group has at most $K+1$
nonempty complementary row gaps.

Condition on all other groups and the refined transcript. A group's near
bits and its gap states determine the entire group: equal stored rows and
state on a gap give equal raw rows there by \eqref{eq:gap-bank-recovery};
the original receiver decoders then give the corresponding raw columns;
forward column locality determines every remaining far bit. This comparison
uses only actual correct inputs, never a hybrid input supplied to a decoder.
The sum of the groups' conditional entropies is therefore at most $N+Cs$.
\formal{GapBankLeafEntropy}{TransposeFollowup.GapBankCode.grouped_leaf_entropy}

For a full reference fiber $\mathcal R_a$, put
$e_a=\log_2|\mathcal R_a|-H(Y\mid A=a)$. Rectangularity bounds the conditional
total correlation of the groups by $e_a$. Complement subadditivity gives
\[
 H(Y\mid A=a)\le N+Cs+(Q-1)e_a.
\]
The reference is the uniform full cube. Its fiber-deficit identity gives
$\E e_A\le P-H(X)$, while prefix freedom gives $H(A)\le\E\ell_A$.
Thus the transcript is charged once, not again as an external helper.
Combining these inequalities proves \eqref{eq:adaptive-coded-forest} for
one bank. Conditioning on the actual helper and averaging replaces the
source deficit by $D_{\mathrm{tot}}$.
\formal{FullFiberEntropy}{TransposeFollowup.coordinateRectangle_full_join,TransposeFollowup.fullFiberReference_deficit}
The averaging lemma also permits a leaf-dependent overhead and charges its
expectation. Here the uniform geometric bound $N+Cs$ suffices.
\formal{FullFiberAveraging}{TransposeFollowup.fullFiber_expected_cost_of_leaf_cost,TransposeFollowup.fullFiber_expected_cost}
\end{proofoutline}

\begin{corollary}[Near-full answer cost]\label{cor:adaptive-coded-rate}
Along $d\to\infty$, allow $b\ge1$ and all bank and protocol data to vary,
with $\Delta$ fixed. If
\[
 \sigma=o(d),\qquad s+D_{\mathrm{tot}}=o(P),
\]
then $\E\ell\ge P-o(P)$. If, more specifically, $\sigma$ is fixed and
$s\le A_sW$, $D_{\mathrm{tot}}\le A_DW$ for fixed nonnegative constants,
then $\E\ell\ge P-O(P/\log\log d)$, uniformly in $b$ and the code.
\end{corollary}
\formal{AdaptiveForestAsymptotics}{TransposeFollowup.AdaptiveForestLaw.near_linear}
\formal{AdaptiveForestLaw}{TransposeFollowup.AdaptiveForestLaw.explicit_rate}
\formal{AdaptiveForestRate}{TransposeFollowup.AdaptiveForestLaw.logarithmic_logarithmic_rate}

\begin{proofoutline}
For the qualitative assertion, first fix $R$, then a sufficiently large fixed
$M$, and then let $d$ grow. The normalized near loss is at most
$\nu[1/M+(1+2\sigma)/d]$; for fixed $R,M$, the remaining normalized loss
tends to zero. Finally let $M$ and $R$ grow.
For the rate, put $t=\log_2d\ge\delta^8$ and take
\[
 R=\floor{\frac{\log_\delta t}{4}},\qquad K=\delta^R,
 \qquad M=\floor{d^{1/(4K)}}.
\]
The checked finite consequence is
\[
 1-\frac{\E\ell}{P}\le\frac{8\log\delta}{\log t}+e_d,
 \qquad
 e_d=K^2\left[2d^{-1/(4K)}+\frac{1+2\sigma}{d}\right]
       +\frac{(K+1)A_s+A_D}{\sqrt d}.
\]
Here $K^4\le t$, $Q\le\sqrt d$, and $e_d\log t\to0$. In particular, for all
sufficiently large $d$,
\[
 \E\ell\ge\left(1-\frac{8\log\delta+1}{\log\log_2d}\right)P.
\]
\formal{AdaptiveBankRateLimit}{TransposeFollowup.adaptiveRateError_log_t_tendsto}
\end{proofoutline}

\subsection{Expander shears and local recovery}

Let $d=2m$ and let $L\in\F_2^{m\times m}$ be the incidence matrix of a simple
bipartite graph on two sets of $m$ vertices. Suppose its maximum degree is
at most $\Delta$ and every set $S$ of at most $m$ vertices has at least
$\eta|S|$ neighbors outside $S$, where $0<\eta\le1$. For rows of width $W$,
define, independently in every bit position,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:expander-shear}
 \Phi_L(A,B)=(A,B+LA),\qquad P=2mW,\qquad
 c_0=\frac{\eta}{4(\Delta+1)^2}.
\end{equation}
This map is its own inverse. Each output bit depends only on raw bits in the
same bit position. Its inverse row supports and the number of inverse
supports containing any one stored row are at most $\Delta+1$.
\formal{ExpanderShear}{TransposeFollowup.ExpanderShear.shear_involutive}
\formal{ExpanderShearLocal}{TransposeFollowup.ExpanderShear.rawRow_factorsThrough_inverseRows,TransposeFollowup.ExpanderShear.inverseRows_card_le,TransposeFollowup.ExpanderShear.inverseRows_reuse_le,TransposeFollowup.ExpanderShear.shear_same_lane}

\begin{theorem}[Recovery across every pair of half-row sets]\label{thm:shear-recovery}
Let $Y=\Phi_L(X)$ and $D_X=P-H(X)$ for any input law. For every two sets
$I,J$ of $m$ rows each,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shear-half-entropy}
 \max\{H(X_I\mid Y_J),\ H(X_{I^c}\mid Y_{J^c})\}
 \ \ge\ c_0P-D_X.
\end{equation}
The input and stored half sets may be chosen independently.

More generally, allow any finite joint law of $X$ and a public helper $Z$;
here $Z$ need not be a deterministic function of $X$. The helper may select
both half sets and arbitrary bijections applied separately within each raw
and stored row. Suppose that, throughout each helper branch $Z=z$, both
raw half-row views can be recovered from their respective stored half-row
views and states whose ranges have at most $2^{s(z)}$ values, for a common
real budget $s(z)$ in that branch. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:shear-helper-recovery}
 \E s(Z)\ \ge\ c_0P-\bigl(P-H(X\mid Z)\bigr)
       =c_0P-D_X-I(X;Z),
\end{equation}
where $I(X;Z)=H(X)-H(X\mid Z)$. For deterministic $Z=Z(X)$ and a constant
budget $s$, this gives $s\ge c_0P-D_X-H(Z)$. The states and decoders may be
nonlinear. The bound does not cover a further change of coordinates that
combines different rows.
\end{theorem}
\formal{ShearGap}{TransposeFollowup.ExpanderShear.balanced_conditional_gap_sharp}
\formal{ShearGapRecoveryRanges}{TransposeFollowup.ExpanderShear.helper_recovery_gap_conditional_of_state_range_bits}

In particular, every raw-row order and stored-row order has an aligned prefix
or suffix half requiring this much recovery information. For fixed $\eta>0$ and $\Delta$, if
$D_X+I(X;Z)=o(P)$, no such ordering and within-row recoding can give all
interval states a common branch budget with expectation $o(P)$.
Independent public randomness contributes no information charge.
The bounded inverse-support property of the earlier
packet theorem is compatible with this obstruction: recovering a row from a
few arbitrary stored rows is different from recovering a whole interval from
only the stored rows in that same interval.

\begin{proofoutline}
Write the shear matrix as $T=\begin{pmatrix}I&0\\ L&I\end{pmatrix}$.
For selected halves $I,J$, let $q$ count the diagonal mismatches and $e$ the
crossing edges of the bipartite core in the two submatrices
$T[J,I^c]$ and $T[J^c,I]$. These occupy disjoint matrix entries, so their
combined nonzero count is at least $q+e$. The smaller core side has at least
$(d-q)/2$ vertices. Expansion gives $\eta(d-q)\le2e$; since $\eta\le1$,
the combined count is at least $\eta d/2$.
\formal{ExpanderShearCut}{TransposeFollowup.ExpanderShear.mismatch_add_coreCross_le,TransposeFollowup.ExpanderShear.balanced_cross_nnz_sharp}

A matrix with at most $\Gamma$ nonzero entries in each row and column has
at most $\Gamma^2\operatorname{rank}(M)$ nonzero entries. Indeed, basis rows
meet at most $\Gamma\operatorname{rank}(M)$ columns; all other columns vanish,
and each remaining column has at most $\Gamma$ nonzero entries. Taking
$\Gamma=\Delta+1$, one crossing rank is at least $c_0d$.
\formal{ExpanderShearRank}{TransposeFollowup.ExpanderShear.nnz_le_rank_mul_sq}

For an arbitrary input law, fiber counting for the crossing linear map gives
$H(X_I\mid Y_J)\ge W\operatorname{rank}(T[J,I^c])-D_X$.
Applying this to both cuts proves \eqref{eq:shear-half-entropy}.
A finite recovery state can supply at most the logarithm of its number of
possible values. Within-row bijections preserve the relevant information,
and averaging the branch bounds gives the exact conditional deficit
$P-H(X\mid Z)$. This proves \eqref{eq:shear-helper-recovery} with average
state budgets and no independence or linear-decoder assumption.
\formal{ShearGapEntropy}{TransposeFollowup.ExpanderShear.conditional_rowView_ge_cross_rank}
\end{proofoutline}

\subsection{A uniformly paid encoder}

The recovery obstruction can occur after a linear-time tape computation.
The following construction uses the rational monotone expander already
proved for the padding theorem, with XOR passes over the input data in place of
empty head excursions. The expander and compiler results are reused; the
new obligations are the data transformation and its complete native cost.

\begin{theorem}[Cheap encoding with expensive interval recovery]\label{thm:cheap-shear}
There are constants $\Delta,C\in\N$, $0<\eta\le1$, and one fixed
deterministic multitape machine $E$ with the following property. For every
even canonical dimension $d=2m\ge2$, there is a simple bipartite incidence
matrix $L_m$ satisfying the degree and expansion hypotheses of
\cref{thm:shear-recovery}. Put $W=db(d)$ and $P=dW$. On every raw $P$-bit
input $(A,B)$ in row-major order, $E$ outputs
\[
 (A,B+L_mA)
\]
in at most $CP$ steps. It starts with blank work tapes and uses the original
read-only input and append-only raw output conventions. Its alphabet,
finite control and tape count are independent of $d$.

The resulting banks are full-cube involutions with zero forward column
radius and inverse row support and reuse at most $\Delta+1$. For the
same constants, \cref{thm:shear-recovery} applies uniformly in $d$.
\end{theorem}
\formal{ExpanderShearNativeFamily}{TransposeFollowup.ExpanderShear.exists_fixed_native_shear}
\formal{NativeExpanderShear}{TransposeFollowup.NativeExpanderShearMachine.native_shear,TransposeFollowup.NativeExpanderShearMachine.fixed_resources}
\formal{ExpanderShearEncoding}{TransposeFollowup.ExpanderShear.rawBank_surjective,TransposeFollowup.ExpanderShear.boolShear_involutive}

Thus linear-time encoding, even with bounded inverse incidence and zero
forward column radius, does not force sublinear interval-recovery states
after row reorderings and separate within-row bijections, including randomized
choices with $D_X+I(X;Z)=o(P)$ and budgets charged in expectation. This rules out
that proposed route for automatically applying the adaptive forest bound
to fast tape computations. The construction is a workspace primitive; it
has no transpose decoder and proves no complete-exchange saving. The
earlier fixed-packet obstruction still applies to this bank under its
packet hypotheses. A further globally mixing change of representation is
outside the recovery theorem; applying the shear again restores the raw
input in linear time.

\begin{proofoutline}
The existing continuous-expander theorem supplies one fixed finite rational
family. Symmetrization and the finite overlap construction give positive
ordinary vertex expansion at every positive half-dimension $m$. The
incidence matrix is the simple, deduplicated overlap graph. Its edge pages
are partial increasing matchings, with a fixed page count $q$ and degree
bound independent of $m$. The ordered-graph address convention used to
prove expansion is transferred by a graph equivalence; it is not a free
physical row permutation.
\formal{ExpanderShearFamily}{TransposeFollowup.ExpanderShear.exists_rational_shear_family}
\formal{ExpanderShearGraph}{TransposeFollowup.ExpanderShear.rationalSymmetric_core_expansion,TransposeFollowup.ExpanderShear.rationalMatrix_row_support,TransposeFollowup.ExpanderShear.rationalMatrix_col_support}

The machine loads the raw input, determines its canonical dimension and
$m=d/2$, and executes the existing paid rational opcode generator at $m$.
The generator assigns each simple edge exactly one page. An actual tape
routine creates the unary row clock of length $W$, and fixed controllers
extract the page flags and repeat each row flag across its $W$ bit positions.
All metadata, clocks, delimiters and data copies are constructed from the
raw input and blank work tapes.

In a page pass, the source and target heads advance through their respective
rows. False flags skip unchanged bits; true flags pair the selected streams
in order and XOR source bits into the target. Increasing matching endpoints
make one forward pass sufficient. The source data are preserved; target
bits outside the matching are unchanged. Paid resets prepare the next pass.
Each page costs $O(mW)$, and $q$ is fixed. The actual generated masks select
precisely that page's edges, and their matrices sum to $L_m$ without
characteristic-two cancellation.
\formal{ExpanderShearRationalBridge}{TransposeFollowup.ExpanderShear.rational_page_masks,TransposeFollowup.ExpanderShear.rational_page_matrix_sum}
\formal{ExpanderShearNativeBridge}{TransposeFollowup.ExpanderShear.mask_page_stream_eq_shear,TransposeFollowup.ExpanderShear.boolShear_fold}

After the final page, the target is $B+L_mA$. A native output scan emits the
unchanged $A$ and updated $B$ in raw row-major order. The trace proof counts
initial loading, dimension arithmetic, opcode generation, clock and flag
preparation, all data passes, resets and output. Their sum is bounded by a
constant depending only on the fixed rational family times $P$. The
finite-state compilation has no dimension-dependent control and no initial
advice or preloaded graph.
\formal{NativeExpanderShearNumerics}{TransposeFollowup.NativeExpanderShearNumerics.total_budget_le}
\end{proofoutline}
