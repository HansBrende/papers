EXACT STATEMENTS
All two-by-two matrix norms and distances below are the native Frobenius ones; parameter vectors use the supremum norm. The identity has norm sqrt(2), bounded by 2 in the proof, never incorrectly set to 1.

1. POSITIVE-TRACE CENTRALIZER CHART.
Let b be a real traceless two-by-two matrix. For u=cI+t b in SL2(R),
  c^2+det(b)t^2=1.
If M>=1, 0<=r<=1, norm(b)<=M, |det(b)|<=M, and
  u=cI+t b, v=pI+q b are in SL2(R), c,p>=1/2, |t|,|q|<=r,
then
  norm(u-I),norm(v-I)<=3Mr,
  norm(u-v)<=5M|t-q|,
  norm(u-v-(t-q)b)<=4Mr|t-q|.
The more precise separate coefficient and matrix bounds are also proved. No division by det(b) or by a discriminant is used.

2. TRIPLE-PRODUCT REMAINDER.
In any normed ring with norm(1)<=2, if r<=1, all u_i,v_i lie within r of 1, and all norm(u_i-v_i)<=Y, i=0,1,2, then
  norm(u0u1u2-v0v1v2-sum_i(u_i-v_i))<=12rY.
The proof is an exact ordered telescoping identity plus existing norm inequalities.
For three centralizer charts as in (1), with 3Mr<=1, put
  U=u0u1u2-v0v1v2,
  L=sum_i(t_i-q_i)b_i, N=norm(t-q).
Then norm(U-L)<=192 M^2 r N.

3. EXPLICIT FRAME INVERSE.
Let T be a real four-by-four matrix whose columns 1,2,3 are the four matrix coordinates of b0,b1,b2. Column0 is arbitrary. Assume every |T_ij|<=M, d>0, |det(T)|>=d, and
  36864 M^6 r<=d.
With the preceding chart assumptions,
  norm(t-q)<=192 M^4/d * dist(u0u1u2,v0v1v2).
This reuses MatrixFour.mulVec_inverse_bound (96 M^4/d) and absorbs the explicit nonlinear remainder. It does not assert that such a T has been selected from our original matrix set.

4. CUBIC PACKING.
Let F be a finite set of real parameters. For each i=0,1,2 and x in F, let u_i(x)=c_i(x)I+x b_i be in SL2(R), with c_i(x)>=1/2 and |x|<=r. Assume the same frame and radius bounds, eta>=0, and
  |x-y|>384 M^4 eta/d for distinct x,y in F.
For S={u0(x0)u1(x1)u2(x2):x0,x1,x2 in F},
  N_eta(S) is finite and N_eta(S)>=|F|^3.
The actual map is injective and its distinct outputs have distance>2eta. The theorem also permits empty F and eta=0. It uses mathlib's externalCoveringNumber and the existing separated-sample comparison, not a new covering-number definition.
