\section{Historical feedback through actual halt}\label{sec:feedback}

This appendix measures a different property of an original execution.
Let $B=db$ and $P=dB$. Fix the native machine, a linear budget $C(P+1)$,
and work-window widths $w_h\ge2$, independent of $d,b$. Fix also an
enlarged finite alphabet containing the source symbols and distinct spool
markers and bits, with one $\kappa$-bit encoding taking blank to zero.
Use this \emph{same} encoding throughout. Each encoded $B$-cell work block
has $\kappa$ binary planes of declared capacity $B$ bits, even when a
plane happens to be constant.

The raw monitor initially retains blocks $-1,0$; work monitor $h$ retains
blocks $-(w_h-1),\ldots,0$. Admit raw row zero initially. After each native
step process a raw exit first, then work exits in fixed tape order. An
exit shifts that window by one block. A raw shift admits the valid entering
row or synthesizes exterior markers. A work shift writes every plane of
the leaving block before reading every plane of the entering block.
Overlapping blocks and partial writes remain in the resident window. Include
pending exits after the successful transition entering the halting
configuration; there is no terminal flush in the original statistic.

A write creates a new \emph{date} at its fixed address (tape, block, plane),
even when it repeats the same value. A read sees the latest preceding date,
or its initial constant. The current raw-row context starts at zero and
changes only when a valid raw row is admitted; exterior shifts retain it.
For each produced date imported at least once before the original run ends,
let $\ell_v$ be the maximum minus minimum of the contexts at its writer
and all its importing readers. Keep dates subsequently overwritten. Set
\[
 \Phi(X)=\sum_v B\ell_v,\qquad W(X)=\sum_v B.
\]
Repeated readers of one date share its span; a copy write creates another
date. This is a statistic of the specified monitor and encoding, not a
minimum over recodings or an entropy of its values.

We first prove the block-information estimate needed for the native adapter.
All entropy in this appendix is in bits.

\begin{lemma}[One partition for a complete producer and consumer]\label{lem:feedback-product}
\formal{FeedbackGlobalProduct.lean}{feedback_global_matrix_bound,feedback_global_product_bound}
Consider a fixed finite word of block operations. A producer begins with
constant memory, has at most $S$ bits of complete resident state, reads
raw rows and previously written block versions, and emits no transpose
output. Every local map has one deterministic completion on its entire
available-data domain. A subsequent read-only consumer starts at the true
handoff, has at most $M$ bits of complete state, reads only raw rows and
surviving final archive blocks, and emits all $d$ correct columns in a fixed
order at designated positions of the operation word, on one law of input
entropy $P-\Delta$. It has no readable scratch
or output readback. Let $Q_r$ count all consumer reads, $V$ be the total
variation of the producer's raw-row admission sequence, and
$\Phi=\sum_v c_v\ell_v$, where $c_v$ is the declared capacity of each
produced version read during production and its span uses the writer and
all those readers. Then for every integer $1\le g,w\le d$,
\[
 P\le\frac{SV+\Phi}{g}+\lceil d/w\rceil(M+\Delta)+Q_r gwb,
\]
and consequently
\[
 \max(1,Q_r/d)\left(1+\frac{SV+\Phi}{P}\right)
              \left(1+\frac{M+\Delta}{B}\right)\ge d/72.
\]
All controller, address, clock, buffer and pending fields belong to the
stated resident capacities.
\end{lemma}
\begin{outline}
Partition raw row indices into shifted width-$g$ intervals. Assign every
producer operation the group of its current context, updating the context
before a raw admission. For a uniformly chosen shift, two integer contexts
at distance $h$ have different groups with probability $\min(1,h/g)$:
if $h<g$ exactly $h$ residues place a boundary between them, and otherwise
every residue does. A produced version is read by an owner different from
its writer precisely when its extreme participating contexts have different
groups. Thus one shift simultaneously gives
\[
 S s+\sum_{v\in E}c_v
 \le S\sum_j\min(1,|i_{j+1}-i_j|/g)
       +\sum_vc_v\min(1,\ell_v/g)
 \le(SV+\Phi)/g,
\]
where $s$ is the number of owner switches and $E$ the distinct produced
versions used by a foreign owner. Both charges use the same shift.

Let $Z$ contain all complete switch states and those literal foreign
versions. Its full-cube fibres are Cartesian products over owner groups.
Here is the precise reconstruction proving that assertion. For an attained
$z$, replay all segments owned by a group in chronological order from its
own raw rows and $z$. Start the first global segment in constant state;
other segments use their prescribed entrance state. A read uses its own
raw row, an initial constant, a foreign version in $z$, or an earlier
own version retained in this mathematical replay. Require each segment's
exit state to match the prescribed switch state, and each exported version
to match its entry in $z$. These are tests only on that group's raw rows.
Every actual input with transcript $z$ passes them. Conversely, if every
group passes, induction through the fixed global word shows that raw,
own-version and foreign-version reads all agree: a foreign writer has
already passed its export test. Hence the actual transcript is $z$.
This proves the product-fibre assertion. The same replay gives one
group-local function for each surviving final version, common to all its
later reads. The proof-side retention of old versions adds no runtime state.

On the one actual law let
$\mathcal T=\sum_j H(X_{I_j}\mid Z)-H(X\mid Z)$.
Then $0\le\mathcal T\le\Delta$. Indeed the uniform full-cube law,
conditioned on $z$, is the product of uniform laws on the just-proved
group fibres. Decomposing relative entropy with respect to that law gives
\[
 \Delta=D(\mu_Z\Vert U_Z)+\mathcal T+
  \sum_j\mathbb E_z D(\mu_{I_j\mid z}\Vert U_{I_j,z})\ge\mathcal T.
\]
All reference laws are positive on the actual support. This neither assumes
the actual conditional law is a product nor asserts correctness of mixed
inputs. Also $H(Z)\le Ss+\sum_{v\in E}c_v$.

Divide the $d$ designated final column writes into
$m=\lceil d/w\rceil$ chronological windows of at most $w$ columns, the
first starting at actual consumer entry. In window $a$, let $J_a$ be its
columns and $q_a$ its reads, and let $H_a$ be the owner groups of queried
records. There are at most $q_a$ such groups. Given $Z$, their complete
raw rows and the actual $M$-bit entry state, replay recovers every window
output, because every queried version has the same group-local function.
The entropy of the unqueried groups' output rectangle conditional on those
raw rows and $Z$ is therefore at most $M$. Total correlation cannot increase
under separate projections of the groups; subtracting the nonnegative
correlation of the queried groups gives
\[
 \sum_jH(X_{I_j\times J_a}\mid Z)
       \le M+\mathcal T+g|J_a|bq_a.
\]
Summing over windows and using conditional subadditivity on each group's
partition into output columns gives
\[
 gb\sum_a|J_a|q_a
 \ge P-\Delta-H(Z)-mM-(m-1)\mathcal T.
\]
Now $|J_a|\le w$, $\sum_aq_a\le Q_r$ and $\mathcal T\le\Delta$
prove the finite inequality. The transcript is paid once, each real
window-entry state separately, and the law never changes.

For the product put $a=(SV+\Phi)/P$, $y=(M+\Delta)/B$, $z=1+y$ and
$q_0=\max(1,Q_r/d)$. Choose $g=\max(1,\lceil4a\rceil)$ and
$w=\lceil8z\rceil$. If $g>d$ then $4a>d$, and if $w>d$ then
$8z>d$; either already gives the product bound. Otherwise division by
$B$ yields $d\le ad/g+\lceil d/w\rceil y+(Q_r/d)gw$.
The first term is at most $d/4$ (zero if $a=0$). The second is at most
$d/8+z\le d/4$, since $w\le d$ implies $z\le d/8$.
Thus $d/2\le q_0gw\le36q_0(1+a)z$, as required.
The formal proof starts from one producer and one complete consumer word.
It constructs the common shifted partition, actual transcript, window-entry
states, slices of the consumer execution, and total read count. The final
integer optimization retains the constant $72$. Lean allows any bounded
handoff-state map and any dated producer version in the consumer's queries,
so the stated true-handoff and surviving-version restrictions are special
cases. Retaining old versions in the proof does not assert a free physical
archive.
\end{outline}

\begin{theorem}[Whole-run historical feedback]\label{thm:whole-feedback}
\notformalized
For the fixed native machine, linear budget and monitor there is $c_*>0$
such that, for every $d,b\ge1$, $\varphi\ge0$ and nonempty set $F$ of
inputs correctly transposed within the budget with $\Phi\le\varphi$,
\[
 \left(1+\frac\varphi P\right)
 \left(1+\frac{P-\log_2|F|}{B}\right)\ge c_*d.
\]
There is no original phase, read-only-work or alignment assumption.
\end{theorem}
\begin{informalproof}
First postpone the complete output by a paid spool. Add one initially blank
tape with a width-two monitor. Write a start marker, then simulate the
original machine, writing each appended bit to the next spool cell instead
of real output. Preserve all original heads and symbols in any extra spool
microstep. Continue through the actual original halt, including original
work after its last output and pending monitor events. Write an end marker,
then declare a phase change and flush every retained work window. Only
after this phase rewind and read the spool, appending its bits to real output.
Original tapes remain parked. A conservative step bound is
\[
 T_{\rm wrap}\le2T+4P+10\le\overline C(P+1),\qquad
 \overline C=2C+10.
\]
Markers avoid an uncharged length scan or counter.

On original tapes the extra steps only insert pauses. Their raw/work shift
order, per-address dates, values, partial writes and raw contexts agree
exactly with the original monitor. The spool moves only right or stays in
production, so its entering blocks are virgin and none of its produced
versions is read in production. The terminal flush likewise has no later
production reader. The old participating dates and spans therefore agree
exactly: $\Phi_{\rm wrap}=\Phi$. The artificial phase is before rewind;
spool rereads are consumer reads, not missing feedback.

The monitor realizes a complete block program. Outside each retained window
the block store equals the native tape; inside it the resident window is
authoritative. Write-before-load in either direction, retaining overlap,
preserves this invariant even with partial overwrites. Serialize all
$\kappa$ outgoing planes before incoming ones. At phase change the flush
makes the whole final archive exact; recovery is read-only. Count all
windows, incoming plane tuples, unfinished payload, source control, positions,
offsets, clock, pending exits and an output-column buffer. Fixed constants
$p_0,m_0$ bound complete producer and consumer state by $p_0B,m_0B$:
there are fixed many $B$-cell buffers and integer registers of
$O(\log(2P))$ bits, and $\log_2(2P)\le3B$ for $d,b\ge1$.
There is no free state reset at the phase.

A width-at-least-two monitor needs at least $B$ head moves before its first
exit and between successive exits, in either direction. With $K=k+1$
work tapes including the spool, valid raw admissions have total row variation
$V\le v_0d$, $v_0=4\overline C$, and all recovery reads obey
$Q_r\le q_0d$, $q_0=2\overline C(1+\kappa K)$. To see the former,
entering raw blocks change their valid indices by at most two per raw shift;
exterior shifts cannot increase the variation of the valid subsequence.
The latter follows by bounding every raw/work exit by
$\lfloor T_{\rm wrap}/B\rfloor\le2\overline C d$, counting all planes.
Likewise original participating capacity satisfies
$W\le u_0P$, $u_0=\max(1,2\overline C\kappa k)$, since every such
date is exported at an original work shift. Terminal flush versions and
initial constants do not participate.

Fix a coarse event word recording raw/work shifts with tape and direction,
the phase, and complete output-column flushes. Its alphabet has $2K+4$
letters; its length is at most
$[(2\overline C(K+1)+2)d]$. Such a word fixes all addresses, latest-date
ties, raw contexts and phase labels; flushes expand into fixed lists of
planes. Thus there are at most $2^{C_ed}$ words, where
\[
 C_e=[2\overline C(K+1)+2]\log_2(2K+5).
\]
One nonempty fibre $F_0\subseteq F$ has
$|F_0|\ge|F|2^{-C_ed}$, hence
$\Delta=P-\log_2|F_0|\le P-\log_2|F|+C_ed$.
Use its one uniform law throughout.

The fixed-word block maps have common local full-cube completions. Between
prescribed I/Os simulate from the complete resident tuple with the bounded
clock, stopping before any unexpected external observation. Invalid local
codes, wrong events, premature halt or timeout give fixed local defaults
for the remaining prescribed stages. Previously imported values are
resident data; the next prescribed read is the only new external value.
All guards and clocks are already charged. On $F_0$ every guard follows
the actual wrapped computation; off it, correctness and native validity
are unnecessary. No support-membership or global input test is used.

Apply \cref{lem:feedback-product}. It gives
\[
 \max(1,q_0)(1+p_0v_0+\varphi/P)
 (1+m_0+(P-\log_2|F|+C_ed)/B)\ge d/72.
\]
Since $d/B=1/b\le1$, take
\[
 c_*=[72\max(1,q_0)(1+p_0v_0)(1+m_0+C_e)]^{-1}.
\]
The displayed inequality implies the result by bounding each parenthesis
above by its constant factor times the corresponding parenthesis in the
statement. This proves the claim for every nonempty $F$ and every width.
\end{informalproof}

\begin{corollary}[Success tails and many long-span dates]\label{cor:feedback-tail}
\partformal{FeedbackTail.lean}{FeedbackTail.success_count,FeedbackTail.fixed_linear_count,FeedbackTail.linear_in_dimension_probability,FeedbackTail.many_long_span_dates}{the native monitor/spool theorem and its declared-capacity budget. Given those inequalities, Lean proves the exact counts, the ambient probability bound and the finite dated-plane conclusion.}
Let $E_\varphi$ be the timely correct inputs with $\Phi\le\varphi$.
Then, including the empty case,
\[
 |E_\varphi|\le2^{P-B[c_*d/(1+\varphi/P)-1]_+}.
\]
For every fixed $s\ge0$, $|E_{sP}|\le2^{(1-\eta_s)P}$ at large $d$,
with $\eta_s=c_*/[2(1+s)]$. For $\alpha=c_*/4$,
$|E_{\alpha Pd}|/2^P\le2^{-B}$ at large $d$.
Every other timely correct execution has more than $\alpha d/2$ dated
$B$-bit planes spanning at least $\alpha d/(2u_0)$ raw-row contexts.
\end{corollary}
\begin{informalproof}
Rearrange \cref{thm:whole-feedback} and use nonnegative source deficit.
For $\varphi=sP$, take $d\ge2(1+s)/c_*$. For
$\varphi=\alpha Pd$ and $d\ge1/\alpha$, the quotient
$c_*d/(1+\alpha d)$ is at least two, giving deficit at least $B$.
For an execution with $\Phi>\alpha Pd$, let $H_*$ be the total declared
capacity of dates with span at least $\beta d$, $\beta=\alpha/(2u_0)$.
Since all spans are at most $d-1$ and $W\le u_0P$,
$\Phi\le\beta dW+dH_*\le(\alpha/2)Pd+dH_*$.
Thus $H_*>(\alpha/2)P$, which is more than $\alpha d/2$ planes.
\end{informalproof}

The probability statement is an ambient joint success/low-feedback bound;
under full correctness it is a probability over the whole input cube.
Different dates at the same cell may contribute separately. Large feedback
is itself cheap: copy the raw rows in order, park the raw context at $d-1$,
then rescan the copy. On a tape with fixed window width $w$, block $j$
is exported when the aligned copying heads first enter block $j+w$.
For $0\le j\le d-w-2$, its produced date has writer context $j+w$ and
is imported during the rescan at context $d-1$. All $\kappa$ planes
count, including constant ones. Consequently, for $d>w+1$,
\[
 \Phi\ge\kappa B\sum_{j=0}^{d-w-2}(d-1-j-w)
       =\frac{\kappa B(d-w-1)(d-w)}2.
\]
The declared capacity of all participating dates is $O(P)$ and every span
is at most $d-1$, so $\Phi=\Theta(Pd)$ in $O(P)$ native time.
The reads may feed a parity accumulator without changing this statistic.
Hence neither the feedback nor its long dates are a time bill or independent
useful information.
