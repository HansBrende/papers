\section{Connected demand pairs and a depth-independent coding deficit}
\label{sec:cd}

The coloring in the preceding section separates the two ports of almost
every coordinate.  We now give a paid native modification for which no
such separation by connected components is possible after a sublinear
coordinate deletion.  Nevertheless its full cone relations have a
positive all-code deficit whose coefficient is independent of the depth.
The proof uses a finite row-phase description and an independent rectangle,
and does not recover a half-rate bound.

We retain $d=2m$, $b=\max(1,\lceil\log_2d\rceil)$, $W=db$, $L=d^2b$,
the graphs $H_m$, and the physical transpose-demand labels of
\cref{sec:ne}.  Indices of $B$ rows in this section are global:
$m\le j<d$.  Define a row-dependent rotation by
\[
 R:(i,c,s)\longmapsto(i,c+i\pmod d,s).
\]
The new machine computes the invertible map $R^{-1}\Phi R$.
Throughout this section, $k$ and $\beta$ denote the work-tape count and a
whole-step value-width bound for this rotated machine $M_R$, including
its additional buffers and controller tapes.  They need not equal the
corresponding bounds for the unwrapped machines.  We use
$B_D=\sum_{h=0}^{D}(k+1)^h$ with these enlarged resources.

\subsection{A robust count of connected pairs}

\begin{lemma}[Prescribed pairs in damaged expanders]
\notformalized
\label{lem:cd-connected-pairs}
Let $H_1,\ldots,H_W$ be disjoint graphs on $2m$ vertices, each of maximum
degree at most $\Delta$ and with outside vertex expansion at least
$\eta|S|$ for every $|S|\le m$.  Prescribe $n_*$ pairs of distinct
vertices, with all $2n_*$ endpoints distinct and each pair contained in
one graph.  After deletion of $f$ edges and $z$ vertices, at least
\[
 n_*-\frac f\eta-\left(1+\frac\Delta\eta\right)z
\]
prescribed pairs survive with their endpoints in the same connected
component.  The bound holds for every finite $m\ge1$ and arbitrary
distributions of the deletions among the graphs.
\end{lemma}

\begin{informalproof}
Vertex expansion implies an edge-boundary bound $|\partial_E S|\ge\eta|S|$.
Consider one graph, and write $f_0,z_0$ for its deletion counts.  If the
surviving graph has a component $G$ of more than $m$ vertices, let $S$ be
the union of its other surviving components.  Then $|S|<m$.  Every original
edge from $S$ to $G$ was deleted, and at most $\Delta z_0$ edges go from
$S$ to deleted vertices.  Thus
\[
 \eta|S|\le f_0+\Delta z_0.
\]
Every prescribed pair wholly inside $G$ is connected.  All other pairs
can be charged to distinct endpoints in $S$ or the deleted set, since the
prescribed endpoints are disjoint.  Their number is at most
$f_0/\eta+(1+\Delta/\eta)z_0$.

If every surviving component has size at most $m$, sum the edge-boundary
inequality over those components.  Edges between surviving components
were deleted and are counted at most twice; edges incident to a deleted
vertex contribute at most $\Delta z_0$.  Hence
\[
 \eta(2m-z_0)\le2f_0+\Delta z_0,
 \qquad
 m\le\frac{f_0}{\eta}+\frac{\Delta+\eta}{2\eta}z_0.
\]
There were at most $m$ prescribed pairs in this graph.  Declaring all of
them lost therefore satisfies the same asserted bound.  This includes an
empty surviving graph.  Summing over the copies proves the lemma.
\end{informalproof}

\subsection{A physical rotation wrapper}

\begin{proposition}[The rotated native shear]\label{prop:cd-rotated-native}
\notformalized
There is one fixed deterministic oblivious native machine $M_R$ which
computes $R^{-1}\Phi R$ in $O(L)$ transitions at all sufficiently large
even canonical dimensions.  All work heads are home-based.  For each
edge $A_iB_j$ of $H_m$ and each internal lane $(g,s)\in[d]\times[b]$
there is a literal backward path from the final physical append
\[
 (j,g-j\pmod d,s)
 \quad\hbox{to a raw read at}\quad
 (i,g-i\pmod d,s).
\]
These paths have endpoint-inclusive whole-step vertex congestion at most
$C_1=4\Delta$.  Their lengths need not be bounded.
\end{proposition}

\begin{informalproof}
Use the two banks and the paid setup and page executor of
\cref{prop:ne-native-shear}.  Add two reusable physical row-buffer
tapes, denoted $P_{\mathrm{buf}},S_{\mathrm{buf}}$, each with an origin and a movable
end marker.  Their symbols have fixed bit, origin, and end tracks.

We first specify a paid split-and-concatenate primitive.  Given a public
cut $a\in[0,W]$, clear the previous cut marker by a complete outward and
return sweep of the row clock.  Initialize an origin-based binary
countdown to $a$, move the clock one cell after each decrement, mark the
cut when the countdown becomes zero, and rewind the clock.  The countdown
does not perform a full zero test at each decrement: mark its highest
one, update that mark when a borrow removes it, and remember zero when
no one remains.  The total digit traversal in a countdown of $a$ is
$O(a+\log(a+1))$, including returns to the low digit.  Initial parameter
arithmetic uses the complete-sweep compiler of the preceding section.
The cut therefore costs $O(W+\operatorname{polylog}L)$, and all these
head motions are home-based.

To split a sequential $W$-bit source row, scan its bits with the row clock.
In one transition per bit copy into $P_{\mathrm{buf}}$ before the cut and into
$S_{\mathrm{buf}}$ at or after it, advancing only the source, active buffer,
and clock.  A fixed mode changes at the cut flag.  Mark the two buffer
ends and rewind the buffers and clock completely.  An empty buffer has
its end marker at zero.  On reuse, writing a bit clears any former end
flag at that cell; if a shorter string is stored, its new first end marker
stops subsequent reading.  Cells beyond that end are neither read nor
erased.  To concatenate in rotated order, scan $S_{\mathrm{buf}}$ and then
$P_{\mathrm{buf}}$, copying to the sequential destination or directly appending
to the real output.  Each bit transfer is one native transition, and the
buffers are fully rewound afterward.  There is no long-lived payload
register and at most one output bit per transition.  The entire primitive
costs $O(W+\operatorname{polylog}L)$.

For input row $i$, split at $W-ib$ and concatenate $S_{\mathrm{buf}}$ followed
by $P_{\mathrm{buf}}$ into its bank row.  This is a right rotation by $ib$
bits, so raw column $c$ reaches internal column $c+i\pmod d$ without
changing its within-entry bit offset.  Process all rows in order, loading
$A$ and then $B$, with no output appends.  Each bank head moves monotonically
through its bank and pauses during the buffer operations.  Write its
row-start flags on the first concatenated bit of each row and its end
marker on completing the bank; no full-bank setup sweep is used.  After loading,
rewind both banks completely.  Execute the $J$ shear pages exactly as in
\cref{prop:ne-native-shear}, including suffix scans and complete
synchronized rewinds.

Finally process bank rows $i=0,\ldots,d-1$ in order.  Split the bank row
at $ib$, and append $S_{\mathrm{buf}}$ followed by $P_{\mathrm{buf}}$.  This is a
left rotation by $ib$ bits and implements $R^{-1}$.  A source bank row is
copied into buffers before its output is emitted.  In particular, even
the unchanged $A$ output is delayed until after every core page.  We do
not give its syntactic cone an unjustified restriction to $A$ inputs.
The resulting $B$ output row is, explicitly,
\[
 B_j[c,s]+\sum_{i:A_iB_j\in E(H_m)} A_i[c+j-i\pmod d,s],
\]
with addition modulo two.

There are $d$ input and $d$ output primitives.  All parameter calculations,
cut marking, countdown borrows and returns, data copies, buffer rewinds,
bank rewinds, and appends are included in
\[
 O\bigl(L+dW+d\operatorname{polylog}L
              +m\operatorname{polylog}L+\operatorname{polylog}L\bigr)
 =O(L).
\]
The fixed page family, compiler, counters, buffers, and clocks use a fixed
number of tapes and fixed control.  End flags and public row indices
determine all movements, independently of the payload.  Banks traverse
monotonically from origin to end, with pauses, and return completely;
buffers and clocks make origin-to-end excursions; counters return to their
own origins.  Thus the entire physical trace is home-based.  During
concatenation a bank may pause inside a row, so the row-start parking
property of the unwrapped executor is not claimed here.

It remains to certify the actual paths.  Fix one edge and internal lane.
Its raw source bit is first written to an input-buffer cell, then copied
from that same cell to $A_i[gb+s]$.  The core XOR copies its contribution
to $B_j[gb+s]$; in the final wrapper that bank cell is copied to an
output-buffer cell and then read for the actual append.  Follow all
consecutive visits in these four residence intervals: input buffer to
bank copy, $A$ bank to XOR, $B$ bank to output-buffer copy, and output
buffer to append.  Include every parked rewrite.  Consecutive visits
are previous-visit edges, and successive intervals meet at the same copy
or XOR transition.  Reversing their concatenation gives a literal DAG
path with the displayed physical endpoints.

At any time each active bank carrier participates in at most $\Delta$
chosen paths.  The same holds for a live buffer carrier, because it
belongs to one source or one final target bit.  Reuse of a buffer cell
does not accumulate congestion: its residence intervals for different
rows are disjoint, and each path has left the cell before it is reused.
The input and output phases are disjoint, and buffering an $A$ output
carries none of the selected paths.  A step scans one cell of each of the
four payload tapes, so the union bound gives congestion $4\Delta$,
including steps at which a path changes carrier and all parked endpoints.
\end{informalproof}

\begin{theorem}[Many robustly connected demand pairs]
\notformalized
\label{thm:cd-robust-demands}
For $M_R$, every genie $\Gamma$ and every deleted coordinate set $U$
leave at least
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cd-pair-bound}
 \frac L4-\frac{C_1}{\eta}|\Gamma|
             -2\left(1+\frac\Delta\eta\right)|U|
\end{equation}
surviving coordinates whose two ports are connected in the induced full
cone graph.  There are constants $\varepsilon>0$ and fixed integers
$D,M$ for which the permitted cheap depth-$D$ genies are nonempty and,
for every such genie, the usual degree and own-port-edge coordinate
trims yield a bounded-degree graph on $N=\Theta(L)$ coordinates with at
least $L/8$ connected demand pairs.  After any additional $o(L)$
coordinate deletion at least $L/16$ such pairs remain for all sufficiently
large $d$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{informalproof}
For each internal lane $(g,s)$, the literal paths give a copy of $H_m$
with core ports
\[
 A_i\longleftrightarrow L_{(i,g-i,s)},\qquad
 B_j\longleftrightarrow R_{(g-j,j,s)},
\]
where columns are reduced modulo $d$.  These $W$ cores have $L$ distinct
ports.  For a coordinate $p=(i,c,s)$ with $i<m$ and $c\ge m$, its two
ports lie in the same core lane $g=i+c\pmod d$.  Prescribe these
$m^2b=L/4$ disjoint pairs.  Their distribution among lanes need not be
uniform.

Discard every selected core path meeting $\Gamma$, including endpoints.
At most $C_1|\Gamma|$ edges are discarded.  Every remaining path witnesses
an actual full-cone incidence.  Deleting $U$ removes at most $2|U|$ core
ports.  \cref{lem:cd-connected-pairs} proves~\eqref{eq:cd-pair-bound},
and extra full-cone edges can only join components.

Put $A_*=C_1/\eta$ and $B_*=2(1+\Delta/\eta)$.  Choose
$0<\varepsilon\le\min(1,\beta/(16A_*))$.  For every genie of price
$\beta|\Gamma|\le\varepsilon L$, the first loss in
\eqref{eq:cd-pair-bound} is at most $L/16$.  \cref{lem:ne-depth-import}
supplies a fixed full depth $D$ and nonemptiness.  Fix this $D$ and let
$B_D=\sum_{h=0}^D(k+1)^h$.  Choose $M_0\ge32B_*B_D$.
There are at most $B_D L$ incidence edges, so deleting coordinates with
left degree above $M_0$ costs at most $L/(32B_*)$.  The self-demand
estimate of \cref{cor:nb-self-demands} bounds the own-port-edge deletion by
$O(L/\log d)$, eventually also at most $L/(32B_*)$.  The remaining
maximum degree is at most $M=\max(M_0,B_D)$ and its coordinate count is
at least $L-L/(16B_*)$.  Formula~\eqref{eq:cd-pair-bound} now leaves
at least $L/4-L/16-L/32-L/32=L/8$ pairs.  An additional set $U'$ costs
at most $B_*|U'|$ in that formula, proving the last assertion.

The choices have the order machine, $\varepsilon$, $D$, $M_0$, and
finally sufficiently large $d$.  None of $D,M_0$ grows with dimension.
All conclusions hold for every genie satisfying the fixed price and
depth bounds, rather than for a selected favorable genie.
\end{informalproof}

An edge-monochromatic port coloring is constant on each connected
component.  Thus \cref{thm:cd-robust-demands} precludes separating
all surviving own-port pairs by such a coloring after $o(L)$ further
deletion, even after paying the fixed degree and self-demand trims.
This does not contradict the directed incidence bridge: for every fixed
radius, that bridge bounds the number of short paired paths by
$O(L/\log d)$.  Almost all of the connected pairs above consequently
have distance exceeding any prescribed fixed radius.  Connectivity and
bounded-distance recovery are different assertions.

\subsection{Every full cone has a fixed row-phase description}

We next analyze all syntactic dependencies of the same machine, including
the auxiliary tapes.  We do not infer an incidence upper bound from the
selected expander paths or from the support of the shear matrix.

A \emph{row word} with $\ell$ maps has the form
\[
 x_0\ \xrightarrow{\mathrm{band}}\ y_0
 \ \xrightarrow{f_1}\ x_1
 \ \xrightarrow{\mathrm{band}}\ y_1
 \ \xrightarrow{f_2}\ \cdots
 \ \xrightarrow{f_\ell}\ x_\ell
 \ \xrightarrow{\mathrm{band}}\ y_\ell.
\]
There are exactly $\ell+1$ bands, including the endpoint bands.
Each map is a strictly monotone partial injection between integer intervals;
each band permits an arbitrary subset of the pairs $|x-y|\le r$ in its
interval.  For $\ell=0$ the word is one band.  Different maps may have
different intermediate domains.  A word records every attained endpoint,
and need not be single-valued.

\begin{lemma}[A fixed number of row words]\label{lem:cd-row-words}
\notformalized
For the complete trace of $M_R$ there are fixed integers $K,\ell_{\max}$ and fixed
constants $C,h$ with the following property.  For every fixed $D\ge0$
and every full-depth-$D$ whole-step genie, there is a coordinate exception
set $Z$ with
\[
 |Z|\le z_D(d):=B_D C\bigl[W+d(1+\log_2(L+2))^h\bigr]+d=o_D(L)
\]
such that every actual cone incidence $L_pR_q$ with $p,q\notin Z$ has
its physical output row $\operatorname{outrow}(q)$ and raw input row
$\operatorname{rawrow}(p)$ related by one of at most $K$ row words.
Each word has at most $\ell_{\max}$ strictly increasing partial injections and
bands of radius $r=D+2$.  Its direction is from physical output row to
raw input row.  One may take
\[
 M_{\mathrm{ph}}=2J+3,\qquad K=3^{M_{\mathrm{ph}}}+1,
 \qquad \ell_{\max}=2(M_{\mathrm{ph}}-1).
\]
The maps can depend on the dimension's actual scan events; their number
and the word lengths do not depend on $d,D$, or the genie.
\end{lemma}

\begin{informalproof}
Call everything through parameter, endpoint-list, and clock initialization
setup.  Thereafter the successive macro phases are
\[
 I,\ R_0,\ F_1,\ R_1,\ldots,\ F_J,\ R_J,\ O.
\]
Here $I$ is the complete input wrapper, $O$ the complete output wrapper,
$R_0$ the post-load synchronized rewind, $F_a$ the forward part of page
$a$, and $R_a$ its synchronized rewind.  There are $M_{\mathrm{ph}}$ phases,
each occurring once.  A backward edge crossing phases strictly decreases
the phase index.

In a wrapper phase, row episode $i$ contains its parameter calculation,
cut marking, countdown, split, concatenation, tests, and returns.  Episodes
occur in increasing global row order and do not interleave.  In a forward
page, a scan event consists of a whole $W$-bit $A$ skip, $B$ skip, or
matched $A/B$ scan and its following clock return.  Number events in
chronological order, ignoring intervening controller ticks.  There are
at most $2m=d$ events in one page.  Each row of either bank is active in
exactly one event and its active events are in increasing order.  Let
\[
 \tau_{a,A},\tau_{a,B}:[m]\longrightarrow[E_a]
\]
be the corresponding strictly increasing injections.  Their values agree
at a matched scan.  One common event index is used even when one bank is
parked; the parked cursor is not an independently selectable row.

We define a sparse set $X$ of marked real time vertices.  First mark the
first post-setup state vertex and the first post-setup visit to each work
cell touched in setup.  The setup inventory, not its time, is
$O(W+d\operatorname{polylog}L)$.  These marks block every backward edge
entering setup.  Next mark the first and last ticks of every macro phase
and wrapper row episode, all synchronized-rewind turn and end-test ticks,
all slow controller, parser, list, and arithmetic ticks in core pages,
and the first and last ticks of each core row scan and clock return.
The core slow work totals $O(d\operatorname{polylog}L)$.  Wrapper countdowns
are not included in this wholesale marking: their time can be linear.

On each bank mark moving visits to row-boundary cells, and the first tick
of each maximal constant-position run of at least two ticks.  There are
$O(d)$ such runs per fixed phase: a bank pauses at the constant number of
operation boundaries in each wrapper row, and during core row or controller
operations.  A long countdown gives one bank wait, not a new wait at each
decrement.  This rule also covers an interior bank pause between the two
concatenation segments.

For each macro phase, mark its first visit to each cell of the row clock
and the two row buffers.  They occupy only $[0,W]$, giving $O(M_{\mathrm{ph}}W)$
marks.  Finally, for each wrapper row episode mark its first visit to
each cell of every other controller or work tape.  Those tapes use only
polylogarithmically many distinct cells in that episode: metadata heads
park at their origins during wrappers, and the scratch ruler, arithmetic,
and countdown use their bounded polylogarithmic windows.  This costs
$O(d\operatorname{polylog}L)$.  We have not marked first visits to every
bank cell in every phase, nor every clock cell in every row.  Either
would incorrectly charge a linear number of vertices.  The stated rules
give
\[
 |X|\le C[W+d(1+\log_2(L+2))^h].
\]

Assign each unmarked post-setup vertex a chart: its global row-episode
index in $I$ or $O$, its event index in $F_a$, and the common local bank
row $\lfloor\operatorname{position}/W\rfloor$ in $R_a$.  At good rewind
vertices both banks move in lockstep through interior cells.  We check
that every same-phase edge between good vertices changes chart by at
most one.

For state edges this follows from chronological row or event order.
For a wrapper controller tape, an edge into an earlier episode starts at
the first visit to that cell in the current episode and was marked.
All remaining such edges stay in their row, including every countdown
borrow and return.  The row clock receives a complete outward-and-return
sweep while clearing the cut in every wrapper row.  A clock predecessor
therefore lies in the same or preceding row episode.  Its first macro-phase
touch is already marked.

For buffers, every used prefix, including its end, is visited in the row
episode.  During $I$, the $P_{\mathrm{buf}}$ lengths $W-ib$ decrease and the
$S_{\mathrm{buf}}$ lengths $ib$ increase; during $O$ these monotonicities are
reversed.  If a cell has an earlier visit within a wrapper phase, this
monotonicity ensures that the immediately preceding episode also visited
it, unless it has already been visited in the current episode.  Position
zero is visited even for an empty buffer.  The first touch in each macro
phase was marked, so stale earlier-phase visits cause no exception.

For a wrapper bank, any stationary visit after the first has predecessor
$t-1$, while the first tick of a nontrivial parking run was marked.  An
isolated moving interior visit belongs to its physical row episode.
The bank progresses monotonically through that wrapper, so an earlier
same-phase visit to that cell is in the same row.  Moving row-boundary
visits were marked.  These facts cover the possible interior pauses.

At good core forward vertices, controller tapes and buffers are stationary;
after the marked first fast tick their predecessors are $t-1$.  The same
holds for a parked bank after its marked first parking tick.  Each active
interior bank cell is visited once in the forward page, so any other
predecessor leaves the phase.  The moving clock visits each interior cell
on the scan and return of every event, giving a predecessor in the same
or preceding event.  In a synchronized rewind, each bank cell is visited
once, all other heads are stationary, and state edges change local row by
at most one.  Turn and end-test vertices were marked.  This completes the
same-phase edge audit.

Now consider an edge with good endpoints in different phases.  State
crossings are blocked by phase marks, clock and buffer crossings by their
first macro-phase touches, and controller crossings by per-row first
touches in wrappers or by stationary/slow-tick rules in the core.  Setup
crossings have also been blocked.  Only bank cell edges remain.  The
current endpoint cannot be the first tick of a parking run or a moving
row boundary.  It therefore has the chart of its actual active interior
bank row.  At the earlier endpoint, any wrapper pause at that interior
cell belongs to the same physical row; a page or rewind has its unique
active visit.  Thus both endpoints refer to one and the same local bank
row $u\in[m]$.

For bank $H\in\{A,B\}$ define the strict injections
\[
 \begin{array}{lll}
 \iota_{I,A}(u)=\iota_{O,A}(u)=u,&
 \iota_{I,B}(u)=\iota_{O,B}(u)=m+u,\\
 \iota_{R_a,A}(u)=\iota_{R_a,B}(u)=u,&
 \iota_{F_a,A}(u)=\tau_{a,A}(u),&
 \iota_{F_a,B}(u)=\tau_{a,B}(u).
 \end{array}
\]
A backward bank edge from phase $F$ to an earlier phase $F'$ acts exactly
by $\iota_{F',H}\circ \iota_{F,H}^{-1}$ on its chart, with the inverse interpreted
on its image.  This factorization uses a common physical cell and does
not assume that an increasing partial map preserves distances.

Every append occurs in $O$ with chart equal to its physical output row.
After input loading the raw head reads blank.  During $I$ it reads a bit
of the current row while splitting, or waits at the first bit of the next
row during concatenation and controller work.  Exclude the $d$ raw
row-start coordinates.  Every other raw attachment at a good vertex then
has raw row equal to its chart.

Exclude also the demand labels of outputs in the $D$-step forward
neighborhood of $X$ in the full DAG.  There are at most $B_D|X|$ of them.
A free cone of any remaining output can meet neither $X$ nor setup,
because its paths have length at most $D$.  The physical output exclusions
are converted through $\Pi$ to coordinate labels before being united with
the raw row-start exclusions.  This gives the set $Z$ in the statement.

Break a surviving output-to-raw path at its cross-phase edges.  Each
same-phase segment has chart displacement at most $D$.  Each cross-phase
edge contributes the two strict partial injections just displayed.  Since
phase indices strictly decrease, there are at most $M_{\mathrm{ph}}-1$
crossings.  Enlarging every local displacement and every zero gap between
these injections to a band of radius $D+2$ gives the asserted words.
There are at most $2\cdot3^{M_{\mathrm{ph}}-2}$ phase-and-bank choices for paths
from $O$ to $I$; the larger value $K=3^{M_{\mathrm{ph}}}+1$ is convenient and
also covers empty or padded choices.  The bounds on $K,\ell_{\max}$ are independent
of depth.  Finally $W/L=1/d$ and
$d\operatorname{polylog}L/L\to0$, proving the fixed-$D$ error estimate.
\end{informalproof}

\subsection{Threshold pullback through partial monotone words}

Partial increasing injections need not preserve distances: the map sending
$0,100$ to $0,1$ is a counterexample.  For the row words it is sufficient
to pull back thresholds, which does remain valid in the presence of
arbitrary domain gaps.

\begin{lemma}[Finite threshold pullback]\label{lem:cd-threshold}
\notformalized
Let $K$ row words connect the starting and ending intervals $[d]$, each
using at most $\ell_{\max}$ strictly monotone partial injections and bands of integer
radius $r\ge0$.  Maps may be increasing or decreasing.  Partition each endpoint
interval into $B$ nonempty consecutive buckets, where $1\le B\le d$;
the partitions may differ.  There is a set of starting rows $T$ with
\[
 |T|\le\min\{d,\ K(B-1)[(2r+1)^{\ell_{\max}+1}-1]\}
\]
such that, for each word, all attained endings of any remaining starting
row lie in one ending bucket.  That bucket index is monotone in the
starting row, with sign equal to the product of the map signs.  In
particular the union of all retained word relations occupies at most
$K(2B-1)$ starting/ending bucket pairs.
\end{lemma}

\begin{informalproof}
Use threshold predicates $1[x\ge\theta]$ and their complements, allowing
infinite cuts for constants.  Pulling a threshold through a strictly
increasing partial injection yields a threshold of the same polarity on
its domain; a decreasing injection reverses polarity.  Extend across
domain gaps by a half-line cut between the two parts of the domain.
Such an extension exists because the map is strictly monotone.  Exactness
is required only on its domain, not at unused intermediate values.

For a band, a threshold at finite cut $\theta$ is unchanged for every
permitted pair $(x,y)$ except possibly when
\[
 x\in[\theta-r,\theta+r-1],
\]
an interval of at most $2r$ integers.  The same holds for the complement;
a constant threshold has no exceptional values.  Moreover a fixed point
at an intermediate layer has at most $(2r+1)^j$ possible starting
ancestors through a prefix containing $j$ bands: backward traversal of
a partial injection has at most one preimage, and backward traversal of
a band at most $2r+1$.

Fix a word with $\ell\le\ell_{\max}$ maps and one ending-bucket boundary.  Pull
its upper-threshold predicate backward through the word.  At each band
record its ambiguous input values and all their actual starting ancestors.
For the band numbered $j$ from zero, the prefix has $j$ earlier bands,
so this adds at most $2r(2r+1)^j$ starting rows.  Their union has size at
most
\[
 2r\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}(2r+1)^j=(2r+1)^{\ell+1}-1.
\]
For $r=0$ both sides are zero.  Every full path from an uncharged starting
row avoids every ambiguous input, so its endpoint comparison with the
fixed boundary equals the pulled-back threshold at the starting row.
This assertion holds simultaneously for all attained paths, not merely
for a selected representative endpoint.

Take the union over $K$ words and $B-1$ ending boundaries.  Outside this
set $T$, the boundary comparisons determine one ending bucket for every
attained starting row.  Each comparison has the polarity of the word,
so this bucket index is monotone with that sign.  List the attained
starting rows increasingly and reverse ending-bucket order for a negative
word.  Both bucket indices are now nondecreasing.  Between successive
distinct occupied bucket pairs at least one index increases; their total
increase is at most $2(B-1)$.  Thus one word occupies at most $2B-1$
bucket pairs and all words at most $K(2B-1)$.  Missing paths, domain
holes, and empty map domains can only reduce these counts.
\end{informalproof}

In \cref{lem:cd-row-words} the starting row is the physical output row.
Consequently $T$ in \cref{lem:cd-threshold} is a set of output rows.
Under transpose demand identification, deleting that row set means
deleting coordinates $(i,j,s)$ with $j\in T$, at cost $db|T|$ before
overlaps.  Swapping this with the raw row index would be an incorrect
identification of the demand.

\subsection{An independent rectangle and the all-code gap}

\begin{lemma}[A finite independent rectangle]\label{lem:cd-rectangle}
\notformalized
Let any directed recovery relation on $V=[d]^2\times[b]$ have the
following endpoint interface.  There are raw-row exceptions $S$, physical
output-row exceptions $T$, and coordinate exceptions $Z$.  Partition
$[d]$ into $B$ contiguous near-equal buckets and suppose every incidence
$L_pR_q$ with $p,q\notin Z$, $\operatorname{rawrow}(p)\notin S$, and
$\operatorname{outrow}(q)\notin T$ belongs to one of at most
$K(2B-1)$ raw-row/output-row bucket pairs.  Let $B=4K$ and $d\ge B$.
Then the relation has an independent coordinate set $I$ with
\[
 |I|\ge L_0:=\max\left\{0,
 b(2K+1)\left\lfloor\frac d{4K}\right\rfloor^2
       -bd(|S|+|T|)-|Z|\right\}.
\]
Independence means that no recovery neighborhood of a coordinate of $I$
contains a coordinate of $I$, including itself.
\end{lemma}

\begin{informalproof}
In the bipartite graph of occupied bucket pairs, some output bucket has
degree at most
\[
 \left\lfloor\frac{K(2B-1)}B\right\rfloor=2K-1.
\]
Let $C$ be its row set, and let $R$ be the union of its nonneighboring
raw buckets.  At least $2K+1$ raw buckets are in $R$.  Each bucket has
at least $\lfloor d/(4K)\rfloor$ rows.  Define
\[
 I=\bigl((R\setminus S)\times(C\setminus T)\times[b]\bigr)\setminus Z.
\]
Before exceptions its size is at least the leading term in $L_0$.
The raw-row and output-row exclusions cost at most $bd|S|$ and $bd|T|$,
and coordinate exclusions at most $|Z|$.  This bound permits all exceptions
to concentrate in the selected rectangle.

If $p,q\in I$ and $p$ were in the recovery neighborhood of $q$, the
interface would put $\operatorname{rawrow}(p)\in R$ and
$\operatorname{outrow}(q)\in C$ in an occupied pair, contrary to the
choice of $R$.  Thus $I$ is independent in the original relation; no
individual incidence edge has been removed.
\end{informalproof}

\begin{lemma}[Independent coordinates bound every induced code]
\notformalized
\label{lem:cd-independent-code}
If $I$ is independent in a directed recovery relation on $V$, then for
every $Q\subseteq V$ and every storage code $\mathcal C\subseteq
\mathcal A^Q$ for the induced relation,
\[
 |\mathcal C|\le a^{|Q|-|I\cap Q|},\qquad a=|\mathcal A|.
\]
For any random word supported on the code, with any probability law,
\[
 H(X_Q)=H(X_{Q\setminus I})\le(|Q|-|I\cap Q|)\log_2a.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{informalproof}
Projection onto $Q\setminus I$ is injective on the code.  Indeed, two
codewords agreeing there agree on the entire recovery neighborhood of
each coordinate in $I\cap Q$, since that neighborhood contains no
coordinate of $I$.  The storage-code property forces agreement on every
remaining coordinate.  This proves the cardinality and entropy assertions
directly, without an extension assumption or a sequential recovery order.
\end{informalproof}

\begin{theorem}[A positive deficit independent of depth]
\notformalized
\label{thm:cd-uniform-gap}
There is a constant $\rho>0$, depending only on the fixed machine $M_R$,
with the following property.  For every fixed $D$, every whole-step genie
of full depth at most $D$, every induced coordinate set $Q\subseteq V$,
and every nonempty storage code $\mathcal C\subseteq\mathcal A^Q$ for
the actual full-cone relation, put $N=|Q|$, $a=|\mathcal A|\ge2$.
Then
\[
 \log_a|\mathcal C|
 \le N-\max\{0,L_D(d)-|V\setminus Q|\},
\]
where, for all $d\ge4K$, one may take
\[
 \begin{split}
 t_D&=K(4K-1)\bigl[(2D+5)^{\ell_{\max}+1}-1\bigr],\\
 L_D(d)&=\max\left\{0,
 b(2K+1)\left\lfloor\frac d{4K}\right\rfloor^2
                    -bd\min(d,t_D)-z_D(d)\right\},
 \end{split}
\]
with $K,\ell_{\max},z_D$ from \cref{lem:cd-row-words}.  In particular one can
choose $\rho=1/(8K)$, independently of $D$, and obtain
\[
 \log_a|\mathcal C|
 \le N-\rho L+|V\setminus Q|+o_D(L),
\]
along with the trivial bound $\log_a|\mathcal C|\le N$.
No price bound on the genie, linearity assumption on the code, or degree
bound on the relation is required.
\end{theorem}

\begin{informalproof}
Apply \cref{lem:cd-row-words}, and partition the two row orders into
$B=4K$ near-equal buckets.  \cref{lem:cd-threshold} with $r=D+2$
supplies at most $\min(d,t_D)$ exceptional starting rows, which are
physical output rows.  Transposing the occupied bucket pairs gives the
raw-row/output-row interface of \cref{lem:cd-rectangle}; the number
of occupied pairs remains at most $K(2B-1)$.  There are no extra raw-row
exceptions, and the native coordinate exceptions number at most $z_D(d)$.
Thus one independent set $I$, chosen from the relation alone, has size
at least $L_D(d)$.

Since $|I\cap Q|\ge\max\{0,L_D(d)-|V\setminus Q|\}$,
\cref{lem:cd-independent-code} proves the exact finite bound.  The same
$I$ works for every $Q$, alphabet, and code on this relation.
For the asymptotic assertion use $\lfloor x\rfloor^2\ge x^2-2x$ for
$x\ge0$.  The leading term satisfies
\[
 b(2K+1)\left\lfloor\frac d{4K}\right\rfloor^2
 \ge\frac{2K+1}{16K^2}L-\frac{2K+1}{2K}db.
\]
Its coefficient exceeds $1/(8K)$.  For fixed $D$, the output-row loss is
$O_D(db)=o_D(L)$ and $z_D(d)=o_D(L)$.  This proves the asserted choice
of $\rho$.  The eventual dimension threshold can depend on $D$, while
$\rho$ cannot and does not.  The empty code satisfies the cardinality
bound separately.
\end{informalproof}

\begin{remark}[What the gap establishes]\label{rem:cd-scope}
The same native trace simultaneously has the robust connected demand pairs
of \cref{thm:cd-robust-demands} and the all-code deficit of
\cref{thm:cd-uniform-gap}.  Its independent rectangle sacrifices a
fixed positive fraction of coordinates.  It forbids internal recovery
edges, and does not claim to separate almost all paired ports into
different components or to eliminate longer paired paths.

The row-word proof depends only on the full head positions, raw-read
positions, and append times, not on the semantic meaning of the shear's
updates.  Thus its relation-level code bound also applies if one changes
the bounded-alphabet local rules while keeping exactly that trace.  A
cheap-genie fibre count would exclude a transposer on this specified
trace: choose price less than $\rho L$, fix the resulting depth, apply
the full-code bound to each genie fibre, and sum the fibre cardinalities.
We do not present that consequence as a new algorithm exclusion.  The
first paper's pass theorem~\cite[Theorem P]{BrendeTapeCoding} already excludes this fixed trace after
absorbing its sublinear nonbank workspace into finite control at each
dimension.  The new content here is the uniform-over-genies, every-induced-
code statement about the actual cone relation.

The fixed number of phases, full rewinds, monotone buffer-length sequences,
small controller span, and common scan-event chart are essential verified
properties of this example.  No normalization of an arbitrary native
schedule to these properties has been proved.  In particular the theorem
does not supply a deficit for all cheap constant-depth native cones, an
adaptive common relation, or the general transposition lower bound.
\end{remark}
