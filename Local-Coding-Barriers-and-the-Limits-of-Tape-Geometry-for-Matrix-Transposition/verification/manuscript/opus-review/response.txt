RESPONSE TO THE OPUS 5.5 REVIEW
Local Coding Barriers and the Limits of Tape Geometry for Matrix Transposition
Review of the suggestions supplied for commit 2c05e49, October 2026

All 25 suggestions were checked against the manuscript, formal sources, or
reproduction tools as appropriate. Most are accepted; items 14 and 15 contain
mathematical overstatements, and item 23 was subsequently expanded to sanitize all public records while
preserving raw historical evidence privately. Additional qualifications to
items 3, 5, 6 and 9 are recorded below. No numbered result was removed, no
mathematical Lean statement or proof was changed, and no new research claim
is made. Seven additional existing declarations are now cited explicitly.

FRAMING

1. Accepted. The abstract distinguishes the finite coding models from native
   machine results. Coefficients 2 are universally optimal over finite instances;
   the d=2 witnesses do not establish sharpness when both information charges
   are o(P). The text now says so.

2. Accepted. The padding discussion uses an immediately halting empty-output
   payload. This unconditionally produces a machine satisfying the geometric
   conditions without transposing; no hypothetical linear transposer is needed.

3. Accepted with scope qualifications. The idle tape count, K0=53+q, and the
   role of 4q<=3k eta in the coarse memory-boundary charge are explicit. This
   describes how this construction proves its cut inequality; it is not a
   theorem that idle tapes are necessary for every possible construction.
   Deleting idle heads preserves the S=empty instances, excess bound, and
   existence of nonmonotone/nonantitone motion. We do NOT assert preservation
   of the full M* inequality for nonempty S, whose RHS also depends on k.

4. Accepted. Padding is a data-free prefix, generated and executed once before
   the payload. Coupling such motion to actual encoded data at successive
   recursive levels remains open. The first-paper section locator is fixed.

5. Accepted with a qualification. The recursion theorem excludes stages with
   its precise affine regularity. A stage limit agreeing with a non-affine
   Mobius map on an interval has no finite affine cover. The first paper's
   intended non-affine profiles therefore escape this criterion if realized
   by the actual execution. The exceptional-work toll is made explicit.

6. Accepted with qualifications. The 1/(2G) bound is summed physical head-time
   outside affine regions, NOT a lower bound on distance travelled; half a
   nominal stored-input scan is only a scale comparison. A one-node clock is
   available when its fixed family constants may be chosen, not necessarily
   for every arbitrarily prescribed set of constants. Regularity is the
   substantive restriction.

7. Accepted. The first paper's claim about needing encoder and decoder savings
   is identified as heuristic.

PRECISION

11. Accepted. Fixed recoded-XOR broadcasts are original-row protocols with
    constant helper and full-cube correctness. The separator theorem has the
    stronger path/cycle leading constant. Its loss on the stated 100-cycle
    example is 1810b, versus about 2470.54b for the distance bound. The latter
    is now an illustration; the bounded-degree expander consequence is its
    additional reach. Historical improvement logs remain historical.

12. Accepted. Added the explicit loss epsilon*P+b*K(epsilon)*d and the broader
    expected-treewidth o(d) consequence, retaining the information-charge and
    uniformity hypotheses.

13. Accepted. 'The saving is at most' replaces wording that could imply a
    matching construction or tightness.

14. DISAGREE with the proposed iff as stated. The actual model permits
    same-row pairs. With identity recoding, u1, u2, u1 XOR u2 already have the
    required packet format, but their distinct row-parts are dependent.
    For a fully correct counterexample, broadcast every input bit as a singleton
    and add that same-row XOR; the code is valid on the entire input cube.
    Independence is a useful sufficient condition for homogeneous two-row
    binary-linear packets: extend the row-parts to dual bases. This sufficient
    test and the counterexample to necessity are included. A criterion for a
    more restricted packet format must not be stated as an iff for our model.

15. DISAGREE with automatic field coverage. Family 1 applies after an explicit
    finite binary encoding, with bit costs and any encoding deficit charged;
    an arbitrary field is not automatically a fixed-width bit alphabet.
    Family 2 requires the specified common recoding and coordinate-XOR format.
    General GF(2)- or extension-field linearity alone is insufficient. Added
    these qualifications instead of the proposed blanket extension.

16. Accepted. The companion summary now quantifies geometry over every eligible
