\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\paragraph{The question.} Let $b(d)=\max(1,\lceil\log_2d\rceil)$ and $L=d^2b(d)$. Statement~(T) asserts that one
deterministic multitape Turing machine transposes every $d\times d$ matrix with $b(d)$-bit entries, given and returned in
row-major order, within $O(L)$ steps. The obvious recursive algorithm takes $O(L\log d)$ steps. Whether (T) holds is
open; the series this paper belongs to collects the necessary conditions that a linear-time transposer must satisfy, and
the constructions they leave.

\paragraph{Where the series stands.} For oblivious machines, three kinds of results now constrain a transposer.
\emph{Depth}: the walk DAG of the work heads has cheap cuts of bounded depth whenever the heads enter every cell
boundedly often (Theorem~F of~\cite{BoundedNesting2026}); whether this holds at unbounded nesting is the open
statement $\TCP(2)$, and the deep-nesting pair is the test case~\cite{BoundedNesting2026,DirectedReciprocity2026}.
\emph{Coding}: given depth reduction, a transposer carries a near-full-rate code on cone relations that are
paired-distance separated, and Question~9.1 of \emph{Directed Recovery Structures in Tape
Computations}~\cite{DirectedRecovery2026}, the \emph{crux}, asks whether such codes have rate at most one half; it is
answered for many classes~\cite{SeventhPaper2026,DirectedReciprocity2026}. \emph{Source covers}: oblivious geometries with
bounded span overlap and few large return families are excluded outright~\cite{SourceCovers2026}, and the frozen gap-area
certificate cannot exclude the remaining two-exception inverse-square case at moduli comparable to the protected
rank~\cite{GapAreaMemo2026}. For adaptive machines, the eighth paper's Corollary~F$''$ excludes bounded-entry machines
whose traces carry less than $L/2$ bits~\cite[Corollary~6.4]{BoundedNesting2026}, a hypothesis that padding defeats.

\paragraph{This paper.} We show that, given the crux, every linear-time transposer revisits some tape unboundedly
often, that an oblivious one must do so through deep merges in its code, and that at block scale every transposer leads
to the same coding problem.
\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Merge depth replaces schedule depth} (\cref{sec:hop}). For an oblivious machine whose step values are linear
over a field, Theorems~HC and~HC$'$ split the transposition matrix into a part of small rank and a part supported on
balls of bounded radius whenever the machine's code merges deep data lineages only boundedly often along each path, on
\emph{every} schedule, however deeply nested and whatever its capacity. With the linear crux this excludes such
transposers (Corollary~HC), and with an affirmative answer to Question~9.1 it excludes them for all codes
(Corollary~HC$^*$). Routing through deep hops is hop-limited, capacity is not the operative resource, and the remaining
oblivious question becomes depth reduction of the code's merge structure (\cref{conj:HV}).
\item \emph{Skeleton entropy and input steering} (\cref{sec:skeleton}). Theorem~SE is a form of Corollary~F$''$
that is robust against padding of bounded entry, as the eighth paper asks: it bounds the entropy of the coarse skeleton
of the trace, the head positions rounded to blocks of a fixed width, rather than that of the trace. Its proof turns a
machine into a \emph{block machine} that is oblivious on each skeleton class. Two corollaries follow. No linear-time
transposer, adaptive or not, has bounded entry on every tape (Corollary~RV). A transposer whose work tapes have bounded
entry must therefore revisit input cells unboundedly often, and its input head must carry about $L/2$ bits of the data
in its coarse motion at every scale (Corollary~IS). Padding that revisits input cells meets this last condition at no
cost, so it is a necessary condition rather than an obstruction.
\item \emph{Localization} (\cref{sec:localization}). Theorem~L combines these: given the crux, every linear-time
transposer revisits some tape unboundedly often; if it is oblivious, it revisits a work tape and its code merges deep
lineages unboundedly often; and if its work tapes have bounded entry, it revisits input cells and carries about half of
the input in the coarse motion of its input head.
\item \emph{Unconditional reductions} (\cref{sec:reductions}). Machines of sufficiently low adaptivity are excluded
whenever one of their likely traces falls under the source-cover exclusions (Theorem~LA). At block scale every
linear-time transposer, adaptive or not, yields an external-memory transposition with linearly many I/Os that is
oblivious on $2^{L-O(d)}$ inputs (Theorem~EM). Lower bounds of this kind are known only for algorithms that move records
as indivisible atoms~\cite{AggarwalVitter1988} or under a network-coding conjecture~\cite{FHLS2019} that is false in
general~\cite{BravermanHe2026}; an indivisibility-free bound would refute (T) for every number of tapes.
\item \emph{Deep nesting} (\cref{sec:dnp}). Theorem~BP proves the eighth paper's Conjecture~A$''$ for block labels of the
deep-nesting pair at one odd shift, conditional on a conjecture that the straddle counts of most coarse classes concentrate as the shift varies
(\cref{conj:S}), with
partial results on that conjecture. It is evidence that the deep-nesting pair is depth-robust.
\item \emph{What a construction must contain} (\cref{sec:construction}). Put together, a linear-time transposer must
contain, at some scale, a near-full-rate code with a gain of order $\log d$ over routing on a network of the shape that
tapes produce: a causal code with depth-robust merges on deep hops, or adaptive revisits that the present methods do
not constrain further. Lemma~PG rules out
one natural building block, Tanner codes assembled from projective planes.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{How to read this draft.} Every numbered result carries a status line and a complete written proof. None has
been checked by an independent referee yet, and none is formalized in Lean yet; the formalization is in progress and will
replace the status lines result by result. Results that assume an affirmative answer to Question~9.1, the linear crux or
\cref{conj:S} say so in their statements. Claims that rest on a sketch appear only in remarks marked ``Proof sketch only'',
and numerical observations only in remarks marked as numerical. Results of earlier papers of the series are cited by
their numbers at a pinned revision, with their formal status there.

\paragraph{Related work.} The machine model, the dependency DAGs, genies, the cone lemma and the crux are those of the
fourth and eighth papers~\cite{FourthPaper2026,BoundedNesting2026}; the paired-distance theorem and Question~9.1 are from
the fifth~\cite{DirectedRecovery2026}; the source-cover exclusions are from the ninth~\cite{SourceCovers2026}; the
deep-nesting results build on the tenth~\cite{DirectedReciprocity2026}. Theorem~EM is a standard simulation of tapes in
external memory followed by the popular-trace argument that also underlies the non-oblivious case
of~\cite{FHLS2019}; its point here is the conclusion for transposition at memory $O(B)$.

reductions, \cref{sec:dnp} the block-pair theorem, \cref{sec:construction} states the constructive target, and
\cref{sec:open} lists open problems.
