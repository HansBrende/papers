Can a deterministic multitape Turing machine transpose an $n\times n$ matrix with $\lceil\log_2n\rceil$-bit
entries in time linear in the input length $L=n^2\lceil\log_2n\rceil$? Transposition is the data-movement step of FFT-based integer multiplication on tapes, and by a reduction of Harvey and van der Hoeven a
linear-time multiplier would give a linear-time transposer. The question is open. We study it against a frozen formal statement in Lean~4. Every result in this paper is machine-checked, except three explicitly marked propositions that are proved in full: two barrier results and a translation from gadgets to runs for XOR-frame recursions.

We prove that every transposer with at least one work tape running in time polynomial in $L$ needs $\Omega(\log n)$ passes in which all heads
move monotonically, whatever it does with its data (Theorems~P and~P$'$); that, along its most popular block-level
trace, the macroscopic work-head motion of every linear-time transposer satisfies an isoperimetric inequality that
forces it to fold back (Theorem~M*); and that both statements survive when the
machine is correct only on a $2^{-O(n\log n)}$ fraction of the inputs. We then formulate a statement about network coding: the \emph{Tape Coding Conjecture} asserts that complete exchange admits no
causal code on the block networks of linear-length tape diagrams, equivalently that coding beats multicommodity
flow there by a factor $o(\log n)$. We prove that the conjecture implies that no linear-time transposer exists,
give equivalent and weaker sufficient forms, prove that its plain form implies its robust form for several
classes of codes, and show why a proof must use the shape of tapes. Finally we show that, in an abstract model of XOR-frame recursion (the only technique claimed to beat
$O(L\log n)$), every run realizing a gadget makes at least as many frame changes as the gadget has wires, so that the recursion cannot reach linear time; and we describe what a construction would need.
