# Suggested corrections for "Local Coding Barriers and the Limits of Tape Geometry for Matrix Transposition" (commit 2c05e49)

Source: an independent review by three AI referees (local coding; head motion; padding and framing), plus a full rebuild and axiom audit. No theorem-level errors were found: every numbered statement matches its Lean declaration. The full build (10,069 jobs) passes once the modules in item 21 are built; 142/142 paper-cited and 6,313/6,313 support declarations use only propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound, or no axioms. The items below are about framing, attribution, precision and reproducibility. Line numbers refer to commit 2c05e49.

## A. Framing (should fix)

1. **Abstract (main.tex l.22–24).** "We prove six families of restrictions and constructions for matrix transposition on deterministic multitape Turing machines" — families 1, 2 and 5 are finite abstract coding models (no tapes, time or Transpose.TM); only families 3, 4 and 6 concern machines. Suggest: "restrictions for abstract local-coding models of transposition, and restrictions and a construction for multitape machines in the model of …". Also qualify "sharp deficit and helper coefficients" (here and "sharp in the stated model" in verified-results.txt): the witnesses (deficit_coefficient_at_least_two, helper_coefficient_at_least_two) are single d = 2 instances with D or H(Z) = Θ(P); they show no universal constant below 2 works, not sharpness in the D, H(Z) = o(P) regime where the theorem is used.
2. **padding.tex l.211–215 (barrier argument).** "if a linear transposer exists, the theorem supplies another linear transposer … Those necessary conditions therefore cannot by themselves exclude a linear transposer" is a non sequitur (if a linear transposer exists, nothing excludes it). The real content is unconditional: applied to a machine that halts at once, the theorem gives a fixed linear-time machine that transposes nothing yet satisfies the zero-slack cut inequality and the full M* conclusions. Rewrite in the form introduction.tex l.34–36 already uses ("can be satisfied without performing the transpose").
3. **padding.tex l.122 and the tape count in the padding theorem.** Disclose that the E·max(1, size g) extra tapes are idle (StationaryTapes: blank and at cell 0 for the whole run). They are needed because cut_after_prefix requires 4·rationalPageCount ≤ 3·k·η: the 3k·M_b memory-boundary term absorbs the expander edges lost at label changes. K₀ = 53 + rationalPageCount (UnconditionalPaidPadding.lean l.491). Note that the finite zero-slack CutIneq therefore relies on the coarse per-tape memory-boundary charge (an entry-cell charge would count about one cell per entry point for an idle tape), while the S = ∅ instances of (⋆), excess ≥ 1 and non-monotonicity hold for the moving heads alone.
4. **Construction relevance (introduction.tex l.55–57; padding.tex l.203–206).** "resolves the geometric and cost obligations" overstates. The first paper's design needs rulers that drive the encoder's heads so that the relation graph of the *data* is an expander, at every level of a geometric recursion; the wrapper's excursions carry no data and run once before the payload. What is proved is a data-free version of the first paper's design claims ("rulers can be written in linear time"; "(⋆) is a cost constraint"). Reword, add "coupling to data remains open", and fix the locator \cite[Construction programme]{FirstPaper2026}: the first paper has no such section (use Section 13, "The construction side").
5. **motion.tex l.628–635 (after the per-node corollary).** "it sharpens the geometric recursion proposal of [first paper]: contraction and a bounded number of stages per node cannot yield a transposer if every stage has the regularity …" is true but misleading by omission. The first paper's stages are driven along non-affine Möbius rulers; a non-affine Möbius map is affine on no interval, so those stage limits have no finite affine cover and the proposal escapes. The theorem only demands at least P/(2kG) exceptional ticks, roughly half a scan of the stored input. Say so, and say that what is excluded is variants whose choreographies are all affine.
6. **"Use" lines (motion.tex; verified-results.txt families 3 and 6).** Quantify how weak the requirement is: 1/(2G) of physical time is one head traversing half of the stored input, and every linear-time run admits a trivial one-node clock (J = 1, V = cP), so the clock hypothesis restricts nothing and the whole restriction lies in the regularity hypothesis.
7. **local-coding.tex l.614–616.** "its proposed recursion needs savings at both encoder and decoder" — the first paper says this only heuristically ("the necessity of both savings is argued here only informally"). Add the qualifier.

## B. Attribution

8. **External library (related-work.tex l.83–88; verified-results.txt).** Besides OAI.Falconer.Regularization.scalar_weak_radius_projection, the development imports the OAI modules ScalarDilationBound, ScalarPluennecke, ScalarBallCandidates, ScalarCoverCount and ScalarWeakRadiusProjection, and uses scalarCells, scalarBallMass, scalarZoom, scalar_zoom_cells, scalar_zoom_ball_bound, scalar_refined_ball_bound, scalar_ratio_family_ball and scalar_ratio_family_card. Say so in the prose (the attribution ledger already lists the modules). Credit the mathematics: the imported theorem is a form of Bourgain's discretized projection / sum–product theorem (Bourgain, GAFA 2003; J. Anal. Math. 2010), not only the formal source and Bourgain–Yehudayoff.
9. **Amenability (motion.tex, affine-time theorem and outline).** The key step is the Følner property of the affine group (solvable, hence amenable); the paper never mentions amenability or Følner sets. Credit them (and consider Lubotzky–Weiss, "Groups and expanders", for the amenability–expander link). This also explains why expanding Möbius motion, which generates a free non-amenable group, escapes.
10. **Related work (related-work.tex l.29–53).** The closest standard model for edge-local encoders is distributed / multi-sender index coding (each server knows a subset of the messages); only Ong–Ho–Lim is discussed. Please check that literature — possible leads (not verified): Sadeghi–Arbabjolfaei–Kim (2016); Li–Ong–Johnson (cooperative multi-sender index coding, c. 2019).

## C. Precision

11. **Cor 3.10 (paths and cycles) is subsumed by family 1, with a better constant.** Every RecodedXorCode local to G is a family-1 instance (constant helper; F = all inputs, so D = H(Z) = 0; query k is packet k, supported on an edge of G), so graph_worst_case_separator_bound gives B ≥ P − L(G,S) for every S. On a path or cycle, a separator every ≈√d vertices gives L ≈ 2·b·d^(3/2), against Cor 3.10's ≈ 2√2·b·d^(3/2) (ratio → 1/√2; at d = 100: 1810b vs 2471b). Present Cor 3.10 as an illustration and say that family 2's real addition is the bounded-degree (expander) corollary. The 4 → 3 → 2√2 refinements in verified-results.txt do not improve what the paper already proves.
12. **local-coding.tex l.284–286 ("make this restriction quantitative" for hyperfinite graphs).** State the corollary explicitly: removing εd vertices leaves components of size ≤ K(ε), so L ≤ εP + bK(ε)d. More generally, the first paper's question has a negative answer whenever tw(Γ) = o(d), which is strictly broader than hyperfinite.
13. **local-coding.tex l.408.** "the maximum possible saving is O(P/√d)" — no tightness is shown; write "the saving is at most O(P/√d)".
14. **local-coding.tex l.428–433.** State the exact membership criterion: a GF(2)-linear two-row broadcast is in family 2's model iff, for every row, the distinct nonzero row-parts of the functionals touching it are linearly independent (up to repetition and constant shifts). Sufficiency: extend them to an invertible map. Necessity: a dependency among coordinates of a bijection contradicts surjectivity.
15. **Field.** The first paper's question allows linear functionals over an unspecified field, while the Lean results are for bits. Family 1 covers any field (its queries are arbitrary functions of the rows' bits); family 2 covers GF(2)-linear (and GF(2^b) or GF(2)^b) functionals. One sentence where the results are related to that question.
16. **verified-results.txt l.148.** "Geometry is required only for selected input sequences eventually in the correct sets" — the Lean premise (hgeometry) requires it for every strictly increasing φ and every sequence eventually in W_i (motion.tex states this correctly). Suggest: "for all input sequences eventually in the correct sets, and only for those".
17. **motion.tex, physical-units theorem.** Its conclusions are assembled from several declarations; no single exported Lean statement contains them, and the common-region bound uses SatisfiesStar from the original theorem. Export a combined endpoint, or say the theorem is assembled. The common-region clause is formalized only in the "affine on each connected component" form (equivalent for open subsets of ℝ; say so). motion.tex l.42 attaches \formal{NativeAffineMotion}{…AffineTimeRequired} to a definition that does not define it.
18. **verified-results.txt, families 3 and 6.** The "Strengthening:" lines compare against internal source notes, not the first paper, which has no affine-motion theorem (only a prose remark about a common triangle wave). Say so, and note that the proof uses only the S = ∅, θ < 1/2 part of (⋆).
19. **introduction.tex l.32–33.** "its motion escapes the excluded class" is a corollary of the affine theorem applied to the padding's M* limits; label it as such.
20. **Stale names.** UnconditionalPaidPadding.lean l.9–10: the module docstring says "This file proves the reduction, not existence of the gap", but the merged file proves existence (exists_rational_restricted_gap, exists_rational_continuous_expander). verified-results.txt l.207 names a module RationalContinuousExpander that does not exist.

## D. Reproducibility and repository hygiene

21. **README build steps.** `lake build` (default target TransposeFollowup) does not build the first paper's modules that the audit files import (e.g. Transpose.Tape.Statements.Coding), so `lake env lean PaperAxioms.lean` fails with a missing .olean. Add them to a default target, or document building every module the audit files import before running the audits.
22. **verification/check_upstream.py** hashes everything under the original folder with rglob, so on a working tree with build outputs (.lake/), LaTeX intermediates or .DS_Store files it reports additions and exits 1. Restrict it to tracked files (git ls-files) or ignore build artifacts.
23. **Absolute local paths.** 182 tracked files in the follow-up (mostly verification records) contain absolute paths of the form external/research-workspace/…. Replace them with repository-relative paths before wider circulation.
24. **README l.16** "independently reviewed at source level": say the reviewers were AI agents (as verification.tex does) and that no human peer review has taken place.
25. **paper/prior-art.txt** reads as an internal memo ("STATUS: FALSE. Do not spend further work…", "SUGGESTED MANUSCRIPT WORDING", references to a research archive that is not included). Rewrite it as a public note or move it out of the public folder; its content checked out where tested.
