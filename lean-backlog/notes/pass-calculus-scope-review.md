# Scope checks for the pass calculus

Hans Brende and GPT-6 Astra, 13 November 2026.

These are written review notes on [the pass calculus](pass-calculus.md) at
`cf69b90`, checked independently in two reviews. They are not a new
formalization. The rank algebra, five-stage identity, fixed-size four-stage
obstruction, and intended parity upper bound survive. Several surrounding
claims need narrower hypotheses before they can describe the frontier.

## Repertoire, weight and stage size

- **Keep analytic weight separate from rank.** A product operator has
  Kronecker rank one but weight potentially as large as
  $\lVert A\rVert\lVert B\rVert$. Proposition 1(d) gives its uniform
  asymptotic conclusion when the product of stage weights stays bounded
  independently of $d$. Bounded repertoire alone does not make arbitrary
  product stages free for that conclusion. Unrestricted fixed-term Problem R
  remains open over $\mathbb C$ as well as over finite fields, as
  [item D](../D-comparison.md) states. Consequently
  unbounded repertoire is not an established necessary condition for every
  above-half pipeline without norm assumptions or a proof of R.
- A bounded list implies bounded repertoire; repertoire actually means the
  dimension of the span. For example, $U_r=I+rV$ can have many distinct
  values in a span of dimension two.
- Digit exchanges attain the rank-product lower bound among arbitrary
  bounded-Kronecker-rank stages. They are not row-controlled stages, so this
  does not prove tightness for the narrower controlled/product pipeline.
- Proposition 3 keeps every intermediate array $d\times d$. That restriction
  cannot be dropped. Allowing $d^2$ intermediate rows gives three stages:
  $A_1e_j=\sum_i e_{(i,j)}$, controlled block $E_{ji}$ at row $(i,j)$,
  and $A_2e_{(i,j)}=e_i$. Then

  $$
  (A_2\otimes I)\,\operatorname{diag}(E_{ji})\,(A_1\otimes I)
    =\sum_{i,j}E_{ij}\otimes E_{ji}=\tau.
  $$

- Corollary 4 does not need an added nonnegativity assumption on a
  real-valued $Q$. The factorization gives $Q(\tau)\le0$, while
  $\tau^2=I$ and $Q(I)=0$ give $0\le2Q(\tau)$.
- Handle zero Kronecker rank separately in Proposition 1(b). At finite $d$,
  rate $1-\varepsilon$ forces a rank product of at least
  $1/(2\varepsilon+1/d)$; $1/(2\varepsilon)$ is the fixed-$\varepsilon$
  asymptotic expression.

## Address profiles and tape cost

The displayed shear profiles differ: at $d=8$ they are $(3,7,8,4,2)$ and
$(2,4,8,4,2)$. Their log ranks are comparable, which preserves the useful
observation that one cheap shear has large rank at all standard cuts. It
does not prove that no function of the profile separates the two. The
coordinatewise domination rules out charging the column shear more using a
coordinatewise increasing aggregate of these ranks.

An unshuffle need not have bounded rank across an arbitrary choice of address
digits. A cyclic permutation of four row-address bits crosses all four bits
at the partition $\{0,2\}\mid\{1,3\}$ and has operator Kronecker rank 16
there. The constant bound at standard contiguous cuts does not establish
the stronger arbitrary-digit-order claim.

The Fourier identity is algebraically exact under its root-of-unity and
characteristic hypotheses. Coefficient-ring operation counts for phase
multiplication or a chirp do not by themselves establish linear bit time on
a fixed-alphabet tape. Root representation, coefficient sizes, and
multiplication costs remain part of that question. Bluestein uses chirp
factors as well as convolution, with appropriate root or padding conditions;
a DFT is not itself a single translation-invariant convolution.

Likewise [Paper 17's monotone-expander gather construction](../../Order-Symmetry-and-the-Swap-A-Synthesis-of-Matrix-Transposition-on-Tapes/paper/sections/symmetry.tex) takes its marking
strings as supplied. Their linear-time generation is explicitly left open,
and gathering rows does not make arbitrary row functions cheap. Expansion
obstructs the order upper bound; it does not construct an above-half code.
The proposed growing-repertoire expander family is a target, not a proved
exhaustive description of all remaining machines.

## Parity: sufficient tests, not a converse

Proposition 5 should say dimension **at most** $(1/2+o(1))p^2$, and bound
the absolute values of coefficients. Its three ambient character tests are
sufficient. Their failure does not imply an odd relation in the subgroup
generated by the selected matrices.

For example, $R=ST^3$ has infinite order and $PRP=R^{-1}$, so
$\langle P,R\rangle$ is infinite dihedral. The character
$R^nP^e\mapsto(-1)^{n+e}$ sends both chosen generators to $-1$, although
none of the three ambient characters does. Even the claimed mixed-parity
example has exceptions: for $\{P,ST^3,ST^6\}$, reduction modulo 3 lands in
$\langle P,S\rangle\cong D_8$; the character
$S^iP^e\mapsto(-1)^{i+e}$ again sends all three chosen generators to $-1$.

The useful strengthening is to require a character on the **generated
subgroup**, rather than one that extends to all of $GL_2(\mathbb Z)$.
The selected $ST^{-1}$ family really does have an odd relation:
$I+M+M^2=0$ implies that every affine map $x\mapsto Mx+t$ has cube equal
to the identity.

Integral parity need not descend modulo odd $p$. For example, every affine
$ST^2$ has $p$-th power equal to the identity modulo $p$. Thus the script's
unconditional parity assertion needs the short-word qualification. The
printed cutoff with $\max|b|=2$ permits no positive word length at
$p=7,11,13$. It is an asymptotic proof, not an exact finite acyclic bound.

For completeness, generators and inverses have infinity norm at most
$1+\max|b|$. A nonidentity integral linear word of fixed length therefore
remains nonidentity modulo all sufficiently large primes, and has at most
$p$ fixed points. With $H=(T+T^*)/2$ and $\Gamma=w\kappa$, expansion gives
$|\operatorname{tr}H^k|\le\Gamma^k p$ for the admissible odd lengths.
Choose the odd approximation polynomial first, then take $p$ sufficiently
large for its finite degree. This supplies the intended upper bound without
assuming that code vectors are eigenvectors of $H$.

There is also a finite version that does not require parity. Let $F_k$ be
the sum of fixed-point counts over all $(2w)^k$ signed helper words, retaining
multiplicities. Then

$$
|\operatorname{tr}H^k|\le(\kappa/2)^k F_k.
$$

Actual word counts can replace the coarse $\Gamma^k p$ estimate. This is a
possible later test for bounded-coefficient complex codes, not a proof of
the finite-field or nonlinear statement.

The [matched graph experiment](shear-loop-controls.md) stays valid with all
these qualifications. Its first finite exception is explained completely by
self-loops; it supplies no asymptotic counterexample to the parity theorem.
