A single-pass concentration endpoint was implemented and passed targeted

shared concentration theorem under explicit trimming and concentration

showed that this ORDER of operations is unsuitable for the intended scales.
The conditional theorem was true, but its required trimming inequality
could not be made small in that regime. The unused specialization was

Why the budget fails:
For the trace-square inverse, Kinv=3072/(lambda*s), lambda<=32.
The final scalar spacing requires2sigma>=Kinv*delta_s. In the intended
regime delta_s>=eta this forces sigma/eta>=48/s. Hence the coarse
retention factor C=6561(4sigma/eta+1)^4 satisfies C*s^4>=1.
Its CUBIC normalization cost therefore contains s^-12.
Meanwhile Anear has decay only s^kappa. With the usual nonnegative profile
bounds E>=1,K,J>=1,eta<=1,gamma,kappa>=0,R>=1, one has Anear>=s^kappa.
Taking the harmless larger fixed constants/envelopes ensures W,L>=1.
The newly proved obstruction applies for kappa<=12, covering our range.
Thus decreasing s to remove the trace-square critical point cannot make
this single-pass trimming estimate succeed. Earlier optimism about just
choosing smaller exponents overlooked that sigma itself depends on1/s.

This DOES NOT refute the R1848 conditional sampler. It also does not rule
out special inputs or spacings outside the stated regime. It says not to
try to close the main argument with this coupled single-pass budget.
