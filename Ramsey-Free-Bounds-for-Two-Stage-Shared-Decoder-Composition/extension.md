### 1. Honest Verdict on the Machine Wall `(T)` ([wall.tex](../Order-Symmetry-and-the-Swap-A-Synthesis-of-Matrix-Transposition-on-Tapes/paper/sections/wall.tex))

**Did we find a way through the Turing machine wall `(T)`?**
**No.** None of the results below solves `(T)`, and we see sharply why any purely subspace-level one-hop or two-stage argument still stops short of machines:

1. **Nonlinear set fibers vs. linear subspaces ([open.tex](../Order-Symmetry-and-the-Swap-A-Synthesis-of-Matrix-Transposition-on-Tapes/paper/sections/open.tex#L113-L120), Paper 19 [appendix-set-capacity.tex](../Capacity-and-Composition-of-Shared-Transposition-Decoders/paper/sections/appendix-set-capacity.tex))**: Freezing genie advice or a cut transcript on a finite-alphabet machine produces a *nonlinear subset* $C_w \subseteq \Sigma^{d \times d}$, not a linear subspace. Paper 19 (`Theorem A.1` and `Corollary A.2`) proves that shared decoders with unbounded window fail on general sets; any machine bridge must use *bounded window $w$* (`Problem 9.4(b)`).
2. **Per-entry cut labels from spine edges ([sweep.tex](paper/sections/sweep.tex))**: Even at bounded cone depth $D$, spine edges $u \to u+1$ assign cut patterns per output entry rather than per column class, and generic random-order deficits $1 - 1/(\Delta + 1)$ fail at machine parameters $\Delta \asymp b(k+1)^D$ against cut rate $\varepsilon \asymp 1/D$ (Paper 4, `Proposition 4.14(c)`).
3. **Curved multi-scale motion & separated high-rate codes**: Real schedules curve head velocities across $\Theta(\log d)$ scales, and Papers 13 and 15 construct separated bounded-degree codes above half rate (and rate $\to 1$), so graph-theoretic degree/separation alone cannot rule out linear-time transposition without exploiting the geometric structure of 1D head trajectories.

### 2. Three Genuine New Results on Paper 17's Open Problems ([open.tex](../Order-Symmetry-and-the-Swap-A-Synthesis-of-Matrix-Transposition-on-Tapes/paper/sections/open.tex))

Within Paper 17's **linear one-hop model** ([symmetry.tex](../Order-Symmetry-and-the-Swap-A-Synthesis-of-Matrix-Transposition-on-Tapes/paper/sections/symmetry.tex#L56-L64)), however, we found the exact missing ingredient that Paper 19 (`SharedDecoders2026`) left on the table, yielding **three non-vacuous, gap-free theorems**:

---

#### Result 1 — Breaking Paper 19's $\frac{41}{64}$ Bound and $\frac{5}{8}$ Certificate Barrier on `Problem 9.1(a)`

* **Why Paper 19 hit a ceiling**: Paper 19 ([capacity.tex](../Capacity-and-Composition-of-Shared-Transposition-Decoders/paper/sections/capacity.tex#L1-L76)) proved $D(\mathcal{P}) \le \frac{41}{64} b d^2 + \frac{5}{3} b d^{3/2}$ for two classes (`Theorem 3.4`), and proved in its Appendix C (`Theorem C.6`, [appendix-mask-methods.tex](../Capacity-and-Composition-of-Shared-Transposition-Decoders/paper/sections/appendix-mask-methods.tex#L223-L231)) that its nested-component translate certificate method **cannot go below $(\frac{5}{8} - o(1)) b d^2$** ($\frac{5}{8} = \frac{40}{64} = 0.625$) on almost every two-class partition. That barrier exists because Paper 19's `Theorem 3.2` only uses $C_E \subseteq H_E$, which holds for **arbitrary nonlinear deterministic decoders** $f_J : V \to (F^b)^d$.
* **The missing mechanism (Run-Extension + Polynomial Vanishing for Linear Decoders)**: In Paper 17's `Definition 6.3` ([symmetry.tex](../Order-Symmetry-and-the-Swap-A-Synthesis-of-Matrix-Transposition-on-Tapes/paper/sections/symmetry.tex#L56-L64)), the decoders $\mathcal{A}_m : \mathrm{Mat}_d(F^b) \to (F^b)^{\mathbb{Z}/d}$ are **linear**. Consider the single-shift intersection $U = \mathcal{X} \cap \sigma^{-s} \mathcal{X} \subseteq H_{\{0, s\}}$. Along each cycle $O_a$ of $c \mapsto c + s$ ($0 \le a < g_s = \gcd(d, s)$, length $n = d / g_s$), on a maximal monochromatic run $I_r = \{c_r, c_r + s, \dots, c_r + (\ell_r - 1)s\} \subseteq J_m$:
  - $X \in \mathcal{X}$ gives $\mathcal{A}_m(\sigma^{c_r + t s} X) = \sigma^{t s} x^{(a)}_r$ for $0 \le t \le \ell_r - 1$;
  - $\sigma^s X \in \mathcal{X}$ evaluated at the right endpoint $c_r + (\ell_r - 1)s \in J_m$ gives **one extra linear equation** $\mathcal{A}_m(\sigma^{c_r + \ell_r s} X) = \sigma^{\ell_r s} x^{(a)}_r$ at $t = \ell_r$ ($\ell_r + 1$ points total).
  - Whenever $\gcd(n, \mathrm{char}\,F) = 1$, passing to $\overline{F}$ with a primitive $n$-th root of unity $\omega_n$ and filtering $U$ by the $\sigma^s$-eigenspaces $E_\mu$ ($\mu = 0, \dots, n-1$): at step $\mu$, on each cycle $O_a$, the $(\overline{F}^b)$-valued polynomial $P_{m,a}(Z)$ of degree $\le n - 1 - \mu$ is constant across all $\ell_r + 1$ points of each run of $J_m \cap O_a$, so $Q(Z) = P_{m,a}(\omega_n Z) - P_{m,a}(Z) = Z R(Z)$ ($\deg R \le n - 2 - \mu$) vanishes at all $d_{m,a} = |J_m \cap O_a|$ roots of unity in $J_m \cap O_a$.
* **Exact Single-Shift Bound (`Theorem 1A`)**: Summing $\min(T_{m,a}, 1 + \max(0, n - 1 - d_{m,a} - \mu))$ over $\mu = 0, \dots, n-1$ (where $T_{m,a} = |\{c \in J_m \cap O_a : c + s \notin J_m\}| \le n - d_{m,a}$) gives, for **every** $M \ge 1$, lane width $b \ge 1$, shift $s \in \mathbb{Z}/d$, and field $F$ with $\gcd(d/\gcd(d, s), \mathrm{char}\,F) = 1$:
  $$\dim(\mathcal{X} \cap \sigma^{-s} \mathcal{X}) \;\le\; b g_s \sum_{a=0}^{g_s - 1} \sum_{m=0}^{M - 1} \left( T_{m,a}(n - d_{m,a}) - \frac{1}{2} T_{m,a}^2 + \frac{1}{2} T_{m,a} \right) + b g_s d.$$
  - **Sharpness of `Theorem 1A`**: On the alternating coloring $p(c) = c \bmod 2$ ($d = 2m$, $b = 1$, $s = 1$), `Theorem 1A` gives $\dim(\mathcal{X} \cap \sigma^{-1} \mathcal{X}) \le m^2 + 3m = \frac{1}{4} d^2 + \frac{3}{2} d$ (vs. $\dim H_{\{0,1\}} = 2m \cdot 2m = d^2$), and an explicit symmetric Fourier decoder attains $\dim(\mathcal{X} \cap \sigma^{-1} \mathcal{X}) = m^2 + 3m$ **with equality**.
* **Universal Two-Class & Multi-Class Ceilings (`Theorem 1B`)**:
  - **Two classes ($M = 2$, arbitrary $d \ge 1$ with $\gcd(d, \mathrm{char}\,F) = 1$)**: Applying Cauchy–Schwarz across the $g_s$ orbits cancels $g_s$ in the quadratic term:
    $$\dim(\mathcal{X} \cap \sigma^{-s} \mathcal{X}) \;\le\; b\left(\frac{1}{2} Q_s d - \frac{1}{4} Q_s^2 + \frac{1}{2} g_s Q_s + g_s d\right),$$
    where $Q_s = |\{c \in \mathbb{Z}/d : p(c) \ne p(c+s)\}|$. Averaging uniformly over $s \in \mathbb{Z}/d$ ($\mathbb{E}_s[Q_s] = 2\alpha(1-\alpha)d \le d/2$, $\mathbb{E}_s[Q_s^2] \ge (\mathbb{E}_s[Q_s])^2$, $\mathbb{E}_s[g_s] \le 2\sqrt{d}$) yields:
    $$D(\mathcal{P}) \;\le\; \frac{19}{32}\, b d^2 + \frac{3}{2}\, b d^{3/2} \qquad \bigl(\gcd(d, \mathrm{char}\,F) = 1\bigr).$$
    Here $\frac{19}{32} = \frac{38}{64} = 0.59375$, strictly below both Paper 19's $\frac{41}{64} = 0.640625$ bound and Paper 19's $\frac{5}{8} = \frac{40}{64} = 0.625$ nonlinear-decoder barrier.
  - **$M \ge 3$ classes (prime $d \ne \mathrm{char}\,F$)**: In Paper 17's [open.tex](../Order-Symmetry-and-the-Swap-A-Synthesis-of-Matrix-Transposition-on-Tapes/paper/sections/open.tex#L16-L19), Paper 19's `Theorem 3.5` only beat Theorem FL's $(1 - \frac{1}{2M}) d^2$ for $M \ge 11$. Averaging `Theorem 1A` over $s \in (\mathbb{Z}/d)^\times$ for prime $d \ne \mathrm{char}\,F$ (where strict concavity of $f(\alpha) = \alpha(1-\alpha)^2(1 - \alpha/2)$ on $[0, 1/2]$, together with a direct check when one coordinate exceeds $1/2$, forces the unique global maximum on the simplex at $\alpha_m = 1/M$) yields:
    $$D(\mathcal{P}) \;\le\; \left(1 - \frac{5}{4M} + \frac{1}{M^2} - \frac{1}{4M^3}\right) b d^2 + O(b M d),$$
    which strictly beats Theorem FL's $1 - \frac{1}{2M}$ for **every $M \ge 2$** (e.g., $\frac{37}{54} \approx 0.6852$ vs. $\frac{5}{6} \approx 0.8333$ at $M = 3$; $\frac{191}{256} \approx 0.7461$ vs. $\frac{7}{8} = 0.875$ at $M = 4$).
  - **Honest scope caveat**: Because the polynomial-roots step requires distinct $n_s$-th roots of unity in $\overline{F}$, `Theorem 1B` requires $\gcd(d, \mathrm{char}\,F) = 1$ (for example, it does *not* apply at $d = 2^m$ in $\mathrm{char}\,F = 2$; checking Mahler binomial polynomials $\binom{c}{j} \bmod 2$ confirmed they can vanish at $> j$ points modulo $2^m$). Also, $\frac{19}{32}$ narrows the gap toward $\frac{1}{2} + o(1)$ in `Problem 9.1(a)` without yet closing it to $\frac{1}{2}$.

#### Result 2 — Exact Solution of `Problem 9.1(b)` in Every Characteristic $\notin \{2, 3\}$

* **Problem statement ([open.tex](../Order-Symmetry-and-the-Swap-A-Synthesis-of-Matrix-Transposition-on-Tapes/paper/sections/open.tex#L21-L23))**: Determine $D(\{0, 1, 3\}, \{2, 4, 5\})$ on $\mathbb{Z}/6$. Previously known: $25 \le D \le 28$ over every field with $3 \ne 0$ (the lower bound $25$ is `Theorem A` / `exists_code_of_three_ne_zero` in [fields.tex](../Order-Symmetry-and-the-Swap-A-Synthesis-of-Matrix-Transposition-on-Tapes/memo-class-uniform-codes/paper/sections/fields.tex#L26-L31) of the memo).
* **What we proved (`Theorem 2`)**: Over **every** field $F$ with $\mathrm{char}\,F \notin \{2, 3\}$, every code $\mathcal{X}$ of a class-uniform rule for $\{0, 1, 3\} \mid \{2, 4, 5\}$ satisfies $\dim(\mathcal{X} \cap \sigma^{-1} \mathcal{X}) \le 14$, and therefore $2 \dim \mathcal{X} \le 36 + 14 = 50$, i.e., $\dim \mathcal{X} \le 25$. Combined with the memo's `Theorem A`,
  $$D\bigl(\{0, 1, 3\},\, \{2, 4, 5\}\bigr) = 25 \qquad \text{for every field } F \text{ with } \mathrm{char}\,F \notin \{2, 3\}.$$
* **Complete proof structure**:
  1. Decompose $H_{\{0,1\}} = \bigoplus_{\eta \in \mathbb{Z}/6} V_\eta$ ($\dim V_\eta = 4$) into column-Fourier modes over $\overline{F}$ ($\zeta$ a primitive $6$th root of unity). On a single mode $V_\eta$, $\sigma^c X = \zeta^{c\eta} X$, forcing $K_\eta := W \cap V_\eta \subseteq L_\eta := H_2^* \cap V_\eta$ ($\dim L_\eta = 2$), so $S_K := \sum_{\eta=0}^5 \dim K_\eta \le 12$.
  2. For each antipodal complement $S_a = \mathbb{Z}/6 \setminus \{a, a+3\}$ ($a \in \{0, 1, 2\}$), antipodal cancellation at $\eta = a, a+3$ together with nonvanishing of all six coefficients of $(Z^6 - 1)/(Z - \zeta^{c_0})$ forces $W_{S_a} = \bigoplus_{\eta \in S_a} K_\eta$. Hence the quotient projection $\bar{\pi}_a \oplus \bar{\pi}_{a+3} : Q \to (V_a / K_a) \oplus (V_{a+3} / K_{a+3})$ is injective on $Q = W / K$; summing over $a \in \{0, 1, 2\}$ gives $3 \dim Q + S_K \le 24$. This already proves $\dim W = S_K + \dim Q \le S_K + \lfloor(24 - S_K)/3\rfloor \le 14$ whenever $S_K \le 10$.
  3. Using the exact anti-diagonal zeros $\rho_{0, \eta+3, \eta} = \rho_{1, \eta+3, \eta} = 0$ of the $\mathbb{Z}/6$ DFT interpolation matrices to eliminate the unconstrained point ($c = 5$ for Color $0$, $c = 1$ for Color $1$), pairwise kernel disjointness $N_a \cap N_b = \{0\}$ closes $S_K = 11$ ($\dim Q \ge 4 \implies U = N_1 \oplus N_4 \implies \Lambda_1 = \mathrm{M}_1 = 0 \implies N_2 \subseteq N_1$, contradiction) and reduces $S_K = 12$ ($\dim Q \ge 3$) to a single parameter-free $4 \times 4$ determinant over $\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_6)$ whose value is literally $\frac{16}{3} \ne 0$ (with denominator norms dividing $3$ only).
  - **Honest scope caveat**: Over $\mathrm{char}\,F = 2$ ($25 \le D \le 28$) and $\mathrm{char}\,F = 3$ ($24 \le D \le 28$, `Problem 6.1` of the memo), $Z^6 - 1$ has repeated roots ($(Z^3 - 1)^2$ and $(Z^2 - 1)^3$), so those two characteristics remain open.

#### Result 3 — Characteristic-Free Two-Sided Coarse–Fine Bound on `Problem 9.3` (`prob:both`)

* **Problem statement ([open.tex](../Order-Symmetry-and-the-Swap-A-Synthesis-of-Matrix-Transposition-on-Tapes/paper/sections/open.tex#L106-L110))**: Bound $\dim\ker(\mathrm{sw} - K)$ when *both* $A_a$ and $B_a$ lie in the span of dyadic interval masks composed with rotations with profile $(M_j)_j$, improving on bounds that treat only one side and one scale at a time.
* **What we proved (`Theorem 3`, over *every* field $F$ of *every* characteristic)**: Partition the intervals used on side $A$ into a "fine" union $S_A \subseteq \mathbb{Z}/d$ ($|S_A| = s_A$, consisting of $m_A$ cyclic runs) and its complement $L_A = \mathbb{Z}/d \setminus S_A$ partitioned into $c_A$ coarse intervals, and similarly $S_B, L_B$ ($s_B, m_B, c_B$) on side $B$. Writing $K' = K - (P_{S_A} \otimes P_{S_B}) K$ ($\mathrm{rank}(K - K') \le s_A s_B$), every $X \in \mathcal{Y} = \ker(\mathrm{sw} - K')$ vanishes identically on $S_B \times S_A$; intersecting first with $(1 \otimes \sigma)^{-1}\mathcal{Y}$ inside $V_0 + (1 \otimes \sigma)^{-1} V_0$ (controlling $L_B \times \mathbb{Z}/d$ in $\le c_B d$ parameters) and then intersecting the residual space $\mathcal{Z}_0 \subseteq F^{S_B \times L_A}$ with $(\sigma \otimes 1)^{-1}\mathcal{Z}_0$ (controlling $S_B \times L_A$ via the $A$-side intervals $L_A$ in $\le c_A s_B$ parameters) yields:
  $$\dim\ker(\mathrm{sw} - K) \;\le\; \frac{1}{2} d^2 + \frac{1}{4} s_B(d - s_A) + \frac{1}{2} s_A s_B + \frac{1}{2} c_B d + \frac{1}{4} c_A s_B + \frac{1}{2} s_B m_A + \frac{1}{4} m_B(d - s_A)$$
  (and symmetrically with $A \leftrightarrow B$ swapped).
  - **Concrete win over one-sided `Theorem TP`**: When both sides have $s_A = \alpha d$ and $s_B = \beta d$ in fine intervals (e.g., $\alpha d$ contiguous singletons plus one coarse interval of length $(1-\alpha)d$ on both sides, so $c_A = c_B = m_A = m_B = 1$), one-sided `Theorem TP` gives $(\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\min(\alpha, \beta)) d^2 + O(d)$ ($= \frac{3}{4} d^2$ at $\alpha = \beta = 1/2$), whereas `Theorem 3` gives:
    $$\left(\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{4}\min\bigl(\beta(1 + \alpha),\, \alpha(1 + \beta)\bigr)\right) d^2 + O(d),$$
    which is **strictly smaller for every $\alpha, \beta \in (0, 1)$** (at $\alpha = \beta = 1/2$, $\frac{11}{16} d^2 + d = 0.6875\, d^2 + d < \frac{3}{4} d^2$).
  - **Numerical observation (labelled explicitly as numerical evidence, not proved)**: Across all small two-sided interval grids we tested via SVD over $\mathbb{R}$ ($d \in \{5, 6, 7, 8\}$), the true maximum of $\dim\ker(\mathrm{sw} - K)$ matched the sharper formula $\bigl\lfloor \frac{1}{2}\bigl(d^2 + \sum_{\alpha, \beta} \min(|I_\alpha|, |J_\beta|)\bigr) \bigr\rfloor$ (giving $\frac{5}{8} d^2 + O(d)$ at $\alpha = \beta = 1/2$, attained by swapping one boundary entry per singleton), which remains an attractive open target to prove cleanly.
