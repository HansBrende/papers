Depth reduction turns an oblivious tape computation of matrix transposition into an index code whose side information consists of bounded dependency cones. A coding lower bound is useful only if its hypotheses follow from those actual cones. For fixed-depth cones of linear-time native schedules, we prove the following structural statement: after conditioning on heavily reused input coordinates, the input--demand incidence graph has bounded degree, and only $O(L/\log d)$ matched input and output ports lie at any fixed graph distance, where $L=d^2b$ is the bit length of a $d\times d$ matrix. The statement concerns the full syntactic dependency graph and requires no correctness assumption on the computation.

For bounded-degree directed incidence systems with this separation property, we prove a half-rate bound for linear storage codes over the complex numbers with fixed block size and uniformly bounded recovery coefficients. For arbitrary finite alphabets, we prove a half-rate bound under uniform hyperfiniteness. Native examples show that hyperfiniteness and separation of matched ports into distinct connected components cannot be imposed universally: inexpensive tape executions produce robust expanding incidence and many connected demands. Nevertheless, their full cone relations admit nonlinear coding bounds, including a positive rate deficit independent of the fixed depth parameter. An appendix gives matrix certificates over arbitrary fields and applies them to a separate expanding family in characteristic two.

These results identify a directed coding problem naturally forced by tape geometry. The general finite-alphabet coding bound, unrestricted cheap depth reduction, and an all-input treatment of adaptive schedules remain open; no unrestricted transposition time bound follows here.

The conditioning, fibre, independent-coordinate, rectangle, threshold-pullback, port-color, component, separator, damaged-expander, rank-identity, matrix-label, and hyperfinite results are formalized in Lean. The paired-distance bound, self-demands, degree trimming, depth-independent code deficit, bounded-lift torsion, and expanding-family linear-code corollary are partially formalized. The remaining native, complex-analytic, and expanding-family construction arguments have written proofs.
