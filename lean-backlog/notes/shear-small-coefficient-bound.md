# An explicit small-coefficient bound for the three-helper shear family

Hans Brende and GPT-6 Astra, 13 November 2026.

**Status:** written proof, independently reviewed; not Lean-verified.
This supplies a rigorous threshold for the $N_3$ family in the
[pass-calculus note](pass-calculus.md). Below that threshold the rate tends
to zero, a stronger conclusion than its half-rate criterion. Coefficients
near 1, finite-field coefficients, and the main tape question remain open.
The argument uses elementary free-group and moment estimates; no novelty
claim about those tools is made.

## Statement

Let $p$ range through primes and put $V_p=(\mathbb Z/p)^2$. The three
helper maps have linear parts

$$
P=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
A=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
U=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&2\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Their translations may be arbitrary and may depend on $p$. Let $T_p$ be
a complex linear decoder which, at each position, uses those three helpers
with coefficients of absolute value at most $\kappa$. Coefficients may
also depend on position and on $p$. Write $D_p=\dim\ker(I-T_p)$; any
linear storage code using that decoder has dimension at most $D_p$.

Put

$$
\beta=\sqrt{32+2\sqrt3},\qquad
\kappa_0=\frac2\beta=0.335842391894\ldots.
$$

For every integer $k\ge1$ with $3^{2k}+1<p$,

$$
\boxed{\quad
\frac{D_p}{p^2}\le
\left(\frac{\kappa\beta}{2}\right)^{2k}
+\frac{(3\kappa)^{2k}}p.
\quad} \tag{1}
$$

Consequently $D_p=o(p^2)$ if $\kappa<\kappa_0$. At
$\kappa=\kappa_0$, $D_p\le(1/2+o(1))p^2$.
These asymptotic bounds are uniform over the allowed coefficients and
translations. The range $1/3\le\kappa<\kappa_0$ is nonempty; for
$3\kappa<1$ the norm bound already gives $D_p=0$ exactly.
At coefficients $1/3$ on each helper, constant vectors are fixed, so the
nonempty range above $1/3$ includes nonzero codes.

**Exceptional output positions.** Allow arbitrary coefficients on a set
$E_p$ of output positions, with the bound $\kappa$ only outside $E_p$.
Then the right side of (1) increases by at most $|E_p|/p^2$. For fixed
$\kappa<\kappa_0$ this gives

$$
D_p\le |E_p|+o(p^2). \tag{1a}
$$

Thus a family of rate at least $\rho>0$ must have at least
$(\rho-o(1))p^2$ output positions with some coefficient larger in magnitude
than any fixed $\kappa<\kappa_0$. This is a necessary condition within
this helper family, not a claim about the density of all possible transposers.

To prove it, zero the exceptional rows to obtain $T_p^0$. The difference
$T_p-T_p^0$ has rank at most $|E_p|$, and
$\dim\ker(I-T_p)\le\dim\ker(I-T_p^0)+|E_p|$. Apply (1) to $T_p^0$.

## Proof

### A bound for the integral walk

In the group generated by $P,A,U$, the elements

$$
g=A^{-1}U=\begin{pmatrix}1&3\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
h=UA^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\-3&1\end{pmatrix}
$$

freely generate a free group of rank two. Here is the elementary
ping-pong check on the real projective line. Set
$X=\{x:|x|>1\}$, including infinity, and $Y=\{x:|x|<1\}$.
For every nonzero integer $j$, $g^j(Y)\subset X$ since
$g^j(x)=x+3j$, and $h^j(X)\subset Y$ since
$h^j(x)=x/(1-3jx)$. These inclusions give the free-product normal form
for the two infinite cyclic subgroups.

On the left regular representation of the integral group, let

$$
S=2\lambda_P+\lambda_A+\lambda_{A^{-1}}
                 +\lambda_U+\lambda_{U^{-1}}.
$$

The coefficient 2 retains both signed occurrences of the involution $P$.
Among the 36 word terms in $S^2$ are
$\lambda_g,\lambda_{g^{-1}},\lambda_h,\lambda_{h^{-1}}$, once each;
the other 32 terms have total norm at most 32. The sum of these four
selected terms has norm at most $2\sqrt3$. To see the latter bound,
restrict to a coset of the free subgroup: its Cayley graph is a 4-regular
tree. Orient the tree toward one end, so every vertex has one parent and
three children. Apply

$$
2|xy|\le |x|^2/\sqrt3+\sqrt3|y|^2
$$

on each parent-child edge. Summing bounds the adjacency quadratic form
by $2\sqrt3$ times the squared norm. The same bound holds on every coset.
Since $S$ is self-adjoint,

$$
\lVert S\rVert^2=\lVert S^2\rVert\le32+2\sqrt3=\beta^2. \tag{2}
$$

Let $N_\ell$ be the number of signed integral words of length $\ell$
equal to the identity, with the six letters counted with multiplicity.
Then $N_\ell=\langle\delta_e,S^\ell\delta_e\rangle\le\beta^\ell$.

### Transfer to the finite helper maps

Each linear generator and inverse has infinity norm at most 3. Therefore,
if $3^\ell+1<p$, a nonidentity integral linear word of length $\ell$
remains nonidentity modulo $p$. Its affine fixed-point equation has at
most $p$ solutions, whatever translation the word acquires. A word with
identity linear part has at most $p^2$ fixed points. Thus the number
$F_{p,\ell}$ of signed closed helper walks, retaining multiplicities,
satisfies

$$
F_{p,\ell}\le p^2N_\ell+p(6^\ell-N_\ell)
\le p^2\beta^\ell+p6^\ell. \tag{3}
$$

This counts walks rather than distinct graph edges: a repeated helper or
two opposite orientations of one edge are counted separately.

### Even moments bound the code

Put $H_p=(T_p+T_p^*)/2$. Its norm is at most $3\kappa$. On
$\ker(I-T_p)$ its quadratic form equals the squared norm, so spectral
projection onto the eigenvalues at least 1 is injective there. For every
$k\ge1$ it follows that

$$
D_p\le\#\{\lambda(H_p)\ge1\}
\le\operatorname{tr}(H_p^{2k})
\le(\kappa/2)^{2k}F_{p,2k}.
$$

The last inequality expands the trace into signed helper walks and bounds
each product of coefficients by $\kappa^{2k}$. Combining with (3) proves
(1).

If $\kappa<\kappa_0$, take
$k=\lfloor\log p/(4\log3)\rfloor$. For all sufficiently large $p$ the
height condition holds, the first term of (1) tends to zero, and the
second is at most $p^{-1/2}$ because $\kappa<1$. This proves zero rate.

At the endpoint, every fixed even normalized moment has limit superior
at most 1. Moreover $|\operatorname{tr}H_p|/p^2\le3\kappa/p$:
each of the three nonidentity linear helper maps has at most $p$
self-loops. Fix $\delta>0$, and let $t_p$ be the fraction of eigenvalues
below $-1-\delta$. Even moments give
$\limsup_p t_p\le(1+\delta)^{-2k}$ for every fixed $k$.
Writing $m_p$ for the fraction of eigenvalues at least 1 and using
$\lVert H_p\rVert\le\Gamma=3\kappa_0$, the first moment gives

$$
m_p\le\frac{1+\delta+|\operatorname{tr}H_p|/p^2+\Gamma t_p}{2+\delta}.
$$

Take $p\to\infty$, then $k\to\infty$, and then $\delta\to0$.
This proves the endpoint half-rate bound. ∎

## What this corrects and what it leaves

Counts at one prime through word length seven do not bound the limiting
growth parameter in Proposition 6 of the pass-calculus note. That parameter
takes the dimension limit first, then the odd-word-length limit. At any
fixed prime the six-step $N_3$ walk has odd-length growth 6, since each
component is regular and contains the order-three helper cycle. In
particular, the proposed numerical guarantee $\kappa<0.36$ does not
follow from that census. Equation (2) supplies a rigorous replacement.

For the general loop-permitting Proposition 6, an above-half rate only
forces $\kappa\theta\ge2$. The identity decoder with one self-helper
has $\kappa=1,\theta=2$ and rate 1. With vanishing loop density, the
half-rate conclusion extends to equality, as the endpoint argument above
illustrates. Also, every rate statement should be an upper bound, not an
assertion of equality for every code.

The result treats a narrow coefficient range of one explicit family over
$\mathbb C$. It does not exclude that family at coefficients near 1,
does not prove a finite-field rate bound, and does not decide Paper 17's
whole-row or general one-class questions. Its methodological point is that
nonamenable integral helper actions can yield a quantitative **even-moment**
bound despite odd relations. No finite polynomial search or Mergelyan
approximation is needed for this family.

Improving the walk-growth estimate alone cannot make the criterion
$\kappa\theta\le2$ reach coefficients near 1. The affine identity $A^3=I$
holds at every prime. For each Dyck path of length $2k$, choose at each
opening step a signed letter other than the inverse of the current top
letter; there are at least five choices. Closing steps cancel that letter.
This gives at least $C_k5^k$ distinct freely reducing words, where $C_k$ is
the Catalan number. Appending $A^3$ gives closed walks of length $2k+3$
through every position. Hence

$$
\theta\ge2\sqrt5.
$$

In particular the growth-rate criterion cannot apply when
$\kappa>1/\sqrt5$, including $\kappa=1$. This limits that sufficient
criterion, not all finite-moment arguments and not the existence of codes.
