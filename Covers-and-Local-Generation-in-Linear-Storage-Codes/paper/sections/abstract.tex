We study two restrictions on linear storage codes that can support a
coding approach to matrix transposition. First, fix a directed voltage
template over a free group embedded in a free-by-$\Z_p$ pro-$p$ group,
and take the finite quotients induced by a specified separating chain.
If matched input and demand ports are disconnected in the infinite
incidence cover, homogeneous linear recovery has asymptotic rate at most
one half at every fixed block width. The convergence is uniform over all
characteristic-$p$ coefficient fields and coefficient assignments.
The proof combines a finite separation certificate, published rank
approximation, and a finite-type parameter argument.

For growing widths, a quantitative bound isolates the dimension carried
by large simple coefficient modules after algebraic closure. Tightness
of these dimensions suffices for half rate. On independent-chord covers
of a fixed graph with a spanning $2$-factor, nonzero scalar checks have
rate tending to zero; the result extends uniformly to arbitrary-width
blocks preserving a common full flag and having invertible edge maps.
An exact homogenization construction preserves rate but can produce one
large absolutely simple module, so separation does not imply the
small-factor hypothesis.

Separately, over characteristic two, let $\mathcal L_R(\mathcal C)$ be
the span of codewords supported in radius-$R$ balls. If the recovery
graph has no odd cycle of length at most $2R+1$, then
$\dim\mathcal C+\dim\mathcal L_R(\mathcal C)
\le\sum_v\dim\pi_v(\mathcal C)$.
Exact homogenization and the kernel--range, quotient, and split-generator
dimension interfaces are verified in Lean. The asymptotic cover theorems
and the graph-local argument have complete written proofs but are not
formalized. Neither a general nonlinear coding bound nor a native tape
realization is supplied.
