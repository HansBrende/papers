\section{The weak form}\label{sec:weak}

\Cref{thm:T} uses one deficit for all windows. The proof uses less: for given resources it needs a bound at one
window, at one large size, with a deficit above the cost of the cut. Stating exactly that gives a weaker conjecture
with the same consequence. By \cref{thm:N} it may be stated for two-sided codes on symmetric reflexive relations.

For $k\ge5$, $G\ge1$, $c\ge4$, $d\ge1$, $\theta>0$ and $R\ge0$ put $b=b(d)$, $L=d^2b$, $\ell=\lceil db/G\rceil$ and
\begin{align*}
  D&=\operatorname{genieDepth}(k,1,12,\theta),&
  W&=2(G+1)(2k+3)^{D},\\
  K&=c+4+5(k-3)(cG+2),&
  M&=(4kG+1)\,\ell\,\bigl\lfloor\theta\lceil cL/\ell\rceil\bigr\rfloor,\\
  h&=\begin{cases}0,&R\ge d,\\ \min\bigl(d,\lfloor Wd/(R+1)\rfloor\bigr),&R<d,\end{cases}&
  X&=2dh-h^2 .
\end{align*}

\begin{conjecture}[Matrix Coding Conjecture, weak form]\label{conj:weak}
\formal{Wall.lean}{Wall.Conjecture}
For all $k\ge5$, $G\ge1$, $c\ge4$ and every $d_0$ there are $d\ge\max(d_0,1)$, $R$, $\theta>0$ and $\delta$ such that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2em]
\item every two-sided whole-row code on $d\times d$ arrays with entries in $\{0,1\}^{b}$, for a symmetric reflexive
helper map of window at most $W+R+1$, has at most $2^{(1-\delta)L}$ elements, and
\item $\delta L>2(Kd+M)+bX$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{conjecture}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem F: the weak form suffices]\label{thm:F}
\formal{Wall.lean}{Wall.of_commonDeficit, Wall.excludes_adaptive, Wall.of_previous, Wall.commonDeficit_implies_previous}
\Cref{conj:MCC} implies \cref{conj:weak}, and \cref{conj:weak} implies that \textup{(T)} fails.
\end{theorem}
\begin{outline}
The first paper's buffered-machine reduction, \lean{Transpose.Tape.Impl.codes_coentLinear_at}, gives at every
sufficiently large $d$ a solving set of co-entropy at most $Kd$, with all $L$ output bits appended in the final $L$
steps. It uses $k=k_{\rm machine}+5$, the buffered cell-width parameter $G$, and $c=2c_{\rm machine}+4$; the machine's
state count enters only the lower threshold on $d$.

A final output row occupies at most $G+1$ slots, so no exceptional columns are needed and the helper window is
$W=2(G+1)(2k+3)^D$. There are at most $\lceil cL/\ell\rceil$ slots, and at most
$\lfloor\theta\lceil cL/\ell\rceil\rfloor$ cut slots, giving the exact message bill $M$.

For a one-sided fibre, Theorem N first passes to a two-sided code. Rows of reverse degree exceeding $R$ number at
most $h$ as defined above; there are none if $R\ge d$. Freezing those rows and the same columns costs at most
$X=2dh-h^2$ entries. The assumed normalized exponent therefore bounds the original one-sided exponent by
$[d^2+(1-\delta)d^2+X]/2$. Substitution in (2) gives
\[
 L\le Kd+M+\tfrac b2\bigl(d^2+(1-\delta)d^2+X\bigr),
\]
contradicting condition (ii). Cofinality supplies a witness $d$ beyond the machine's threshold; all code and helper
choices remain universally quantified at that $d$.

Conversely, a common deficit supplies the normalized bound with any smaller positive deficit. Choose fixed
$\theta$ small enough, then $R$ large enough, and finally $d$ large enough. The estimates
$M\le(4kG+1)\theta(cL+db)$ and $X\le2Wd^2/(R+1)$ absorb the finite overheads and prove condition (ii).
This establishes implication, not strict separation of the two open conjectures.
\end{outline}

\begin{remark}[What the weak form asks]\label{rem:weak-demand}
Condition~(ii) reads, up to lower-order terms, $\delta>2(4kG+1)c\,\theta+2W/(R+1)$ at window $U=W+R+1$. So a bound
$1-f(U)$ on the rate at window~$U$ need not have $f$ bounded below: it suffices that for some $\theta$ and $R$ the
value $f(W+R+1)$ exceeds the right-hand side. \emph{Heuristic:} the depth $D$ of the cut grows very fast as $\theta$
falls and $W$ is exponential in~$D$, so that $\theta$ is of the order of an inverse power of $\log\log W$; a deficit
that falls more slowly than every such power would then suffice. We have not proved an estimate of this form. On the other hand the general
bound $f(U)=1/(U+1)$ cannot meet even the exact finite invoice. Indeed a useful witness has $U<d$, and, writing
$t=R+1$, one has $h\ge\lfloor d/t\rfloor$ and $X/d^2\ge1/t>1/(U+1)$. For the middle inequality write
$d=at+s$, $0\le s<t$: then $t(2da-a^2)-d^2=a^2t(t-1)-s^2\ge0$. The symmetric arrays show
$\delta\le(d-1)/(2d)$, and a window of $d$ or more admits all arrays, so every witness has $W+R+1<d$.
\end{remark}
