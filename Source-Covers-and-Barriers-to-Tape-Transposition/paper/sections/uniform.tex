\section{Uniform exclusions and necessary source complexity}\label{sec:uniform}

The finite bound has constants independent of the number of selected
core families. This permits a numerical limit argument. It does not
permit an infinite source bank or a cut parameter growing with dimension.

\subsection{Original hop and variation profiles}

Fix $c,\beta>0$, $N\ge1$ and an integer $q\ge1$. In each admissible
geometry let $\mathcal K_0$ be an original $\mathrm Q_2$ core and let
$\mathcal E$ contain at most $q$ other complete families. Let $a_0$ bound
the hop count of every family outside $\mathcal K_0\cup\mathcal E$.
Sort the original core by nonincreasing hop count, with a fixed rule
for ties, and denote it by $G_1,G_2,\ldots$. Both profiles below are
zero at indices with no family. The variation profile uses this same ordering, not an ordering
by variation.

\begin{definition}[Near-full sequence and joint profile]\label{def:joint-profile}
\formal{SourceCovers/JointProfiles.lean}{SourceCovers.NearFullSequence,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.ofGeometry,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.JointProfile,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.exists_jointProfile,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.JointProfile.hop_mass,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.JointProfile.variation_mass,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.JointProfile.hop_antitone,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.JointProfile.hop_le_rank,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.JointProfile.finite_sums_tendsto,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.JointProfile.prefix_add_tail,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.JointProfile.finite_tails_tendsto,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.JointProfile.tails_tendsto_zero}
A \emph{near-full sequence} consists of admissible fixed geometries and
nonempty correctness sets indexed by $\nu$, with common $c,\beta,N,q$,
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:near-full}
 d_\nu\longrightarrow\infty,\qquad
 a_{0,\nu}/L_\nu\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \Delta_\nu/L_\nu\longrightarrow0.
\end{equation}
Its original profiles are
\[
 h_i^{(\nu)}=|G_i|/L_\nu,\qquad
 v_i^{(\nu)}=\Var(x_\nu;[p_{G_i},U_{G_i}])/L_\nu.
\]
A \emph{joint profile} is a coordinatewise subsequential limit
$(h_i,v_i)_{i\ge1}$ of both sequences of profiles. Write
\[
 \tau_R=\sum_{i>R}h_i,\qquad V_K=\sum_{i>K}v_i.
\]
\end{definition}

Every near-full sequence has a joint profile. For every such profile,
both coordinates are nonnegative and summable, with
\[
 \sum_{i\ge1}h_i\le c,\qquad \sum_{i\ge1}v_i\le2c,
 \qquad h_{i+1}\le h_i\le c/i\quad(i\ge1).
\]
Consequently $\tau_R\to0$ and $V_K\to0$. Finite coordinate sums converge
along the common subsequence; afterwards, finite tails of the limiting
profile converge to its infinite tails. None of these statements asserts
convergence of the finite geometries' tail masses or stabilization of
their family topology.

\begin{outline}
Order each geometry's complete core families by nonincreasing hop count,
using any fixed tie-breaking rule, and pad both profiles with zero after
the last family. The variation profile uses the same list. The formal
proof uses Mathlib's list sorting and a sum identity for this enumeration.
The Q2 accounting bounds every finite coordinate sum by $c$ for hops and
$2c$ for variation, using $T\le cL$.

Place both countable sets of coordinates in one product of compact real
intervals. Mathlib's compactness theorem gives one strictly increasing
subsequence along which all coordinates converge. For any such limit,
nonnegativity and every finite-sum budget pass to the limit. Mathlib's
bounded-partial-sum criterion then gives summability and the stated total
bounds; it does not exchange an infinite sum with a limit. Hop
antitonicity also passes to the limit, and
$ih_i\le\sum_{j=1}^ih_j\le c$ gives the rank bound.

The verified prefix-plus-tail identities and the convergence of partial
sums give the two tail conclusions. Lean indices start at zero: its
entry $i$ is the paper's $G_{i+1}$. Its shift by $R$ therefore corresponds
exactly to the paper's tail after the first $R$ families. Strictly increasing subsequences
preserve all three near-full conditions. These proofs allow arbitrary
positive entry widths, including the logarithmic widths of~(T).
\end{outline}

This supplies the common numerical profile used by the next theorem.
The next theorem applies the finite deficit to each selected prefix and
justifies the successive limits in that inequality.

\begin{theorem}[Theorem U: necessary hop tails]\label{thm:necessary-hop-tail}
\formal{SourceCovers/NecessaryHopTails.lean}{SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.JointProfile.necessary_hop_tail,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.JointProfile.tail_floor,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.JointProfile.not_summable_moment,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.JointProfile.moment_partial_sums_tendsto_atTop}
For every joint profile of every near-full sequence and all integers
$K,R\ge0$, $M\ge\max(2,R+1)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:necessary-hop-tail}
 \frac{\beta\tau_R}{M-R}
 \ge \frac{(1-V_K)_+^2}{2(2K+1)(2M+4)^q}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, for every $K,R\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tail-floor}
 \tau_R\ge
 \frac{(1-V_K)_+^2}{2\beta4^q(2K+1)(R+2)^{q-1}}.
\end{equation}
If $q\ge2$, every such profile satisfies
$\sum_{i\ge1}i^{q-1}h_i=\infty$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{outline}
The formal library first retains actual cover averages as in
\cref{thm:necessary-cover}, then substitutes their terminal-orbit lower
bound. We describe the shared finite-prefix calculation here.
Fix a joint-profile subsequence and $K,R,M$. For each fixed integer
$J\ge\max(K,R)$, select the first $J$ original core families, distinguish its
first $K$, protect its first $R$, and rebuild the forest and ranks of
that finite selection. These are actual complete families; the formal
prefix identities preserve their full-span weights. Distinguishing a family
does not require protecting it. Absent families are simply omitted. Sorted hop
counts and their total bound imply that every omitted core family has
at most $cL_\nu/(J+1)$ hops. The finite theorem therefore applies with
\[
 a_\nu=\max\{a_{0,\nu},\lceil cL_\nu/(J+1)\rceil\},\qquad
 w_\nu=\lceil\lambda L_\nu\rceil,
\]
where $\lambda>0$ is also fixed before $\nu$ tends to infinity.
Use the full undistinguished variation and unprotected hop sums as the
finite budgets. The latter bounds the nonleaf-only payment after the
forest is rebuilt. Their limits are
\[
 \alpha_\nu=\sum_{K<i\le J}v_i^{(\nu)}
   \longrightarrow V_{K,J}:=\sum_{K<i\le J}v_i,\qquad
 \tau_\nu/L_\nu=\sum_{R<i\le J}h_i^{(\nu)}
   \longrightarrow\tau_{R,J}:=\sum_{R<i\le J}h_i.
\]
The normalized residual bill is bounded in the limit by
$\beta NC_qc^2/[\lambda(J+1)]$; the normalized movement, outer-lower
and stripe errors vanish. The width error is at most
$2w_\nu/L_\nu\to2\lambda$. These limits reuse Mathlib's normalized-ceiling
and finite-sum results, together with $T_\nu\le cL_\nu$; no convergence
of $T_\nu/L_\nu$ is required.
Using \cref{cor:orbit-count-deficit} and \cref{eq:near-full}, we obtain
\begin{equation}\label{eq:finite-prefix-limit}
 \frac{(1-V_{K,J})_+^2}{2(2K+1)(2M+4)^q}
 \le\frac{\beta\tau_{R,J}}{M-R}+2\lambda
       +\frac{\beta NC_qc^2}{\lambda(J+1)}.
\end{equation}
This is an inequality for every fixed $J\ge\max(K,R)$ and $\lambda>0$.
Now let $J$ tend to infinity in these numerical inequalities, using the
verified summability of the limiting profiles. Finally let $\lambda$
decrease to zero; formally, take $\lambda=\delta/2$ for each $\delta>0$
and remove the resulting additive $\delta$.
The constant $C_q$ depends on $M,q$, not $J$, so this proves
\cref{eq:necessary-hop-tail}.

Taking $M=2R+2$, including $R=0$, gives $2M+4=4(R+2)$ and proves
\cref{eq:tail-floor}. A finite $K$ with $V_K\le1/2$ always exists.
If $\sum_i i^{q-1}h_i$ were finite, then for $R\ge0$
\[
 (R+2)^{q-1}\tau_R
 \le3^{q-1}\sum_{i>R}i^{q-1}h_i\longrightarrow0,
\]
contrary to \cref{eq:tail-floor} for that $K$. The shared formal lemma
compares scaled tails with a summable majorant and permits any fixed
nonnegative shift. Mathlib's nonnegative-series criterion turns the
resulting nonsummability into divergence of the partial sums to $+\infty$.
Every step used a finite certificate. Omitted families remained in the
residual bill until after the dimension limit; no family was discarded
by an exchange of limits.
\end{outline}

The independent cutoffs remove the earlier restriction $R\ge K$:
the certificate may protect fewer families than it discounts in the
variation estimate. This strengthens
the necessary profile constraints without by itself enlarging the
asymptotic exclusion classes below. The theorem still concerns the
specified admissible geometries, not arbitrary tape computations.

\begin{corollary}[One arbitrary exceptional family]\label{cor:one-exception}
\formal{SourceCovers/UniformDeficit.lean}{SourceCovers.one_exception_uniform_deficit,SourceCovers.uniform_deficit_of_no_nearFull,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.one_exception_false,SourceCovers.AdmissibleGeometry.deficit_card_iff}
Fix $c,\beta>0$ and $N\ge1$. There exist $\eps>0$ and $d_0$ such that
for every positive entry width $b$, every admissible geometry with
$L=d^2b$, a $\mathrm Q_2$ core, at most one other
complete exceptional family, $d\ge d_0$, and $a_0\le\eps L$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:uniform-deficit}
 \Delta\ge\eps L,\qquad
 |\mathcal C|\le2^{(1-\eps)L}.
\end{equation}
The same constants work for all $b$. No envelope on the sorted
core-family sizes is assumed.
\end{corollary}
\begin{outline}
The formal proof first excludes every near-full sequence with $q=1$.
Its joint profile has summable hop mass. The verified critical-moment
argument of \cref{thm:necessary-hop-tail} also applies at $q=1$, where
the weight $i^{q-1}$ is one, and contradicts that summability.

Next apply a shared sequential-exclusion lemma to the two nonnegative
costs $a_0/L$ and $\Delta/L$. Nonnegativity of the latter follows from
$1\le|\mathcal C|\le2^L$. If no uniform gap existed, for each
$n\ge0$ one could choose an actual admissible geometry with
\[
 d_n\ge n,\qquad
 a_{0,n}/L_n\le\frac1{n+1},\qquad
 \Delta_n/L_n<\frac1{n+1}.
\]
Mathlib's reciprocal limit and squeeze theorem give a near-full
sequence, contradicting the preceding step. The argument makes no
choice of a width function before extracting these geometries, so the
constants are uniform over all positive widths. A class-restricted
version retains any additional predicate on every selected geometry;
it is reusable for the later envelope criteria.

Finally, for nonempty $\mathcal C$, Mathlib's base-two logarithm and
real-power equivalence gives
\[
 \eps L\le\Delta
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 |\mathcal C|\le2^{(1-\eps)L}.
\]
Thus both conclusions use the same constants. The Lean statement also
covers nonpositive ambient capacity bounds: the necessary-tail
inequality then contradicts the positive geometric credit directly.
\end{outline}

This verifies a uniform exclusion for the stated return geometries,
including bit matrices and logarithmic-size entries. It does not bound
general transposers without the full-span partition and core hypotheses.
The proof gives existence of uniform constants, not numerical values.

\begin{corollary}[Common size envelopes]\label{cor:common-envelope}
\formal{SourceCovers/CommonEnvelopes.lean}{SourceCovers.EnvelopeTailSmall,SourceCovers.finite_prefix_envelope_uniform_deficit,SourceCovers.common_envelope_uniform_deficit,SourceCovers.power_envelope_tailSmall,SourceCovers.power_envelope_uniform_deficit}
Fix $c,\beta,N,q$ as above and a nonnegative summable sequence $(g_i)$
such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:envelope-test}
 \liminf_{R\to\infty}(R+2)^{q-1}\sum_{i>R}g_i=0.
\end{equation}
Write $s_i=|G_i|/L$ in the original hop-sorted order, with $s_i=0$
after the last family. There exist $\eps>0$ and an integer $m\ge0$
such that every admissible geometry satisfying
\[
 d\ge m,\qquad a_0\le\eps L,\qquad
 s_i\le g_i+\eps\quad(1\le i\le m)
\]
satisfies \cref{eq:uniform-deficit}. The constants are common to all
positive entry widths. No envelope condition is imposed beyond rank $m$.
In particular, the conclusion holds under the original exact common bound
$|G_i|\le Lg_i$ on all core families. Every common power envelope
$g_i=H/i^p$, with fixed $H\ge0$ and real $p>q$, satisfies
\cref{eq:envelope-test} and is covered.
\end{corollary}
\begin{outline}
First suppose a joint profile obeys $h_i\le g_i$ for every $i$.
Both sequences are summable, so their tails satisfy
$\tau_R\le\sum_{i>R}g_i$. Choose $K$ with $V_K<1$ in
\cref{eq:tail-floor}. It gives a constant $A>0$ such that
\[
 A\le(R+2)^{q-1}\tau_R
   \le(R+2)^{q-1}\sum_{i>R}g_i
 \quad\text{for every }R\ge0,
\]
contradicting \cref{eq:envelope-test}. The lower limit is taken in
$[0,+\infty]$: being zero means that for every $\eta>0$ and every
$R_0$, some $R\ge R_0$ has scaled tail below $\eta$. The formal proof
uses this equivalent condition, which needs no upper bound on the
scaled tails.

To obtain the stronger finite-prefix statement, consider an actual
admissible geometry together with a cutoff $J$, a slack $\eta\ge0$,
and one sorted order satisfying $s_i\le g_i+\eta$ for $1\le i\le J$.
Apply the shared uniform-gap lemma to effective dimension $\min(d,J)$
and the two costs
\[
 \max\{a_0/L,\eta\},\qquad \Delta/L.
\]
If effective dimension tended to infinity and both costs tended to zero,
the geometries would form a near-full sequence with $J\to\infty$ and
$\eta\to0$. Retain each supplied order for both numerical profiles.
Along their common subsequence every fixed rank eventually lies below
the cutoff, hence $h_i\le g_i$ by passage to the limit. The preceding
contradiction excludes such a sequence. The uniform-gap lemma therefore
provides $\eps,m$; taking $J=m$ and $\eta=\eps$ proves the claimed
deficit, and the verified logarithmic equivalence gives its cardinality
form. The exact-envelope conclusion follows by adding the positive slack.
Only one order is supplied per geometry; no additional requirement on
tie breaking is introduced.

For $g_i=H/i^p$, Mathlib's $p$-series theorem gives
\[
 \sum_{i\ge1}i^{q-1}g_i
   =H\sum_{i\ge1}i^{q-1-p}<\infty
 \qquad(p>q).
\]
The existing moment-to-tail lemma then gives
$(R+2)^{q-1}\sum_{i>R}g_i\to0$, which implies
\cref{eq:envelope-test}. This reuses the weighted-tail argument of
\cref{thm:necessary-hop-tail} without a separate integral estimate.
Lean's rank $i$ corresponds to the paper's rank $i+1$ throughout.
\end{outline}

Thus ruling out a common limiting envelope also rules out a finite set
of approximate leading-rank bounds. At sufficiently large dimension, a
construction in this admissible class must violate at least one such bound if its outside families and
correctness deficit are both small. The hypotheses on the full geometry
remain necessary; the constants and cutoff are not given numerically.

\begin{corollary}[A fixed finite skeleton]\label{cor:finite-skeleton}
\formal{SourceCovers/FiniteSkeleton.lean}{SourceCovers.AdmissibleGeometry.withSkeleton,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.empty_core_false,SourceCovers.empty_core_uniform_deficit,SourceCovers.finite_skeleton_uniform_deficit,SourceCovers.large_family_count_of_small_deficit}
Fix $c,\beta>0$, $N\ge1$ and an integer $q\ge0$.
There are $\eps>0$ and $d_0$ such that \cref{eq:uniform-deficit} holds
for every admissible geometry with $d\ge d_0$, any set $\mathcal E$
of at most $q$ complete families, and any integer $a\ge0$ satisfying
\[
 |G|\le a\quad(G\notin\mathcal E),\qquad a\le\eps L.
\]
The chosen skeleton $\mathcal E$ need not agree with the geometry's
previous core or exception labels. The constants are common to all
positive entry widths and all previous classifications.
Equivalently, with these constants, $d\ge d_0$ and $\Delta<\eps L$ force
\[
 \#\{G:\ |G|>\eps L\}>q,
\]
where the count ranges over all complete families of the computation.
\end{corollary}
\begin{outline}
Reclassify the complete families by taking the core empty, the exceptions
to be $\mathcal E$, and the outside bound to be $a$. The full work walks,
raw path, output times, records, parent relation, correctness set and
$N$-class span partition are unchanged. The supplied size hypothesis
justifies the new outside bound; no family or hop is deleted.

A hypothetical near-full sequence with empty cores has an explicit zero
joint profile, using the identity subsequence. The verified zero-padding
lemmas give both coordinates. The verified form of
\cref{eq:necessary-hop-tail} also permits $q=0$. At $K=R=0$ and $M=2$,
it becomes $1/(2\cdot8^q)\le0$, a contradiction for every $q\ge0$.
The existing uniform-gap theorem gives the empty-core deficit, and
reclassification transfers it to the proposed skeleton. The constants
are chosen before the original classification or entry width. The Lean
proof also permits nonpositive ambient capacity bounds.

For the counting form, take $\mathcal E$ to be the families with
$|G|>\eps L$ and $a=\lfloor\eps L\rfloor$. If $|\mathcal E|\le q$,
Mathlib's natural-floor bounds give the preceding hypotheses, contradicting
$\Delta<\eps L$. Conversely, the count precludes any proposed skeleton of
size at most $q$ when all remaining families have at most $a\le\eps L$ hops.
\end{outline}

Thus a construction with sufficiently small correctness deficit needs
more than any prescribed fixed number $q$ of families of size proportional
to $L$, with the proportionality constant allowed to depend on $q$.
This conclusion retains the full-span partition hypothesis.

These are restrictions on a common full geometry. In the one-exception
case they settle the paid-deficit comparison without a coding conjecture.
For $q=2$, the necessary tail floor is of order $1/R$, compatible with
an inverse-square size profile; a further geometric argument is needed.
The proof supplies uniform constants by contradiction, not effective
numerical values of $\eps$ and $d_0$.

\subsection{Necessary fractional-cover complexity}

Along a fixed joint-profile subsequence, select the first $J$ original
core families, protect its first $R$, and fix $M\ge\max(2,R+1)$.
Rebuild the forest and phases for each selected prefix. For every legal
phase and column put
$\kappa_{\nu,J,R,M,\rho,j}=\kappa_\rho(S_\rho(s_j))$.
These are the fractional covers of the complete selected source banks;
they are finite and at least one. Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reciprocal-cover-limit}
 Q_{R,M}^*=\limsup_{J\to\infty}\limsup_{\nu\to\infty}
       \mathbb E_{\rho,j}\frac1{\kappa_{\nu,J,R,M,\rho,j}},
 \qquad Q_R^*=Q_{R,2R+2}^*.
\end{equation}
The expectation is the arithmetic mean over legal phases and columns.
Both limits hold $R,M$ fixed. We suppress $M=2R+2$ in the subscript of
$\kappa$ when using $Q_R^*$.

\begin{theorem}[Necessary reciprocal-cover bound]\label{thm:necessary-cover}
\formal{SourceCovers/NecessaryCover.lean}{SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.JointProfile.reciprocalCoverLimit,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.JointProfile.reciprocalCoverLimit_bounds,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.JointProfile.weighted_reciprocal_cover,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.JointProfile.necessary_reciprocal_cover,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.JointProfile.necessary_reciprocal_cover_default,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.JointProfile.coverProbabilityLimit,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.JointProfile.coverProbabilityLimit_le_reciprocal,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.JointProfile.hop_le_of_pointwise,SourceCovers.NearFullSequence.JointProfile.inverse_square_cover_probability}
For every joint profile of every near-full sequence, all integers
$K,R\ge0$, and every $M\ge\max(2,R+1)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weighted-necessary-cover}
 \frac{(1-V_K)_+^2}{2(2K+1)}Q_{R,M}^*
 \le\frac{\beta\tau_R}{M-R}.
\end{equation}
If $V_K<1$, this gives
\[
 Q_{R,M}^*\le
 \frac{2\beta(2K+1)\tau_R}{(1-V_K)^2(M-R)}.
\]
In particular,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:necessary-cover}
 Q_R^*\le
 \frac{2\beta(2K+1)\tau_R}{(1-V_K)^2(R+2)}.
\end{equation}
The cutoffs $K,R$ are independent, including $R=0$, and the statements
hold for any fixed number $q$ of exceptions.
If the limiting profile satisfies $h_i\le H/i^2$ for some $H\ge0$ and
$V_K\le1/2$, then for every $R\ge1$ and every fixed real $s\ge0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cover-saturation}
 \limsup_J\limsup_\nu
 \Pr_{\rho,j}\{\kappa_{\nu,J,R,\rho,j}\le s\}
 \le \frac{8\beta(2K+1)Hs}{R(R+2)}.
\end{equation}
A common bound $|G_i|\le HL_\nu/i^2$ in the supplied original hop order
is sufficient for this limiting-envelope hypothesis.
\end{theorem}
\begin{outline}
Retain the actual mean reciprocal cover in the finite paid-deficit
inequality. The prefix and rounding estimates described in the proof of
\cref{thm:necessary-hop-tail} are proved once in this stronger form.
For fixed $J\ge\max(K,R)$, $M$ and $\lambda>0$, they give
\[
 \frac{(1-V_{K,J})_+^2}{2(2K+1)}
    \limsup_\nu\mathbb E_{\rho,j}\frac1{\kappa_{\nu,J,R,M,\rho,j}}
 \le \frac{\beta\tau_{R,J}}{M-R}+2\lambda
       +\frac{\beta NC_qc^2}{\lambda(J+1)}.
\]
The finite averages and their successive upper limits lie in
$[(2M+4)^{-q},1]$. Thus real upper limits are bounded throughout.
A shared lemma uses Mathlib's subsequence attaining the upper limit to
pass a converging coefficient through this inequality. It also handles
a zero limiting coefficient; no division is made at this stage.

Apply the same lemma as $J$ tends to infinity. Summability gives
$V_{K,J}\to V_K$ and $\tau_{R,J}\to\tau_R$, while the residual error
tends to zero. Finally take $\lambda=\delta/2$ for every $\delta>0$
and remove the additive $\delta$. This proves
\cref{eq:weighted-necessary-cover}. When $V_K<1$, division by its
positive coefficient gives the general bound, and $M=2R+2$ gives
\cref{eq:necessary-cover}. The dimension limit, prefix limit and
removal of the width error occur in that order.

For the probability bound, $\kappa\ge1$ gives the finite pointwise
inequality $\mathbf1_{\{\kappa\le s\}}\le s/\kappa$ for every
$s\ge0$. Averaging and taking the same two upper limits preserves it.
Mathlib's finite inverse-square sum estimate, followed by the summable
limit, gives $\tau_R\le H/R$ when $R>0$. The bound
$(1-V_K)^2\ge1/4$ then gives \cref{eq:cover-saturation}.
Only the limiting envelope is used. The stated common finite envelope
passes coordinatewise to the same joint profile.
\end{outline}

This strengthens the necessary condition by retaining the actual cover
complexity: replacing $Q_{R,M}^*$ by its terminal-orbit lower bound
$(2M+4)^{-q}$ recovers \cref{eq:necessary-hop-tail} directly.
It does not prove that arbitrary admissible geometries have small
covers. In the inverse-square case it limits the fraction of phase-column
pairs with small cover, not only their mean cover complexity.

\begin{corollary}[Uniform cover criteria]\label{cor:uniform-cover}
\formal{SourceCovers/UniformCover.lean}{SourceCovers.AdmissibleGeometry.HasMeanCoverBound,SourceCovers.CoverTailSmall,SourceCovers.uniform_deficit_of_no_ordered_nearFull,SourceCovers.linear_mean_cover_uniform_deficit,SourceCovers.envelope_mean_cover_uniform_deficit,SourceCovers.power_cover_tailSmall,SourceCovers.power_mean_cover_uniform_deficit}
Fix $c,\beta>0$, $N\ge1$ and an integer $q\ge0$.
Each of the following defines a class with constants $\eps>0,d_0$ for
which \cref{eq:uniform-deficit} holds when $d\ge d_0$ and
$a_0\le\eps L$, uniformly over all positive entry widths.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item For some common constant $B\ge0$, selecting the first $J$
original core families and protecting the first $R$, for every
$J\ge R\ge1$ and $M=2R+2$, gives
$\mathbb E_{\rho,j}\kappa_{J,R,\rho,j}\le B(R+2)$.
No size envelope is required.
\item For fixed $H\ge0$ and real $p>1$, the original sizes satisfy
$|G_i|\le HL/i^p$, and for a common nonnegative function
$f(R)=o(R^p)$ every such finite prefix selection satisfies
$\mathbb E_{\rho,j}\kappa_{J,R,\rho,j}\le f(R)$.
\end{enumerate}
More generally, in (b) one may replace the power envelope by any common
nonnegative summable envelope $|G_i|\le Lg_i$, and the growth condition by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cover-tail-test}
 \liminf_{R\to\infty}
 \frac{f(R)}{R+2}\sum_{i>R}g_i=0,
\end{equation}
where the lower limit is taken in $[0,+\infty]$.
The size and mean-cover bounds must hold in the same supplied original
hop order. The constants are independent of the choice of that order.
Here $\kappa_{J,R,\rho,j}$ is the fractional cover of the selected
geometry's complete source set at the first output time of column $j$.
The bounds are uniform over the class and dimension; $\eps,d_0$ may
depend on all the fixed class data. In (b), the
inverse-square case requires mean cover $o(R^2)$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{outline}
The formal finite averaging lemma applies Mathlib's Cauchy inequality to
the actual legal phase-column pairs. Their cover weights are finite and
at least one, so
\[
 \mathbb E_{\rho,j}\frac1\kappa
 \ge\frac1{\mathbb E_{\rho,j}\kappa}
 \ge\frac1{f(R)}
\]
whenever the stated mean bound holds. Positivity of $f(R)$ for $R\ge1$
follows from that bound itself. Holding $R$ fixed, pass first through the
dimension upper limit, then through the prefix upper limit. Only the
prefixes $J\ge R$ matter for the latter. The explicit bounds from
\cref{thm:necessary-cover} justify both real upper limits.
Its weighted inequality therefore gives, for every $K\ge0$ and $R\ge1$,
\[
 A_K:=\frac{(1-V_K)_+^2}{2(2K+1)}
 \le\beta\frac{\tau_R f(R)}{R+2}.
\]
Choose $K$ with $V_K<1$, so $A_K>0$. In (a), the right side is
$\beta B\tau_R\to0$, a contradiction. Under a common summable envelope,
coordinatewise convergence gives $h_i\le g_i$ and summability gives
$\tau_R\le\sum_{i>R}g_i$. Condition \cref{eq:cover-tail-test} provides
arbitrarily late values below every positive threshold, again
contradicting $A_K>0$. No boundedness of the scaled envelope tails is
assumed.

For the power case, Mathlib's integral comparison and improper-integral
formula give, for every $R\ge1$,
\[
 \sum_{i>R}\frac H{i^p}
 \le\frac H{p-1}R^{1-p}.
\]
Consequently the expression in \cref{eq:cover-tail-test} is at most
$Hf(R)/[(p-1)R^p]\to0$. The shared formal result allows every real
$p>1$ and compares genuinely summable series before taking their tails.

Finally apply the existing uniform-gap theorem to the class of actual
geometries together with an order witnessing the required bounds.
The adapter retains that same order when forming a near-full sequence
and extracting its joint profile. The contradictions above exclude such
a sequence, giving uniform $\eps,d_0$. The existing deficit/cardinality
equivalence yields both conclusions in \cref{eq:uniform-deficit} with
the same constants. No width function is fixed during this extraction.
\end{outline}

The general criterion covers non-power-law and irregular envelopes;
only arbitrarily late small values of the weighted tail are required.
A proposed construction in this admissible class must therefore violate
the corresponding mean-cover budget if its outside families and
correctness deficit are sufficiently small. The result does not provide
those budgets for arbitrary tape computations or numerical gap constants.

The saturation bound also rules out a misleading average picture:
in a near-full inverse-square sequence, rare very complicated columns
cannot compensate for a positive fraction with cover complexity
$o(R^2)$, in the stated ordered limits. This is a necessary condition,
not a construction of an economical cover.

\subsection{The movement of complete older observables}

For $q=2$, use one cluster for all nonroot elements of $F_\rho(s_j)$
in \cref{prop:continuation-covers}(c), and use $E=0$ when that set is
empty or a singleton. Let $e_{\nu,J,R}$ be the mean of $E/L_\nu$
over phases and columns, with $M=2R+2$, and put
$e_R^*=\liminf_J\liminf_\nu e_{\nu,J,R}$.

\begin{corollary}[Necessary complete-observable movement]\label{cor:necessary-exposure}
\notformalized
Along every subsequence realizing a joint profile of a two-exception
near-full sequence, for every integer $K\ge0$ and $R\ge\max(K,1)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:necessary-exposure}
 e_R^*\ge
 \frac{(1-V_K)_+^2}{16(2K+1)(R+2)}-\frac{\beta\tau_R}{R+2}.
\end{equation}
For fixed $K$ with $V_K<1$ this implies
$\liminf_{R\to\infty}(R+2)e_R^*\ge(1-V_K)^2/[16(2K+1)]$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{informalproof}
The one-cluster estimate, also valid when there are no nonroot seeds,
is $(1-\alpha)_+^2/[4(2K+1)(2M+4)]-E/L$.
Insert it in \cref{thm:paid-source-cover} and move the mean $E/L$ to
the other side. Take the liminf in $\nu$, then in $J$, and then let
$\lambda$ decrease to zero exactly as in
\cref{eq:finite-prefix-limit}. Substitution of $M=2R+2$ gives
\cref{eq:necessary-exposure}; $\tau_R\to0$ gives the last assertion.
\end{informalproof}

Here the moving observables include every older continuation, not just
the immediate input coordinate at an exceptional landing. This is the
quantity that a successful approximation argument would have to control.
