\section{Macroscopic returns on original adaptive runs}\label{sec:returns}

Fix $k,a\ge1$ and $C\ge1$, and put
\[
 T_* =\lfloor C(P+1)\rfloor,\qquad W=\lceil P/(80aC)\rceil,
 \qquad N=\lfloor160aC^2\rfloor+1.
\]
Let $\operatorname{Bad}(H,J)$ consist of timely correct inputs for which some
free head leaves at most $J$ returns of excursion greater than $H$ on the
other heads.

\begin{lemma}[Finite geometry]\label{lem:slab-geometry}
\formal{SlabGeometry.lean}{exists_slab_geometry,native_slab_geometry}
For $P\ge2$, every timely correct run and chosen free head has a slab chunk $C$
of at most $\lfloor P/2\rfloor$ steps and at most $N$ runs. With $U$ from
\cref{eq:omitted}, it satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:slab-margin}
 |U|-a\iota W\ge P/(4N)-3Ndb-a.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{outline}
We use the arbitrary-width slab and stripe lemmas of
\cite{MacroscopicCrossings2026}, Lean-verified there as
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \lean{NativeRelations.exists_offset_slabTransitions_le};
\item \lean{NativeRelations.exists_slab_chunk_dichotomy};
\item \lean{NativeRelations.outIn_card_le_two_indep_add_six_runs}.
\end{itemize}
The actual-input adapter obtains the halting time and all $P$ append events
from timely transposition of that input. Its own raw and work paths are unit
walks, and its actual append times are strictly increasing. No other input
is required to share this trace. The finite theorem constructs literal slab
intervals from these walks and times; it does not assume a suitable chunk.
Their finite statements imply this particular margin as follows.
An averaged grid offset has at most $(T-1)/W<N$ wall crossings. The short
case takes all visits to its slab from time zero, has at least $P/(2N)$
outputs and pays no snapshot. The long case takes a time slice and has
$P\lfloor P/2\rfloor<4T|O|$. In either case the stripe estimate is
$|O|\le2|U|+6Ndb$. Since $P\ge2$, $T\le2CP$ and
$\lfloor P/2\rfloor\ge P/3$, the long case gives
\[
 |U|-aW>P/(48C)-P/(80C)-3Ndb-a
       =P/(120C)-3Ndb-a.
\]
Now $N>160aC^2\ge160C$ implies \cref{eq:slab-margin}. The short and long
cases must be distinguished: charging a snapshot against the short-case
output bound would not justify this margin.
\end{outline}

\begin{theorem}[Finite original-run bound]\label{thm:finite-returns}
\formal{NativeReturnCounting.lean}{native_positive_saving_observation,native_finite_return_bound}
For $P\ge2$, using \cref{eq:metadata} and $A\le2^a$,
\begin{align}\label{eq:finite-returns}
 |\operatorname{Bad}(H,J)|\le
 2^{P-P/(4N)+E},\qquad
 E={}&3Ndb+a+a(k-1)N(2H+1)+Nq\\
 &+\log_2\mathcal M+\log_2\mathcal S(kT_*,J,A).\nonumber
\end{align}
There is no all-input correctness or common-trace assumption.
\end{theorem}
\begin{outline}
When the snapshot-adjusted margin is positive, the native slab theorem
selects a nonempty slab observation for each input and qualifying free head.
The encoder constructs its bounded metadata from the actual maximal runs,
head positions, raw extrema and append counters. Equal metadata determines
the retained time set and all these observations, while allowing different
detailed native traces. Unit-path continuity identifies the raw-extrema
union with the actually read cells; the output-counter intervals identify
exactly the actual appends.

The checked \lean{constantSlabClass} constructs the replay class on every
metadata fibre. Its successful prefixes and correct final words come from
the inputs' own native runs. The actual typed import support injects into
completed off-carrier long returns, so at most $J$ imports are paid.
The snapshot-adjusted saving is \cref{eq:slab-margin}; the remaining windows
and states cost at most $a(k-1)N(2H+1)+Nq$. Sum the class bounds over the
explicit menu of cardinality $\mathcal M$. Each finite menu contributes one
logarithm. If the geometric margin is nonpositive, the full $2^P$ input
bound already implies the claimed inequality. The formal endpoint applies
to any set of timely correct inputs satisfying the stated return condition,
without all-input correctness or a common detailed trace.
\end{outline}

\begin{theorem}[Theorem R: two macroscopic return heads]\label{thm:returns}
\formal{ReturnTail.lean}{returnTailEpsilon_pos,returnTailEta_pos,native_macroscopic_return_tail}
For fixed $k,a,C$ there are $\varepsilon,\gamma,\eta>0$ and a dimension
threshold uniform in all $b\ge1$ such that fewer than $2^{(1-\eta)P}$
timely correct inputs admit a free head with at most $\gamma P$ off-carrier
returns of excursion greater than $\lfloor\varepsilon P\rfloor$.
This holds for each individual machine with
$q\le P/(64N^2)$. In particular, for $k\ge2$, every other timely correct
input has two distinct heads each making more than $\gamma P/(k-1)$ such
returns. For $k=1$ the bound applies to the entire timely success set.
\end{theorem}
\begin{outline}
Take $\varepsilon=[64a\max(1,k-1)N^2]^{-1}$ and $\eta=1/(8N)$.
The checked sparse-record estimate \lean{native_sparse_logb_small}, applied
to the alphabet bound $2^a$, gives one $\delta>0$ such that
$J\le\delta P$ implies $\log_2\mathcal S(kT_*,J,A)\le P/(32N)$.
Choose $\gamma=\min(\delta,1/2)$ and $J=\lfloor\gamma P\rfloor$.
This includes $J=0$. The floor bound and the stated $\varepsilon$ give
\[
 a(k-1)N(2\lfloor\varepsilon P\rfloor+1)
 \le P/(32N)+a(k-1)N.
\]

The actual metadata menu satisfies the checked polynomial bound
\[
 \mathcal M\le k(N+1)
 \bigl[(2\lceil C\rceil+3)(P+1)\bigr]^{3+(k+7)N}.
\]
Thus \lean{native_metadata_logb_small} supplies a length threshold $P_0$
with $\log_2\mathcal M\le P/(192N)$ for $P\ge P_0$.
One valid dimension threshold is
\[
 d_0=2+576N^2+P_0+192N\bigl[a+a(k-1)N\bigr].
\]
For $d\ge d_0$ and every $b\ge1$, $P=d^2b\ge d$; the three terms
$3Ndb$, $a+a(k-1)N$ and $\log_2\mathcal M$ are each at most
$P/(192N)$. Their sum is at most $P/(64N)$.
The controller budget contributes another $P/(64N)$, and the proportional
window and sparse costs each contribute $P/(32N)$. The exact finite bound
therefore has exponent at most $(1-5/(32N))P<(1-\eta)P$.
Every threshold is independent of $b$ and of the individual machine within
the stated resource budgets.

For a good input, deleting any one head leaves more than $\gamma P$ returns;
the strict inequality follows from the integer floor cutoff. Partition the
actual return triples by head and choose a head of maximum count. Among the
other $k-1$ heads one has more than $\gamma P/(k-1)$ returns, and the maximum
head has at least that many. These are the checked declarations
\lean{offCarrierReturns_card_eq_sum}, \lean{native_two_return_heads} and
\lean{native_one_head_bad}. In the one-head case the off-carrier set is empty,
so every timely correct input belongs to the bad set.
\end{outline}
Fixed controllers, and uniformly $o(P)$-bit controllers, eventually meet the
displayed controller budget.
