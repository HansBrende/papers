# Finite-field decoders: lifting, joint-image surgery and expansion cores

Hans Brende and GPT-6 Astra, 12 November 2026.

**Status:** written proofs, independently reviewed; not newly Lean-verified.
This working note shares the reductions behind the finite-field discussion in
[item D](../D-comparison.md). It does not resolve Problem R or assert a new
native tape lower bound. The algebra bound is a consequence of existing flag
bounds and standard representation theory; no novelty claim is made.

## Setting and the lifting obstruction

Over a field $F$, put $K=\sum_{a<r} A_a\otimes B_a$, acting on
$d\times d$ matrices by $X\mapsto\sum_a A_aXB_a^T$. Let
$D(K)=\dim_F\ker(\tau-K)$, where $\tau(X)=X^T$. Problem R asks whether,
for fixed $F,r$ and every $\eta>0$, some $C=C(F,r,\eta)$ gives

$$
D(K)\le (1/2+\eta)d^2+Cd
\tag{R}
$$

for all dimensions and factors. A fixed number of terms is a meaningful
algebraic restriction. An arbitrarily chosen rational lift of small analytic
weight is not.

Indeed, for a prime $p$ and positive integers $d,t$, let
$c=(1+pd^2t)^{-1}$ and $\widetilde K=c\tau$ over the rationals. Using
$\tau=\sum_{i,j}E_{ij}\otimes E_{ji}$, its complex weight is at most
$d^2/(1+pd^2t)<1/(pt)$. Its agreement space is zero. Its denominators are
prime to $p$, and reduction gives $K=\tau$, whose agreement space has
dimension $d^2$. More generally, scaling any rational lift by
$(1+pu)^{-1}$ preserves its reduction while decreasing its analytic weight
arbitrarily. This does not refute (R): the displayed construction uses $d^2$
terms. It does not treat bounded integer lifts either.

Nor can every bounded integer lift be required to have negligible rank loss:
$\widetilde K=(1+p)I\otimes I$ has zero agreement space in characteristic
zero but reduces to the identity decoder with dimension $d(d+1)/2$.
Rank loss must be compared with the slack in the desired bound.

## Joint-image surgery

**Lemma.** Let $0<s<d$, let $U\le F^d$ have dimension $s$, and set

$$
w=\dim\sum_{a<r} B_a(U),\qquad h=\max(0,w-s),\qquad t=d-s.
$$

There are $r$-term decoders $K_1,K_2$ in dimensions $s,t$, over the same
field, such that

$$
D(K)\le st+D(K_1)+D(K_2)+dh. \tag{1}
$$

**Proof.** Choose an $s$-dimensional space $W_0$ containing the joint
image when $w\le s$, or contained in that image otherwise. In the latter
case choose a complement $H$ to $W_0$ inside the joint image, of dimension
$h$; otherwise set $H=0$. Choose bases $Q,P$ adapted to $U$ and $W_0,H$, respectively.
The substitution $X=PYQ^T$ changes the factors to

$$
A'_a=Q^{-1}A_aP,\qquad B'_a=P^{-1}B_aQ.
$$

Delete the lower-left $t\times s$ block of each $B'_a$. All deleted maps
land in the same $h$-dimensional space, so the change to the whole decoder
has image in $F^d\otimes H$, of dimension at most $dh$. For maps $T,T'$,
$\dim\ker T\le\dim\ker T'+\operatorname{rank}(T-T')$: restrict $T-T'$
to $\ker T$. The repaired factors preserve the first coordinate block.
The unequal-block-defect theorem of [Paper 18](../../Capacity-and-Composition-of-Shared-Transposition-Decoders/paper/sections/appendix-block-defects.tex)
then gives $st+D(K_1)+D(K_2)$. The diagonal factors are the corresponding
compressions of $A'_a,B'_a$, so the term count and field are preserved. ∎

This pays once for the joint image, not separately for each term. Exchanging
the two tensor factors gives the same conclusion for the $A_a$.

## A smallest counterexample must expand on four sides

**Proposition.** Fix a field $F$, a nonnegative integer $r$, and real
constants $\eta\ge0,C$. Let $K$ be an $r$-term decoder of least positive
dimension $d$ violating the displayed inequality (R) for these constants.
Every nonzero proper $U\le F^d$, of dimension $s$, satisfies

$$
\dim\sum_a B_a(U)>s+\frac{2\eta s(d-s)}d. \tag{2}
$$

The same statement holds for $A_a,A_a^T,B_a^T$.

**Proof.** The smaller diagonal decoders in (1) satisfy (R) by minimality,
with exactly the same constants. Thus

$$
D(K)\le (1/2+\eta)d^2+Cd-2\eta s(d-s)+dh.
$$

Failure of (R) forces $dh>2\eta s(d-s)\ge0$, hence $h=w-s>0$, proving
(2). Exchange tensor factors, and use
$\operatorname{rank}(\tau-K)=\operatorname{rank}(\tau-K^T)$ for the
transposed families. ∎

For $s\le d/2$, each of the four families therefore expands every such
subspace to dimension strictly greater than $(1+\eta)s$. At $\eta=0$,
even a balanced source/target block after independent basis changes is
excluded. This is a reduction to an expanding core, not a bound on that core.

For fixed $0<\eta<1/2$, failure for every $C\ge0$ yields arbitrarily
large cores: $D(K)\le d^2$ forces $d>C/(1/2-\eta)$. A finite seed above
the pairing value therefore does not refute the asymptotic statement.

## A sharp certificate from either factor algebra

Let $a_A,a_B$ be the dimensions of the unital algebras generated by the two
factor families. For $d\ge1$, over every field,

$$
D(K)\le\frac{d^2+d\min(\sqrt{a_A},\sqrt{a_B})}{2}. \tag{3}
$$

**Proof.** Extend scalars to an algebraic closure. The matrix-word spans
defining the algebras and the agreement kernel retain their dimensions.
Take a composition series of the $B$-algebra module, with simple-factor
dimensions $s_1,\ldots,s_m$. On each factor the algebra maps onto the full
matrix algebra: Schur's lemma makes the commutant scalar, and the
[Jacobson density theorem](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~andyp/notes/JacobsonDensity.pdf),
Theorem A, gives surjectivity. Hence $s_j^2\le a_B$. In a basis adapted to
the series, [Paper 17's block-flag bound](../../Order-Symmetry-and-the-Swap-A-Synthesis-of-Matrix-Transposition-on-Tapes/paper/sections/order.tex)
gives

$$
2D(K)\le d^2+\sum_j s_j^2
\le d^2+d\max_j s_j\le d^2+d\sqrt{a_B}.
$$

Exchange tensor sides. A composition series suffices; semisimplicity of the
original algebra is not assumed. ∎

For sharpness take $d=sm$, $A_{ij}=E_{ij}\otimes I_m$ and
$B_{ij}=E_{ji}\otimes I_m$, with $i,j<s$. Both algebras have dimension
$s^2$; all $s^2$ blocks of a codeword must independently be symmetric.
Thus $D=s^2m(m+1)/2=(d^2+sd)/2$, attaining (3), also in characteristic two.

Either algebra being $o(d^2)$-dimensional gives asymptotic half rate. But
fixed generator count does not imply this: a cyclic permutation and $E_{00}$
generate every matrix unit over every field. The remaining target is the
interaction between the two expanding families through their pairing in
$\sum_a A_a\otimes B_a$, beyond a one-sided algebra or flag certificate.
