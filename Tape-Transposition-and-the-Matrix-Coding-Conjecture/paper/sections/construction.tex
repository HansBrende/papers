\section{The construction side}\label{sec:construction}

What would a counterexample to the conjecture, and what would a linear-time transposer, have to look like?

\subsection{What a code of rate near one must avoid}\label{sec:constr-code}

By \cref{thm:N} it may be taken two-sided on a symmetric reflexive relation. By \cref{thm:I} it breaks every pairing
symmetry on a positive fraction of the entries. By \cref{prop:known} and the classes of \cref{sec:invariance} its
relation has window at least three and spread rows and offsets, and by \cref{thm:O} acyclic sets and closure say
nothing against it when the relation mixes. \Cref{thm:E} shows that entropy inequalities can still exclude it.
\draftnote{The codes of the thirteenth and fifteenth papers: what is known about realizing them as whole-row codes.}

\subsection{What the conjecture forgets about machines}\label{sec:constr-forgets}

Between a machine and \cref{conj:MCC} lie several statements, each obtained from the one before by forgetting
something: the run as a table of steps, which is a restatement of the machine
(\lean{SameTable.uniformTableaux_iff}); the values on a cut with the few records that each output can reach; an index
code for all inputs on the cone system; the whole-row codes on its fibres. The reduction of \cref{sec:rows} never uses
that all steps apply the same rule.

\begin{proposition}[Runs that a cheap cut splits]\label{prop:hyperfinite}
\notformalized
Let an oblivious machine with $k$ work tapes run in time $c(L+1)$ with steps of $s$ bits. Suppose that for some
$\theta$ with $s(k+2)c\,\theta<\tfrac12$ and all large $d$ there is a set of at most $\theta T$ steps whose removal
leaves components of the dependency graph, edges taken without direction, with $o(\log\log d)$ output steps each.
Then the machine does not transpose.
\end{proposition}
\draftnote{Complete proof from the local-view inequality (\lean{LocalRecordViews.half_invoice}, formalized), the cut
of the fourteenth paper and the separation theorem of the fifth paper. Relation to the classes of the fourth paper:
no implication is established in either direction.}

\begin{remark}[Fibres are nearly Markov]\label{rem:markov}
Proof sketch only: the set of inputs with a given cut message is cut out by constraints on boundedly many rows each,
and the fibre used in \cref{thm:T} is close to such a set in relative entropy.

\end{remark}

\subsection{What a machine would need}\label{sec:constr-machine}

A linear-time transposer yields, for every $\eps$, whole-row codes of rate at least $1-\eps$ at a window that depends
on $\eps$ only. Such codes would not yet be a machine: they would have to be produced by passes over tapes in linear
time, and the motion would have to satisfy the conditions of the earlier
papers~\cite{FourthPaper2026,SeventeenthPaper2026}.
