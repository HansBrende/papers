\section{Entropy beyond order}\label{sec:entropy}

A \emph{valid polymatroid} for $\rho$ is a normalized, monotone, submodular function $h$ on sets of entries with
$h(\{x\})\le1$ and $h(H_x\cup\{x\})=h(H_x)$, where $H_x$ is the union of the helper rows of the column of~$x$.
Entropy, in units of $\log\card\Sigma$, is such a function for every whole-row code. The largest possible value on
all entries is the \emph{Shannon value} $\Sh(\rho)$; it bounds $\log_{\card\Sigma}\card C$ without an assumption on
the alphabet or the recovery functions~\cite[Theorem~6.3]{FourthPaper2026}.

\begin{theorem}[Theorem E: entropy beats order]\label{thm:E}
\formal{FanoFull.lean}{FanoFull.full_upper, FanoFull.wholeRow_card_cube}
\formal{FanoAttainment.lean}{FanoAttainment.full_attainment, FanoAttainment.full_shannon_exact}
\formal{FanoLaneCode.lean}{FanoLaneCode.through_card, FanoLaneCode.mem_code_iff, FanoLaneCode.update_incident, FanoLaneCode.isWholeRowCode, FanoLaneCode.code_card, FanoLaneCode.log_card, FanoLaneCode.optimal, FanoLaneCode.exists_code_for_cardinality}
\formal{GridFull.lean}{GridFull.full_upper, GridFull.storage_card_fifth}
\formal{WholeRowAcyclic.lean}{WholeRowAcyclic.fano_exact_acyclic_card, WholeRowAcyclic.fano_gap, WholeRowAcyclic.grid_exact_acyclic_card, WholeRowAcyclic.grid_gap}
\formal{FanoLift.lean}{FanoLift.conditional_gap, FanoLift.conditional_acyclic_gap, FanoLift.conditional_gap_one_percent, FanoLift.completeLift_gap}
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=2em]
\item \emph{Fano.} Let $d=7$ and let $\rho(j)$ be the three points on line $j$ of the Fano plane. Then
$\Sh(\rho)=119/3$ and $\sigma(\rho)=41$. Over $A^3$, for every finite abelian group~$A$, there is a whole-row
code with exactly $\card A^{119}$ words. In particular, for every integer $q\ge2$ a code over an alphabet of $q^3$
symbols attains the Shannon value. Every acyclic-set bound exceeds it by at least $4/3$ entries.
\item \emph{Grid.} For the $9\times6$ array whose columns are the six lines of the $3\times3$ grid and whose rows are
its nine points, $\Sh\le204/5$ and $\sigma=41$. There is no mutual triangle of nonincident entries. No equality
for this Shannon bound or for the capacity is claimed.
\item \emph{Lifts.} Let $n\ge1$, $1\le r\le n$, and replace every row and column of the Fano relation by $n$ copies.
Suppose every helper of a fine column is a copy of a point on its base line. Suppose also that every $r$ copies of
a point and every $r$ copies of a line through it contain a helper incidence. For every valid polymatroid $h$ and
every acyclic set~$A$ on the $d=7n$ array,
\[
  d^2-\card A-h(\text{all entries})\ \ge\ 41\,(n-r+1)^2-\tfrac{119}{3}\,n^2 .
\]
The right side exceeds $d^2/100$ when $99n\le100(n-r+1)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{outline}
First condition on the incident entries: there are $21$ in Fano and $18$ in the grid. On the remaining $28$ and
$36$ entries the certificates give, respectively,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:paid-residual}
 3h(V)\le2\sum_{v\in V}h(\{v\})+\sum_{v\in V}h(v\mid H_v),\qquad
 30h(V)\le19\sum_{v\in V}h(\{v\})+11\sum_{v\in V}h(v\mid H_v).
\end{equation}
Here $h(v\mid S)=h(S\cup\{v\})-h(S)$. These are exact positive combinations of $33$ and $18$ semantic
inequalities: submodularity, purchases of entries, and recovery with its conditional cost retained. One first
averages $h$ over the $168$ Fano automorphisms or the $72$ grid automorphisms. This preserves the total, the sum
of singleton values and the sum of recovery costs; it imposes no symmetry on $h$ or the code. The generated Lean
proofs check the automorphisms, each set containment used in recovery, and the integer coefficient identity.
The saved rational certificates and their independent checkers are supplied with the paper.

For exact recovery the last sums vanish. Adding the conditioned entries costs at most their number, giving
$21+56/3=119/3$ and $18+114/5=204/5$. The attaining Fano polymatroid is the sum, over its $28$ mutual triangles,
of one third of the rank-two uniform matroid on each triangle, plus the free incident entries. Each nonincident
entry belongs to three triangles, so it has singleton value one. Every triangle's other two entries are available
as helpers, proving validity. Its total is $119/3$. The order certificates enumerate the $128$ Fano point subsets
and the $512$ grid point subsets in an exact recurrence, and display orders of cost $41$ attaining the lower bound.
\Cref{thm:O} converts these values to maximum acyclic-set sizes $8$ and $13$.

For a lift, let $B_{ij}$ be the $n^2$ fine entries over base entry $(i,j)$ and set
$g(S)=h(\bigcup_{(i,j)\in S}B_{ij})/n^2$. The blocks partition the fine array. Thus $g$ is a polymatroid with
unit singleton caps, and helper containment makes it valid for Fano. Consequently $h(V)\le(119/3)n^2$.
This is an instance of \lean{MacroProjection.upper_of_projection}, which more generally allows any fibre-size
cap and does not require the projection to be onto.

For the order bound put $a=n-r+1$. In any fine row order let $\tau_i$ be the one-based position of the $a$-th row
in group $i$. Order the seven groups by these distinct thresholds. If $i$ is the $k$-th group in this order, then
$\tau_i\ge ka$. For a base line $j$ choose its incident group $i$ with largest threshold. Just before $\tau_i$,
that group has $r$ unvisited rows. At most $r-1$ fine columns of group $j$ can already have all their helpers:
otherwise those columns and the $r$ unvisited rows would contradict mixing on this incidence. Therefore at least
$a$ columns have last-helper position at least $\tau_i$. Summing over the seven line groups gives cost at least
$a^2\sigma(\text{Fano})=41a^2$. This and \cref{thm:O} prove (c). Finally
$41(99/100)^2-119/3=15523/30000>49/100$.
\end{outline}

For clarity, the paid inequalities used above have the following scope.
\begin{proposition}[Paid certificates]\label{prop:paid-entropy}
\formal{FanoCertificate.lean}{FanoCertificate.paid_fano}
\formal{GridCertificate.lean}{GridCertificate.paid_grid}
\formal{FanoFull.lean}{FanoFull.paid_full}
\formal{GridFull.lean}{GridFull.paid_full}
The two inequalities in \eqref{eq:paid-residual} hold for every polymatroid on the respective residual host, without
recovery or singleton-cap hypotheses. On the full host, if $Z$ is the set of incident entries, their left sides
are $3h(V\mid Z)$ and $30h(V\mid Z)$, their singleton terms become $h(v\mid Z)$, and their recovery terms may be
charged using the original full helper sets. Here $h(V\mid Z)=h(V)-h(Z)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{outline}
The same nonnegative certificate retains every conditional recovery cost. Apply it to
$S\mapsto h(S\cup Z)-h(Z)$ on the residual entries, and use that enlarging a conditioning set decreases an
increment of a polymatroid. This proves the full-host forms without charging $Z$ twice.
\end{outline}

\begin{outline}[Code realization in \cref{thm:E}(a)]
Let $A$ be any finite abelian group. The saved list of $28$ mutual triangles has exactly three triangles through
every nonincident entry. Choose a fixed bijection between these three triangles and the three lanes of that entry.
For each triangle impose one zero-sum equation on its three assigned lanes. Every lane is recoverable from the
other two entries of its triangle, which belong to the helper rows of the target column. Incident entries recover
themselves and their three lanes occur in no check, so they remain free.

The $28$ supports are nonempty and pairwise disjoint on the $147$ scalar coordinates. The shared disjoint-sums
fibre theorem therefore gives exactly $\card A^{147-28}=\card A^{119}$ arrays. The same argument permits any
prescribed triangle sums. The full Fano upper bound proves optimality among all whole-row codes over~$A^3$.
Taking $A=\Z/q\Z$ supplies every integer $q\ge2$; the normalized logarithm is $119/3$ symbols over the alphabet
of size $q^3$. No field structure is used.
\end{outline}

\begin{proposition}[Mixing lifts exist]\label{prop:lifts}
\notformalized
For every sufficiently large $n$ there is a lift as in \cref{thm:E}(c) with $r=\lceil n/100\rceil$, with at most
$3600$ helper rows for every column and at most $3600$ columns helped by every row, and with incidence girth at least
$c_0\log n$ for an absolute constant $c_0>0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{informalproof}
Put $t=1200$ and $\eta=1/100$. On each of the $21$ base incidences choose $t$ independent uniform bijections
between its two clone sets, each of size $n$, and take the union of their edges. Both degrees are at most $3t$.
All logarithms in this proof are natural. Choose $0<\eta_0<\eta$ so close to $\eta$ that
$t\eta_0>2\log(e/\eta_0)$, possible since $12>2\log(100e)$. Put $r_0=\lceil\eta_0 n\rceil$.
For one matching and fixed sets of $r_0$ rows and columns, the probability that no edge joins them is
\[
 \frac{\binom{n-r_0}{r_0}}{\binom n{r_0}}
 \le (1-r_0/n)^{r_0}\le e^{-r_0^2/n},
\]
with probability zero if $2r_0>n$. A union bound over the base incidences and the choices of the two sets is at most
$21\binom n{r_0}^{2}e^{-t r_0^2/n}$, which tends to zero exponentially. Thus the required mixing at scale $r_0$
holds with probability tending to one.

Girth is measured in the underlying simple incidence graph; for counting, label each edge by a matching that
supplies it. Write $s=7$, $K=4st$ and
$g=\lfloor\log n/(4\log K)\rfloor$. A simple cycle of length $\ell\ge4$ in the union has a choice of one of the
$t$ matchings witnessing each edge. There are at most $(2s)^\ell n^\ell t^\ell$ ordered possibilities for its
base vertices, clone vertices and witnesses. For any particular matching, $m$ distinct prescribed images have
probability $1/(n)_m$ if consistent, and zero otherwise. Since $\ell\le g$ and $n\ge2g$, this is at most
$(2/n)^m$. Independence between matchings therefore bounds the probability of the witnessed cycle by
$(2/n)^\ell$. The expected number of simple cycles of lengths at most $g$ is at most
$\sum_{\ell=4}^{g}K^\ell\le K^{g+1}/(K-1)=O(n^{1/4})$.
By Markov's inequality, fewer than $\sqrt n$ such cycles occur with probability tending to one. Choose a realization
with this property and with the preceding mixing property.

For each short cycle select one of its vertices and isolate every selected vertex: retain all row and column
labels, but delete its incident edges. This isolates fewer than $\sqrt n$ vertices and leaves no cycle of length
at most $g$. Every rectangle of $r=\lceil\eta n\rceil$ clones on a base incidence has at least $r-\sqrt n\ge r_0$
unisolated vertices on each side, for all large $n$. The original mixing property supplies an edge between them,
which survives isolation. Thus mixing at scale $r$, helper containment and the degree bounds all persist.
Finally $g\ge\log n/(8\log K)$ for large $n$. Isolated labels cause no problem: an empty-helper column is constant
in a code, and the macro projection remains valid.
\end{informalproof}

The complete lift is a simple non-vacuity check: take every clone of every incident point as a helper. It has width
$3n$, mixes at $r=1$, and gives the gap $(4/3)n^2$. It is not a fixed-window family. The sparse lifts of
\cref{prop:lifts} have window $3600$ and a positive-density gap at growing girth; no attaining code on these lifts
is asserted. Ordering whole row groups in a base-optimal order gives $\sigma\le41n^2=(41/49)d^2$ even for them.
Thus this separates Shannon inequalities from all acyclic bounds at fixed window; it does not approach the
rate-one regime of the Matrix Coding Conjecture.
