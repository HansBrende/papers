\section{What remains for transposition}\label{sec:open}

The question (T) remains open. A hypothetical linear-time transposer must
meet the return, cut and storage requirements simultaneously. The direct
bounds apply to its original adaptive execution at every positive width.
The simulator pullback adds every-large-output crossing expansion and
entry-capacity certificates at canonical widths. Thus these are constraints
on the actual computation, not only on an auxiliary trace.

Two concrete routes remain. First, prove an output-specific upper bound
on a cut or a jointly replayable boundary record that contradicts the
corresponding lower bound. The record must retain current-version
dependencies and raw reads, paying shared information once. Second, find a
low-capacity certificate on the actual original entry relation. With the
fixed $s,\delta$ of \cref{cor:entry-success-tail}, that certificate would
have to give, for some $B,J$,
\[
 \Delta_{b-6s}(Q_X^B[J])>\delta P+v_sG_s|B|.
\]
The theorem gives the reverse inequality on every timely correct input outside its
ambient exception. An exclusion must establish the conflicting certificate
under the proposed class's actual execution hypotheses.

The decoder program of \cite[Problem~9.4]{SeventeenthPaper2026} is a
possible source of such certificates, but it still needs an interface
theorem. Our entry quotient does not automatically have shared decoders
or a linear correctness source. A large arbitrary fibre does not supply
those properties. Conversely, a construction must deliver all intermediate
representations and the ordered transpose through paid native accesses,
rather than merely exhibit a high-rate abstract code.

The other results distinguish useful premises from insufficient statistics.
Coherent head clusters cannot hide all long-range communication; fixed
catalogues and locally readable references cannot explain all but one
store throughout the run. However, persistent marginal information may
remain redundant across tapes, and cheap copying may satisfy the
historical-feedback requirement. These controls rule out interpreting
each large store or each reused version as an independent time charge.
A superlinear bound still requires a justified way to combine obligations
across the computation; no such general multiscale charge is proved here.

The remaining formalization obligations are separate from (T): the new
carrier and catalogue compilers, raw-relative and historical extensions,
last-visit eraser, redundant archive and block-monitor/spool construction.
Their precise boundaries are recorded in \cref{sec:formalization}.
The finite hash, menu, entropy and asymptotic steps, concrete scan loader,
and simulator/source correspondences are checked.
